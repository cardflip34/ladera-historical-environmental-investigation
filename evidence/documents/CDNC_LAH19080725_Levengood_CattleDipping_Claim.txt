Special to The Herald. . SANTA ANA, July 24.-A claim for $400 was flled today by
E. J. Levengood against the county of Orange as compensation for damages and
loss to his herd of dairy cows, sustained by dipping for the cure of Texas tick.
The dipping was done by order of the county veterinarian, Dr. W. S. McFarlane,
and he it was who supervised the mixing of the preparation used. , , . Six of the
cows, after coming out of the dip, sickened and died, and several others for days
refused to eat and fell off In flesh to such an extent that their value was
reduced at least one-half, according to the claim : made by the owner. : -y The
state veterinarian visited the herd, and other veterinary surgeons were called in
to pass Judgment as to where the blame should be placed, but none of them talked
for publication and Levengood has been forced to take this step in order to
preserve his standing in court.

ESTABLISHED (credible contemporaneous report, B2):
  - A county-ordered cattle dipping program for "Texas tick" (cattle tick fever,
    Rhipicephalus / Boophilus annulatus) was operating in Orange County, CA in
    mid-1908.
  - The dipping was ordered by, and the dip solution mixed under supervision of,
    the Orange County veterinarian, named as Dr. W. S. McFarlane. This is a
    NEW NAMED LEAD for locating the primary county record.
  - E. J. Levengood, a Santa Ana-area dairyman, filed a $400 claim against the
    County of Orange on 24 July 1908.
  - Alleged harm: six cows died after the dip; others sickened and lost roughly
    half their value.
  - The State Veterinarian inspected the herd; other veterinary surgeons were
    consulted; no finding was made public.
