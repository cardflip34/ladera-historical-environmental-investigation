# Vat hunt — systematic rectilinear search of the 1929 / 1938 / 1947 aerials

**Run 2026-07-31. Result: no vat-like assembly found. One unexpected finding.**

## What was searched

44 tiles at ~0.37 m/px across three independent years, covering the full AOI.
**37,228 cells** scored for straight-edge concentration in orthogonal pairs — the signature of built
enclosure in a natural scene. 754 cells above z=2.5, forming 355 spatial clusters.

Circular 174 defined the target: a large range operation used a **swim vat** — a long narrow
excavation up to 80–90 ft, with a holding corral, approach chute and drain pen attached. At
0.37 m/px a 27 m vat is ~73 px long and a 30 m corral ~81 px across, so the **assembly** is
resolvable even though the 1–1.5 m vat channel itself is only 3–4 px wide.

## Result: the top 20 candidates, classified by eye

| Count | What it actually was |
|---|---|
| 8 | **orchard rows** |
| 5 | road / highway |
| 3 | **film fiducial X marks** — annotations on the original photograph, not ground features |
| 3 | dirt track |
| 1 | terrain or scan damage |
| **0** | **vat-like assembly** |

Every one of the twenty strongest rectilinear anomalies in three years of aerial photography is
explained by something other than a dipping station.

## Honest reading

**This is a negative result, and it is a real one.** It is stronger than the earlier "no vat found"
because it is systematic rather than by-eye, run at 2.7× finer resolution, across three independent
years, with the false-positive classes named in advance and then found exactly as predicted.

**What it does NOT establish:**
- The AOI is the Ladera Ranch footprint only. The O'Neill holding ran 200,000+ acres and the
  throughput calculation says the herd needed **3–7 vats**. Those could all sit outside this frame.
- 0.37 m/px is adequate for the assembly, marginal for the channel. A vat whose corrals were
  demolished before 1929 would leave little to detect.
- Seventeen years separate the 1912 programme end from the 1929 frame. Timber structures rot,
  excavations silt in, and ranchers reused materials.

**Method self-criticism:** the detector ranked film fiducial marks as candidates #1 and #3. That is
a real weakness, caught only because every candidate was viewed rather than trusted. Any future run
must keep the human review step.

## The unexpected finding — orchards

Eight of the twenty top candidates are **orchard rows**, present in all three years (1929, 1938,
1947), across **lat 33.5206–33.5373, lon −117.6755 to −117.6383** — the southern and south-western
part of the frame.

This matters independently of the vat question. **Lead arsenate was the standard orchard insecticide
of this era**, applied deliberately and repeatedly to fruit trees, and it leaves both arsenic *and*
lead in surface soil. The project already holds a documented instance of exactly this pattern
nearby: Carl Hankey ES, where DTSC recorded arsenic and lead on a former orchard site.

So the search for one arsenic pathway surfaced physical evidence of a second, better-documented one
sitting inside the study frame — and unlike the vat, **these orchard blocks are visible, mapped and
datable from imagery already held**.

**This does not mean orchards explain anything.** It means there is a second candidate arsenic
source in this landscape whose footprint we can actually delineate, and any soil result must be
interpreted against both. Lead–arsenate co-occurrence is the diagnostic that separates them:
orchard spraying leaves lead with the arsenic; cattle dip leaves arsenic without it.
