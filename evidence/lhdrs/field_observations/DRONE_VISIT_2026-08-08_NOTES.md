## Vegetation-stress hypothesis — status and test
Arsenic IS phytotoxic at high soil concentrations and bare "kill zones" persisting decades are
documented at historic dip sites. HOWEVER the photographed mosaic has a strong mundane
explanation: normal late-summer coastal sage scrub phenology (drought-dormant sagebrush = grey;
California buckwheat in late-summer seed = rust-red; evergreen oak/laurel sumac = green).
Standing dead brush is NOT the arsenic signature; persistent BARE soil with sharp edges is.
Planned tests: (a) multi-year persistence check of the exact patches across 2012-2022 imagery
once EXIF pins them; (b) XRF transect across a stressed patch into healthy brush — gradient
present = signal, absent = phenology. Added to sampling plan as low-cost high-value test.
