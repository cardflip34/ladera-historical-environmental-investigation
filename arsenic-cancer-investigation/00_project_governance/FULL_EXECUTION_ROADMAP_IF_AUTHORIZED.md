## How to read this
Four **tracks** run substantially in **parallel** once authorized, because they answer different
questions and don't block each other (environment vs. individual exposure vs. population pattern vs.
tumor biology). They converge at **Month 9–12** for integration. Each track has go/no-go checkpoints.

## TRACK 1 — Environmental (soil), Gate B → Month 0–3
**Answers:** was arsenic (which kind) actually left in the ground, and where?
| Step | Duration | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Finalize SAP with landowner/agency (Starr Ranch/Audubon, schools, parks) | 2–4 wk | GATE B authorization |
| Field sampling (0–6 in, chain of custody, priority grounds first) | 3–7 days field | permission granted |
| Lab: total + bioaccessible As, Pb, **speciation** | 2–4 wk turnaround | certified lab queue |
| QA/QC + report | 1–2 wk | — |
**Go/no-go:** proceeds independent of other tracks — cheapest, fastest, and the strongest
single early read on the historical predicate. **Elevated low-lead arsenic (trioxide) on documented
dip ground would be the most consequential single finding in the project.**

## TRACK 2 — Biomarker / exposure reconstruction, Gate B → Month 1–5

## TRACK 3 — Epidemiology (CCR), Gate B → Month 1–6
**Answers:** is the observed pattern statistically unusual once properly standardized?
| Step | Duration | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Preregister + **time-lock** the analysis plan (already drafted) | immediate | physician/epi sign-off |
| CCR data-use agreement + authorization | 2–4 mo (typically the long pole) | CCR review queue |
| Data delivery + analysis (SIR, Bayesian, SaTScan, sensitivity, negative controls) | 2–4 wk once data arrives | biostatistician |
| Independent statistical review | 1–2 wk | — |
**Go/no-go:** run regardless of Tracks 1/2 results — a different evidentiary axis (pattern vs.
mechanism). Start the CCR request the day Gate B clears; it is usually the critical-path bottleneck
for the whole project.

## TRACK 4 — Tumor / mechanistic, Gate B → Month 2–8 (longest lead time)
**Answers:** does the tumor biology show anything consistent with an arsenic exposure/mechanism?
| Step | Duration | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Legal hold + retrieval of FFPE blocks (already drafted letters) | 2–6 wk | custodian response |

**Go/no-go:** **specimen-scarce** — block adequacy determines whether this track can run at all for
a given case. Lowest statistical power of the four tracks (stated up front in the protocol); treat
results as **hypothesis-strengthening or -weakening, not decisive alone.**

   level g/d toward a/b, in either direction.
2. **General-vs-specific-causation re-assessment** (template already built) — this is where the

   assessed."
3. **Final integrated report** (skeleton already built) — populated, dual-reviewed.

## REALISTIC SCENARIOS (plan for all three; none is assumed)
| Scenario | What it would look like | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| **Null across the board** | Clean soil, no elevated biomarkers, no epi signal, no tumor signature | Report states this plainly; retires the arsenic-specific hypothesis for this community with documented rigor; alternative hypotheses (Workstream 13) move to the fore |
| **Mixed / partial** | e.g., soil hit on documented dip ground, but individual biomarkers null | Strengthens the historical predicate without establishing individual exposure — most likely realistic outcome; narrows follow-up to the sites/individuals where a signal exists |
| **Convergent positive** | Soil + biomarker + epi + tumor all point the same direction | Still **general causation only strengthens toward "supported"** — specific causation per individual still requires the full evidentiary chain (dose, timing, alternatives excluded) before any legal use |

## RESOURCE / COST NOTE (rough order of magnitude, for planning only)
Soil program: low-to-mid five figures. Biomarker program: cost scales with n and specimen type
(teeth/nails cheaper; blood-spot methylation arrays pricier). CCR data-use fees + biostatistician time:
variable, often the largest line item due to review-queue delays rather than direct cost. Tumor
sequencing: highest per-sample cost, lowest sample count expected. **Attorney/physician team should
get current quotes from named labs before committing — this project does not procure services.**
