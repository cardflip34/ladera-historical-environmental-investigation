# WORKSTREAM 4 — Could arsenic create or favor an EWSR1 fusion? (mechanistic bridge)

**Search date:** 2026-07-23 · single-reviewer (flag for dual review) · **no causation asserted.**
Evidence levels: a fact · b strong · c suggestive · d biological plausibility · e hypothesis ·
f inference · g unknown.

**Central question:** could environmentally relevant inorganic arsenic increase *EWSR1*-region
double-strand breaks (DSBs), bias their repair toward translocation, or select/expand a
fusion-positive mesenchymal progenitor?

**Net position:** a genuine, *plausible* bridge now exists — arsenic suppresses high-fidelity DSB
repair in ways *associated with* chromosomal translocations, Ewing's cell of origin is a
developmentally restricted mesenchymal progenitor, and Ewing has a real (if unresolved)
agricultural/pesticide epidemiological signal. **But no study connects arsenic to the specific
EWSR1 fusion, and there is meaningful counter-evidence.** The bridge is level **d (plausible)**, not
demonstrated.

---

## Bridge A — Arsenic suppresses homologous recombination, shifting to error-prone repair linked to translocations · level (d), strengthened
- Arsenic trioxide **decreases homologous recombination (HR)** and single-strand annealing but does
  **not** impair non-homologous end joining (NHEJ), in human pre-B Nalm-6 cells — PMID **37530740**.
- Arsenic-altered DSB repair **favors error-prone pathways (NHEJ) and is associated with
  chromosomal translocations** and genomic instability — "Oncogenomic disruptions in arsenic-induced
  carcinogenesis," PMC**5421966** (Oncotarget).
- **Relevance:** Ewing is *defined by* a translocation (t(11;22) EWSR1::FLI1). A carcinogen that
  demotes error-free HR while leaving error-prone joining intact is a mechanism by which mis-joined
  breaks (translocations) can arise/persist **in general.**
- **Directness to Ewing:** INDIRECT. No data on the EWSR1/FLI1 loci, on DSB frequency at those
  loci, or in mesenchymal progenitors. → the critical missing experiment.

## Bridge B — Genomic instability / LINE-1 derepression · level (d)
- Arsenic dysregulates LINE-1 retrotransposons, driving genomic instability (PMC5421966). Instability
  is a permissive backdrop for structural rearrangement. Non-specific to EWSR1.

## Bridge C — Epigenetic reprogramming (Polycomb/H3K27me3) · level (d/e)
- Arsenic disrupts H3K9me3/H3K27me3 balance via PRC2 (PALI1 inhibition) — PMC**12223765**.
- EWS::FLI1 oncogenesis is itself a chromatin-reprogramming disease. Whether arsenic's epigenetic
  shifts intersect Ewing enhancer biology is untested → hypothesis.

## Bridge D — Developmental susceptibility window · level (d/e)
- Ewing's cell of origin is a **mesenchymal stem/progenitor cell, most permissive at the embryonic
  stage**; EWS::FLI1 in human embryonic MSCs yields Ewing-like tumors, and the fusion causes
  transcriptional reprogramming + **defective DNA-damage repair** — PMC**2952797**; Nat Commun 2025
  **s41467-025-64475-y**.
- Arsenic crosses the placenta; prenatal/early-life is an established arsenic susceptibility window.
- **Convergence (hypothesis):** if the fusion arises prenatally/early in a mesenchymal progenitor,
  the timing of arsenic exposure could matter. No data test this for Ewing.

## Cross-cutting epidemiology — Ewing ↔ agricultural / pesticide exposure · level (c) for pesticides, (g) for arsenic specifically
- **Real, replicated signal** (contra the flat "no environmental risk factors" summary):
  - Australia national case-control: risk **doubled** with maternal pesticide/insecticide handling;
    more cases lived on farms — PMID **12020108**.
  - Paternal agricultural occupation / herbicide-pesticide-fertilizer exposure, **RR ~6.1** (SF study);
    PMIDs **1311140**, **15551353**.
  - Systematic review: significant pesticide–Ewing/osteosarcoma association.
  - California perinatal fine-particulate study — PMC**11887085** (different exposure, shows this
    community/registry is actively studied).
- **Bridge to Ladera:** historical arsenicals (lead-arsenate, arsenic trioxide) *were* agricultural
  pesticides / the cattle-dip. **But "pesticide" studies did not isolate arsenic** — they cannot be
  read as arsenic-specific evidence. This defines a measurement gap, not proof.

---

## COUNTER-EVIDENCE (weighted equally)
1. **Arsenic trioxide *treats* a translocation cancer.** As2O3 is standard curative therapy for acute
   promyelocytic leukemia (APL, t(15;17) PML-RARA fusion) — arsenic can *kill* fusion-driven cells,
   not only create risk. Direction of effect is context-dependent.
2. **EWS-FLI1 alone does not transform normal human cells;** cooperating events are required, and the
   fusion itself impairs DNA repair (Nat Commun 2025). The rate-limiting step may not be break
   formation at all.
3. **Strong germline/ancestry component:** Ewing incidence is markedly lower in African-ancestry
   populations and has multiple GWAS susceptibility loci — pointing to heritable, not environmental,
   drivers (PMC6085378; PMC12919121).
4. Arsenic's *established* human cancers are epithelial (bladder/lung/skin), not mesenchymal.

## Verdict for the briefs
- **Plausible, not proven.** The mechanistic and epidemiological pieces are individually real; the
  arsenic→EWSR1 link is **unstudied**, and counter-evidence is substantive.
- **The single highest-value experiment:** test whether environmentally relevant inorganic arsenic
  increases EWSR1-region DSBs and/or biases their repair toward fusion in Ewing cells of origin
  (embryonic/pediatric mesenchymal progenitors) — see 16_proposed_experiments.
