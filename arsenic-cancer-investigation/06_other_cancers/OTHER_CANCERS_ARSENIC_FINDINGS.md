## Signals most relevant to a sarcoma hypothesis
- **Hepatic angiosarcoma** is an *established* arsenic-caused **sarcoma** (mesenchymal/endothelial) —
  proof arsenic **can** cause a sarcoma in humans, at a specific site (liver vasculature). Does not
  extend to bone/Ewing, but defeats any claim that "arsenic only causes carcinomas."
- **STS in vineyard workers** (arsenical pesticides) — occupational arsenical → soft-tissue sarcoma.
  Suggestive, not bone-specific.

## Early-life / in-utero (relevant to a pediatric hypothesis)
- In-utero + childhood arsenic → increased young-adult mortality from bladder/laryngeal/liver cancer
  (Antofagasta cohort, PMC3556614); in-utero arsenic → DNA damage/micronuclei in newborns
  (Environ Health 2019, s12940-019-0481-7). Establishes early-life vulnerability windows — not a
  pediatric-bone-cancer link.

## Honest net
Arsenic's human cancer footprint is real but **site-specific and largely epithelial**, with **one
established sarcoma (hepatic angiosarcoma)** and a **suggestive occupational STS** signal. For **bone
sarcomas (osteosarcoma, Ewing) there is no direct human evidence** — a gap, not a refutation.
