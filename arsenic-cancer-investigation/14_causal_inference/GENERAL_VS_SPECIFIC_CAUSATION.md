# GENERAL vs SPECIFIC CAUSATION — framework & current status (DRAFT, gated)

**Status:** DRAFT scientific framework for the physician + attorney teams. **Not a legal conclusion.**
This engine draws no legal conclusions; it maps where evidence does and does not exist along each
causal element. Levels: a fact · b strong · c suggestive · d plausible · e hypothesis · g unknown.

## The two questions (kept strictly separate)
- **General causation:** *Can* inorganic arsenic, at environmentally relevant exposure, cause Ewing
  sarcoma (or another reported cancer) in humans, in principle?
- **Specific causation:** Did arsenic exposure cause *this* individual's cancer — i.e., were they
  exposed, at a sufficient dose, during the etiologic window, before diagnosis, with alternatives
  reasonably excluded?
Specific causation **cannot** be reached unless general causation is first supported. Neither is an
argument this engine makes; both are evidentiary maps.

## What this means for the teams (neutral)
- The honest current position is **"unmeasured and unestablished," not "refuted."** The value of
  Phases 2–4 is that they can move each cell above from *g/d* toward *a/b in either direction.*
- Any public or legal statement must respect these levels; **do not state or imply specific causation.**
- Confounding is central: former-orchard **lead-arsenate** and DDT co-occur with the dip-era arsenic
  and with the *documented* pediatric-cancer environmental signal (pesticides) — separating them
  (speciation, As:Pb, molecular) is prerequisite to any specific-causation reasoning.

## Falsification (either direction advances the science)
- General causation weakened if: mechanistic tests show no EWSR1-region effect (F-series), and/or
  exposure reconstruction is null across affected individuals.
- Specific causation weakened for an individual if: no exposure/dose in the etiologic window, or a
  competing cause is established.
