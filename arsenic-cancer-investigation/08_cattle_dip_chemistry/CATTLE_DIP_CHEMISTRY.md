# CATTLE-DIP CHEMISTRY & SOIL FATE (Workstream 6 + fate link to 09)

**Date:** 2026-07-23 · single-reviewer. Not all arsenic compounds are interchangeable; the dip
signature, toxicity, and soil behavior differ by species. Levels: a fact · b strong · c suggestive ·
d plausible · g unknown.

## Chemical crosswalk (historical dip → modern identity)
| Historical name | Modern chemical | Formula | CAS | As valence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "White arsenic" / arsenic trioxide | Arsenic(III) trioxide | As₂O₃ | 1327-53-3 | +3 (trivalent) | the dip poison base; ~8 lb / 500 gal per USDA formula |
| Sodium arsenite | Sodium meta-arsenite | NaAsO₂ | 7784-46-5 | +3 | **made from As₂O₃ + caustic soda**; the active dip solution; highly soluble |
| Sodium arsenate | Disodium hydrogen arsenate | Na₂HAsO₄ | 7778-43-0 | +5 (pentavalent) | interconverts with arsenite in soil |
| Lead arsenate (contrast, orchard) | Lead hydrogen arsenate | PbHAsO₄ | 7784-40-9 | +5 | **orchard pesticide — arsenic WITH lead**; the *other* signature |

**Key toxicology (a/b):** inorganic > organic; **trivalent arsenite ~10× more toxic than pentavalent
arsenate**; trivalent As binds critical **sulfhydryl groups**, disrupting cellular metabolism.
Historical dip fluids reached ~**200 mg As/L**, acutely toxic to cattle that drank vat fluid.

## The two signatures (decisive for testing — mirrors B5 board)
- **Cattle-dip:** arsenic **trioxide → arsenite/arsenate**, **low/absent lead** → *arsenic-with-little-
  or-no-lead* signature.
- **Orchard:** **lead arsenate** → *arsenic bound with lead* signature.
- A lab distinguishes them by **speciation + the As:Pb ratio.** Local soil tests to date targeted the
  orchard/lead signature (see historical investigation); the dip signature has never been sought.

## Co-contaminants historically present
Dip fluids/sites commonly co-occur with **DDT** (later additive) and, per formulation era, sulfur/
lime; disposal and vat sludge concentrate arsenic. → any local sampling must run **As speciation +
lead + organochlorines** to separate sources (Workstream 13 alternatives).

## Environmental fate at former dip sites (analog evidence, level b for the analog)
From studied former cattle-dip sites (chiefly Australia/South Africa; the same program the historical
investigation compares against):
- **> 1,000–1,600** former dip sites documented (NSW/QLD); a large legacy-contamination class.
- Surface-soil arsenic **up to ~3,000 mg/kg** (range ~34–2,941 mg/kg, 0–10 cm), **highest at the tank
  and drip-pad**; co-contaminated with DDT (up to ~60,000 mg/kg).
- Arsenic **remains sequestered decades later**, binding **iron oxides and clays**; **exceeds EPA
  residential screening levels**; groundwater exceedances reported; microbial effects persist 25 yr.
- **Arsenic is an element — it does not degrade.** Persistence is expected (a).

## Relevance to the Ladera/Coto question (careful)
- **Supports the *source/pathway* plausibility (d):** if a vat operated locally, high, persistent,
  low-lead arsenic in the tank/drip-pad footprint is the expected signature — and it has **never been
  looked for** here.
- **Does not establish local contamination (g):** the analog sites are elsewhere; **only authorized
  local speciated soil sampling can confirm or exclude** a dip signature at these parcels.
- **Confounder discipline:** a high-As-with-lead result would point to **orchard** legacy, not the dip;
  a high-As-**low-lead** result is the dip signature. Speciation is the discriminator.
