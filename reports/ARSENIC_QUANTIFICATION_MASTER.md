# Arsenic in the ground at Ladera Ranch — consolidated quantification

**Assembled 2026-07-31 from the project's own calculation outputs.** Regenerate with
`scripts/lhdrs_build_master_arsenic_report.py`; every number below is read from JSON, so this
document cannot drift from the math behind it.

> ## What this document is
> A quantified, **hypothetical** analysis of how much arsenic a compulsory 1907–1912 cattle-dipping
> operation would have put into this landscape, where it would have concentrated, and where to test
> for it.
>
> ## What it is not
> **It is not a measurement.** No soil or sediment anywhere in Ladera Ranch has been sampled for
> arsenic by this project or, on the record searched, by anyone else. It does not establish that a
> vat existed inside the study area, that arsenic is present at any location, that any material
> moved, that any person was exposed, or that any illness has any environmental cause.
>
> **A single accredited laboratory result would outweigh this entire document.** That is the point
> of producing it: to justify and direct that testing.

**Unresolved and material:** Circular 174 does **not name** the one large ranch. The profile fits

**undated** — "at one time" — so it is a documented historical peak, not a 1907–1912 census.

## 2. Which vat, and how many

Herd size sets vat count, because compulsory dipping means the **entire herd** passes through
every 14–21 days. At ~300–500 head per vat per working day:

| Herd | Vats required simultaneously |
|---|---|
| 2,000 | 1 – 1 |
| 5,000 | 1 – 2 |
| 10,000 | 2 – 3 |
| 25,000 | 3 – 7 |

**A single vat cannot cycle a large herd inside the mandated interval.** An operation at this scale
needed a **network** of swim vats distributed across the range — sited where the cattle already
were, near water and handling grounds. That reframes the search: we were looking for *a* vat when
we should have been looking for *one of several*, which is why a negative in one 5 × 5.7 km frame
carries little weight against a 200,000-acre system.

### Arsenic standing in a vat at any moment

| Vat capacity | Arsenic trioxide | Elemental arsenic |
|---|---|---|
| 2,088 gal | 33.4 lb | 11.5 kg |
| 3,000 gal | 48.0 lb | 16.5 kg |
| 5,000 gal | 80.0 lb | 27.5 kg |

## 3. How much arsenic left the vats — the throughput math

**Drag-out** is solution carried out on the animal's coat. It is the dominant loss route and the
dominant route into surrounding ground. Over five years of compulsory dipping:

| Herd | Elemental As range (kg) | Mid case (kg) | Mid case (lb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2,000 | 239 – 1,433 | 573 | 1,264 |
| 5,000 | 597 – 3,583 | 1,433 | 3,159 |
| 10,000 | 1,194 – 7,166 | 2,866 | 6,319 |
| 25,000 | 2,986 – 17,914 | 7,166 | 15,797 |

Parameters: 8.0 lb As₂O₃ / 500 gal · dipping every
14–21 days ·
drag-out 0.25–1.0 gal
per head per dip · 5 years.

**This is conservative.** The circular permits 9–10 lb per 500 gal for range cattle — exactly the
class at issue — and the calculation uses 8.

## 4. Total into the ground, O'Neill Ranch operation alone

Drag-out **plus** the standing charge of each vat at decommissioning, since period practice was
frequently to abandon a vat in place and backfill it rather than haul thousands of gallons away:

| Case | Elemental As | | | As arsenic trioxide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 3,035 kg | **6,692 lb** | 3.3 tons | 8,836 lb |
| mid | 7,280 kg | **16,051 lb** | 8.0 tons | 21,192 lb |
| high | 18,029 kg | **39,748 lb** | 19.9 tons | 52,480 lb |

**Headline: on the mid case, roughly 16,051 pounds of elemental arsenic — about
8 US tons — with a plausible range of 6,692 to
39,748 pounds.**

### The persistence point — the one that carries

**Arsenic is an element.** It has no environmental half-life. Unlike DDT or toxaphene, it does not
degrade, break down, or weather away. It can only be moved, diluted, buried, or physically hauled
off. **Mass introduced in 1907–1912 is still in this landscape unless someone removed it.** Grading
redistributes it; it does not destroy it.

## 5. The lethal-dose comparison — and how to use it without being destroyed

A fatal oral dose of arsenic trioxide for an adult is on the order of
**100–300 mg** — less than a pea. That
is precisely why it was the classical poison of the wine cup.

| Case | Nominal lethal-dose equivalents |
|---|---|
| low | 13.4 – 40.1 million |
| mid | 32.0 – 96.1 million |
| high | 79.3 – 238.0 million |

### Read this before using that number anywhere public

**"N lethal doses" is arithmetic, not toxicology.** It is a legitimate way to convey *scale* and an
illegitimate way to convey *risk*. Three objections any competent critic raises inside one sentence:

1. **Bioavailability.** Arsenic bound in soil is not arsenic dissolved in wine. The soil-bound
   fraction actually absorbed on ingestion is typically small and variable — often well under 25%.
2. **Pathway.** Nobody drinks soil. Real exposure is incidental ingestion of dust, dermal contact,
   and inhalation of resuspended particles — orders of magnitude below a poisoning scenario.
3. **Mechanism.** Acute lethality and chronic carcinogenicity are different endpoints with
   different dose-response curves. The concern here is chronic low-dose exposure, about which the
   lethal dose says nothing.

**The honest sentence:** *"The quantity of a non-degrading poison introduced to this landscape was
enormous in human terms, which is why it has to be measured."* True, defensible, powerful.

**The sentence that would end the project's credibility:** any implication that people are being
acutely poisoned. Not supported, and it would take everything else down with it.

## 6. Where the mass actually went — distribution, not a single number

A dipped animal drips for hours, licks its coat, grazes 14–21 days, and excretes wherever it walks.
The mass **spreads**. Mid case, top 15 cm, against a California background of
**1–11 mg/kg**:

| Compartment | Area (ha) | Share | Low mg/kg | Mid mg/kg | High mg/kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vat structure + immediate surround | 0.05 | 10% | 2,890.5 | 6,933.3 | 17,170.5 |
| Drain pen / drip pen | 0.20 | 30% | 2,167.9 | 5,200.0 | 12,877.9 |
| Working corrals, chutes, approach lanes | 2.00 | 20% | 144.5 | 346.7 | 858.5 |
| Trails, water, shade, salt, bedding grounds | 50.00 | 25% | 7.2 | 17.3 | 42.9 |
| Open range - general grazing | 20,000.00 | 15% | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.1 |

**Supports the concern:** vat, drain pen and corral compartments run orders of magnitude above
background even on the low case — and a leaking wooden vat makes the vat compartment a **point
source**, not a smear.

**Cuts against it, stated as plainly:** open grazing range comes out **at or near background** once
the same mass is spread over tens of thousands of acres. A dramatic arsenic signature on general
pasture is *not* the expectation. Saying so is what makes the hotspot figures credible.

**The compartment that matters most** is trails / water / shade / bedding: where cattle
congregated, where dung concentrated, and **where the water was**. Deposition zone and transport
path in the same place.

⚠️ **Compartment fractions are reasoned allocations, not measurements.** They are the weakest
numbers in this project and are flagged as such so a reviewer challenges them rather than
discovering them.

## 7. Where the vats could have been — the imagery search, and its honest result

A systematic rectilinear-feature search was run across the **1929, 1938 and 1947** aerials at
~0.37 m/px — **37,228 cells**, tuned to the documented swim-vat signature (a long, narrow,
high-contrast linear structure with associated pens).

**Result: no vat-like assembly was found inside the study area.** The detector's strongest hits
ground-truthed as **orchard rows**, roads and drainage features.

That is a **negative**, and it is reported as such. Three reasons it is weak evidence of absence:

1. The throughput math says the operation needed **3–7 vats across 200,000+ acres**. The study
   frame is ~28 km² — a small fraction of the ranch. They may all sit outside it.
2. A wooden vat is a **trench with timber**. Decommissioned and backfilled in 1912, it may leave no
   surface expression by 1929.
3. Detection favours precision; a partially collapsed or overgrown structure scores below
   threshold.

### The orchard confounder, mapped

The search also delineated **35 orchard blocks** across 1929–1947, row spacing
5.3–8.0 m
(period citrus/walnut). Lead arsenate was the era's standard orchard insecticide, so orchard ground
is a **second candidate arsenic source**.

**They are almost all outside the community** — far south-west. Nearest block to any school
**2,760 m**; nearest to the drainage **1,439 m**.

This matters for interpretation: **orchard ground carries lead *with* the arsenic; cattle-dip
ground does not.** So a positive in the community core could not be waved away as an old orchard —
the nearest is nearly 3 km distant, and the lead ratio would distinguish them anyway.

### Sampling protocol, so a result would be worth having

- **Depth-resolved cores, not surface grabs.** A pre-1913 layer, if it survives, is at depth, and
  1997–2006 grading may have buried or inverted it.
- **Full analyte suite:** arsenic, **lead**, DDT/DDE, toxaphene, PAHs. The lead ratio is the
  diagnostic that separates dip ground from orchard ground.
- **Speciation** where total arsenic exceeds background — inorganic vs organic changes the
  toxicological meaning entirely.
- **Matched comparison cores** from a similar community chosen on objective criteria
  (development age, geology, climate), **never on cancer counts**.
- **Accredited lab, chain of custody, lab blinded** to which samples are which.
- **Consent required** from LARMAC / County / CUSD depending on parcel. None obtained.

> **A negative result is a real and publishable result.** If depositional-zone cores show arsenic
> at or below regional background, that materially weakens the hypothesis for this community — and
> saying so plainly is what makes every other finding here credible. A study that can only come
> back positive is not a study.
