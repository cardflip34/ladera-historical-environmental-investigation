# CLAIMS_AND_LIMITATIONS.md

Defines the claim levels used throughout the platform and the limitations that constrain
every current output. Each data record and each finding is tagged with exactly one claim
level.

## 1. Claim levels

| Level | Meaning | Test to qualify | Example |
|-------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| **Confirmed** | Established by an authoritative primary source; independently checkable. | An A1/A2 source states it as fact, ideally corroborated. | "Santa Margarita Water District serves Ladera Ranch" (if confirmed by SMWD's own service-area document). |
| **Officially reported** | An official body has stated it, but it depends on data the platform cannot independently verify. | An A1/A2 official statement asserts it. | "The California Cancer Registry, UCI, and the OC Agricultural Commissioner agreed to conduct an updated review" (per OCHCA). |
| **Publicly alleged** | Asserted by non-official or advocacy sources; not independently verified. | Appears in a C/D or unverified B2 source. | "17 different pesticides were applied in a single month" (as attributed to a resident's records review). |
| **Scientifically associated** | A statistical association is reported in peer-reviewed literature — association, not causation. | A1 peer-reviewed source reports an effect estimate with CI. | "Residential proximity to oil & gas development is associated with childhood Ewing sarcoma in a California study." |
| **Estimated** | A model-based or assumption-based value produced by the platform. | Generated by a documented method with stated assumptions. | "Expected Ewing sarcoma cases for a population of this size and age structure over 2013–2026." |
| **Unknown** | Not determinable from available public evidence. | No adequate source found. | "Which specific products LARMAC's vendors applied on which dates." |

## 2. Global limitations (apply to every current output)

1. **No verified case data.** As of the current phase, the platform has **no** access to
   medically verified, registry-confirmed case counts, ages, diagnosis dates, or residence
   histories. All case information is *publicly reported and not independently verified.*
   Case counts differ between sources (e.g., "six" vs "about a dozen").
2. **No individual exposure data.** No measured dose, biomonitoring, soil/water/dust
   sampling, or individual residence-history exists in this platform. Exposure-screening
   scores are prioritization heuristics for **locations and time periods**, never measures
   of what any person contacted.
3. **PUR under-captures urban landscape use.** California Pesticide Use Reporting does not
   comprehensively capture ornamental/landscape maintenance on HOA common areas, structural
   pest control, or consumer use. Absence of a record in PUR is **not** evidence of
   non-application.
4. **Geographic and temporal overlap ≠ causation.** Co-location of a chemical and a case in
   space and time is a screening signal, not proof of contact, dose, or effect.
5. **Population denominators are estimates.** Person-years for a fast-growing, high-turnover
   master-planned community carry real uncertainty; SIR outputs are scenario analyses, not
   findings.
6. **Small numbers.** Ewing sarcoma is rare (~1–3 per million children per year). Any
   community-level count is subject to large random variation, multiple-comparison and
   boundary-selection biases, and the ecological fallacy.
7. **Ancestry structure confounds baseline expectation.** Ewing sarcoma incidence is
   markedly higher in people of European ancestry; a predominantly non-Hispanic-white
   community will have a *higher expected* count than the all-races national rate implies.
   Ignoring this would bias an SIR upward.

## 3. What the platform can and cannot conclude now

**Can:** organize and grade public evidence; characterize what is and is not documented;
compute *hypothetical* SIR scenarios with explicit assumptions; identify which chemicals,
pathways, and alternative hypotheses warrant deeper review; specify exactly what missing
evidence would change the analysis.

**Cannot:** confirm a cluster exists in the epidemiological sense; establish that any
exposure occurred; attribute any illness to any cause; rank hypotheses as if causation were
established.

## 4. Approved vs forbidden phrasing

See `CLAUDE.md`. In short — approved: "The reported pattern warrants investigation"; "The
available evidence does not yet establish causation." Forbidden: "There has to be a
correlation"; any causal claim without explicit statistical support and caveats.
