# RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md

## 1. Background

Since approximately 2013, multiple children and teenagers associated with Ladera Ranch
(a master-planned community in south Orange County, California) have been publicly reported
to have been diagnosed with Ewing sarcoma, a rare bone/soft-tissue cancer (US incidence on
the order of 1–3 per million children per year; ~200–250 diagnoses nationally per year). In
2026 the reported concentration drew local, state, and federal attention, including an
updated data review involving the California Cancer Registry, UC Irvine, and the Orange
County Agricultural Commissioner's Office, coordinated with the Orange County Health Care
Agency. (All case details in this platform are *publicly reported and not independently
verified*; see `CLAIMS_AND_LIMITATIONS.md`.)

## 2. Purpose

Build the most rigorous, transparent, evidence-grounded **public-source** research system
possible to help determine:

1. Whether there is a statistically unusual concentration of verified Ewing sarcoma / other
   pediatric cancers in the relevant population.
2. Whether affected individuals may share environmental, geographic, temporal, residential,
   school, park, recreational, water, landscaping, agricultural, or occupational exposures.
3. What pesticide products / active ingredients were documented, likely, or plausibly used
   over time.
4. What public evidence is currently available online.
5. What evidence remains unavailable and would require final-stage records requests,
   institutional cooperation, consent, medical verification, or laboratory testing.

**This project is hypothesis-neutral.** Pesticide exposure is a major hypothesis to
investigate, not an assumed cause.

## 3. Primary research questions

- **RQ1 (Occurrence).** Given the community's size, age structure, and ancestry
  composition, how many Ewing sarcoma cases would be *expected* over the reported period,
  and how does the *reported* count compare — under a transparent range of assumptions?
- **RQ2 (Shared exposure).** What environmental features (documented pesticide application
  areas, former agricultural land, water/recycled-water systems, drainage, oil & gas
  proximity, environmental-cleanup sites, roadways, construction history) are shared across
  the community, and which are characterizable from public data?
- **RQ3 (Chemical relevance).** Which active ingredients plausibly used in the area have
  toxicological or epidemiological profiles that warrant deeper review?
- **RQ4 (Alternative explanations).** Which non-pesticide hypotheses remain viable and how
  strongly does public evidence support investigating each?
- **RQ5 (Evidence gaps).** What specific missing evidence would most improve the analysis,
  and through what lawful channel could it be obtained?

## 4. Hypothesis set (all investigated with equal rigor)

H1 Landscaping/ornamental pesticides on common areas · H2 Former agricultural land use &
soil residues (DDT/toxaphene/arsenic on ex-farmland) · H3 Imported soil/fill during
grading · H4 Drinking water contaminants · H5 Recycled irrigation water · H6 Construction-
era dust/exposure · H7 Ambient/traffic air pollution · H8 Oil & gas development proximity
· H9 Industrial / environmental-cleanup sites · H10 Landfill (Prima Deshecha) proximity ·
H11 Parental occupational exposure · H12 Household/consumer product use · H13 Genetic /
ancestry susceptibility (European-ancestry predominance) · H14 Population age structure &
high child fraction · H15 Diagnostic/referral clustering & ascertainment · H16 Statistical
coincidence / boundary-selection artifact.

Each hypothesis has an entry in the evidence review and a status: *actively supported by
public evidence for further review* / *plausible, under-characterized* / *weakly supported*
/ *not supported by available evidence*.

## 5. Study design

Descriptive, hypothesis-generating, public-source ecological study. **Not** a causal or
individual-level analysis. Explicitly guards against: ecological fallacy, multiple
comparisons ("Texas sharpshooter"), boundary-selection bias, residential-mobility bias,
ascertainment bias, and confounding by ancestry/age structure.

## 6. Study zones & time scope

See `CLAUDE.md`. Zone A (Ladera Ranch), Zone B (5-mile ring), Zone C (objectively matched
South OC comparison communities). Primary window Jan 2005–present; extended Jan 2000–
present; land-use history as far back as records allow.

## 7. Population definitions

- Denominator: person-years for children (0–19, with 5-year bands) resident in Zone A by
  year, from Census/ACS/CA Dept. of Finance, with uncertainty ranges for a high-growth,
  high-turnover community.
- Comparison: same, computed for Zone C communities and for California statewide, using SEER
  and California Cancer Registry baseline rates stratified by age, sex, and race/ethnicity.

## 8. Outputs

Source registry · publicly reported health-event registry (aggregate) · literature registry
& evidence matrix · GIS layers & interactive map · chemical library · pesticide-data
coverage analysis · site inventory · historical land-use layer · water & environmental
layers · demographic & incidence-scenario notebooks · exposure-screening framework ·
preliminary findings report · ranked final-stage evidence gates.

## 9. Ethics

Governed by `ETHICS_AND_PRIVACY.md`. Aggregate-only health data; no individual
