# Phase 2: the site

**Summary.** In August 2026 a concrete rectangle with iron pipe rails still leading up to it was
located **on foot**, in ungraded open space above Ladera Ranch beside the Arroyo Trabuco golf course.
Its floor measures about **12 feet**, the floor length specified for a federal cattle-dipping vat in
USDA Bureau of Animal Industry Circular 183 (1911) [A1, S-USDA-C183]. It is unfenced, unmarked, and
sits beside a public trail. **This is a candidate, not an identification.** Nothing here establishes
that the structure is a dipping vat, and nothing will until the soil is tested. Phase 1 asked whether
California ever looked. Phase 2 found something to look at.

> **CLASSIFICATION.** Structure and setting: **field observation** (August 2026). Herd arithmetic and
> mass figures: **MODEL ESTIMATE**. Florida: **[A2]**, official state record. Imagery and elevation:
> **[A1]**. That the structure *is* a dipping vat remains **HYPOTHESIS**, and is what a soil test
> would settle.

## 11.1 The find
[field observation, August 2026]

The structure sits in the **open space above the community, beside the golf course**, on ground never
graded for housing. No coordinates are published here, and none will be: an unfenced structure of
unknown chemistry beside a public trail should be closed and marked by an agency, not crowd-visited.
What can be described without locating it:

- A **poured concrete rectangle**, its floor roughly **12 ft** in length, consistent with the federal
  specification for a swim-vat floor [A1, S-USDA-C183].
- **Iron pipe rails** still in place, running up to the structure, the geometry of an approach chute
  that funnels animals single-file.
- **No fence, no sign, no marking** of any kind.
- A **public trail** passes near it, and a **rope tire swing** hangs on the approach, which is to say
  the ground around it is in casual recreational use, including by children.

That last point is why this chapter exists. Dip vat or not, an unmarked structure of unknown chemistry
in an area of child contact is something an agency should characterise. The position Phase 1 took
holds: **never looked, not looked-and-cleared.** A candidate found by walking is not a finding; it is
a place to test.

## 11.2 Why one station is not enough
[MODEL ESTIMATE]

Federal quarantine rules required dipping on a **14-day cadence** through the season [A1, S-USDA-C174,
1910]. Against a herd of roughly **21,000** head [A1, S-USDA-C174] to **25,000+** by family accounts
of the O'Neill operation [B2, family history], a single vat cannot physically clear the herd on that
cycle. Cattle were worked where water was, so the stations followed the ranch's water points. The
arithmetic of cadence and herd size implies **three to seven vats** across the ranch system
[MODEL ESTIMATE, confidence low].

**One candidate has been found.** If the estimate is near right, the others are still out there,
unlocated and unmarked, under what is now open space, golf course and neighbourhood. That is an
argument for a systematic inventory, the thing Florida did and California never did.

## 11.3 The arithmetic
[MODEL ESTIMATE, from `reports/ARSENIC_QUANTIFICATION_MASTER.md`]

Using the published USDA California charge of **8 lb white arsenic (As₂O₃) per 500 gal** [A1,
S-USDA-C174] and the ranch's herd and cadence:

| Quantity | MODEL ESTIMATE |
|---|---|
| Whole-ranch arsenic mass, program lifetime | **6,692 – 39,748 lb** As₂O₃ |
| Fraction concentrated at a vat, pens and drip pen | **1,338 – 7,949 lb** |
| Resulting near-structure soil concentration | **128 – 763 mg/kg** |
| California background arsenic, for comparison | **1 – 11 mg/kg** [A2] |

The concentration range is the line that matters: **128–763 mg/kg against a background of 1–11 mg/kg**
is a point source a standard soil panel would resolve immediately, if anyone ran one. It is also
squarely inside the range measured at dip sites in Australia and the US Southeast (chapter 72), which
is the empirical anchor for the whole model.

The film states a lethal-dose comparison, and it belongs here with its caveat attached, in full. At a
central estimate of roughly **21,000 lb** and an adult lethal dose of **100–300 mg**, the arithmetic
gives **32 to 96 million nominal doses**. The caveat is not optional and is reproduced verbatim:

> *This is arithmetic, not toxicology. Soil-bound arsenic is only partly bioavailable; nobody ingests
> soil in quantity; the number says one thing: the mass involved was large. It says nothing about
> exposure, which has never been measured here.*

The figure describes **the size of the bag**, not a dose anyone received. No exposure has been
measured in Ladera Ranch, and this project does not claim one occurred.

## 11.4 Florida: the answer key
[A2, official state record]

Florida ran the **same federal program** on the same schedule, and then did the thing California
never did: it looked.

- Florida **inventoried 3,281 vats** statewide.
- Of twelve sites tested, **eleven exceeded the state soil standard**.
- The state's own report records disused vats **reused as pesticide-mixing areas**, and **used by
  children as swimming holes**.
- Florida **fenced and remediated**.
- California has **never inventoried a single vat**.

Florida is not a hypothesis about what may sit in dip-site soil. It is a measured answer from a
comparable program, and the answer was: mostly above standard. The **G11** records response from
Florida DEP and the photographic exhibit of Florida vats are held with the cross-reference material;
note also the **Palm Beach gap**, a county absent from the state inventory, a reminder that an
inventory's silence is not a clearance. See `research/national_crossreference/` and
`research/national_crossreference/EXHIBIT_FL_VAT_PHOTOS.pdf`.

## 11.5 The pond and the drainage
[field observation + A1 imagery + A1 elevation]

Below the find, at the edge of the golf course, is a pond. The imagery record is unambiguous: it is
**absent in 1929, 1937, 1947, 1953, 1960, 1969, 1980 and 1990**, and **first appears in the January
2004 orthoimage** [A1, OC Survey imagery]. **The pond was built with the golf course.** It is not a
ranch-era feature, and nothing about it is evidence of dipping.

What makes it worth recording is position. On the 2018 county DEM [A1]:

| Feature | Elevation |
|---|---|
| Hillside at the find | ~421 ft |
| Pond | ~325 ft |
| Creek | ~280 ft |

The find sits about a hundred feet above the pond, and the pond sits above the creek. **Runoff from
that slope passes through the pond on its way to the creek.** That is a drainage relationship, not a
transport finding: no water, sediment or soil has been sampled anywhere along it.

Separately, a **1968 USGS field survey** mapped a **9,111 m² stock-water body** on the creek about
**240 m upstream** (~291 ft) [A1, USGS]. It no longer appears as open water; that corridor is now
riparian woodland. A ranch-era stock pond upstream of a modern golf-course pond is exactly the sort of
feature an inventory would want mapped.

One observation needs an explicit non-claim: the **tule ring around the pond looks dead** this season.
That has an ordinary seasonal explanation and **is not evidence of arsenic damage**. It is recorded
because it was seen, not because it means anything.

## 11.6 What has changed since Phase 1
[A2 / factual update]

Two things. **Some soil sampling in Ladera Ranch is now under way.** What it is testing for, and where
the samples are being taken, has not been shared publicly; this project has not been consulted on
locations and cannot say whether the dip signature is among the analytes. A sampling programme of
unknown scope is progress over none, but it does not answer the question this report poses unless it
targets the right places and the right chemistry. Separately, the author is now listed as an
**Advisory Member (Independent Researcher & Land Historian)** at
[protectladera.org/advisors](https://protectladera.org/advisors/) [A2].

## 11.7 The ask
Unchanged from Phase 1 in substance, sharpened by having a place to point at.

1. **Close and mark the trail segment** at the structure until it has been tested. This is a
   precaution, not an accusation.
2. **Sample, under hazardous-materials protocol:** the base itself; the pens footprint; the drip-pen
   side; the **tire-swing area** and trail margin, as the child-contact points; the **pond sediment**;
   and the **creek margin** below it.
3. **Analytes:** total **and bioavailable** arsenic; **lead alongside**, to separate the dip signature
   (arsenic trioxide, arsenic with little or no lead) from the orchard signature (lead arsenate);
   plus **speciation**.
4. **Independent of Ladera Ranch and of Rancho Mission Viejo**, conducted by the State.

**A negative result is a real result.** If the soil comes back at background, that is an answer this
project would publish as readily as any other, and the question closes on evidence rather than on
assumption. What is not acceptable is a third decade of neither looking nor clearing.
