## Why there is no letter here

The Clerk-Recorder does not respond to a narrative records request. It is an **index you search**,
then order specific documents by number. Sending a PRA-style letter would get a form reply telling
you to use the index. So the deliverable is a search workflow, not a letter, and it cannot proceed
until two inputs exist:

1. **Grantor/grantee names** — Appendix F (91 party name variants) ✅ exists
2. **Document numbers** — partially in Appendix E (182 known identifiers) ✅ exists

Both prerequisites are in fact met. What is missing is a **decision about money**: how many
documents to pull and at what cost. That is the user's call, not a drafting problem.

## Workflow

**Step 1 — search the public index (free).**
Use https://www.ocrecorder.gov/ Grantor/Grantee search. Search each party variant from Appendix F
across 1996–2011. Record document number, recording date, document type, and parties for every hit.

**Step 2 — triage before paying.** Rank hits by evidentiary value:

| Priority | Document type | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Notices of Completion** | closest recorded proxy to construction completion per tract |
| 2 | **Subdivision / tract maps** | recordation dates, phase boundaries |
| 3 | **Declarations of CC&Rs and annexations** | when each village came under LARMAC |
| 4 | **Grant deeds, first conveyances** | first sale, a **lower** bound on occupancy |
| 5 | Easements, dedications | infrastructure chronology |

**Step 3 — order only priority 1–2 first.** Get the cost per document from the counter before
bulk-ordering. Expand only if the first tranche proves useful.

## Do not claim from the response
A **Notice of Completion is not a certificate of occupancy.** A **first conveyance is not an
occupancy date** — it is a lower bound at best. A map recordation is neither. Every one of these is
supporting chronology only, and this project's standing rule is that none of them may be
substituted for a certificate of occupancy.
