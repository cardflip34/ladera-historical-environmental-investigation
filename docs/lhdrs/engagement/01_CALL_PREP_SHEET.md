# Call-prep sheet — first call with Candice Bond (Bond Legal, Irvine)

*Private. Not for sharing. Prepared 2026-08-10.*

## Goals for this call (in order)
1. Learn her plan: team, funding, timeline, and how she sees the council's soils effort.
2. Establish your role category: **retained research consultant**, not volunteer source.
3. Agree on next step: you send the engagement outline; substantive materials follow signature.
4. Leave her more interested than when the call started — without transmitting documents.

## Your 30-second framing (say roughly this)
> "Over the past two years I've built a documented research platform on the historic arsenical
> cattle-dipping program that operated on this land from 1907 to 1912 — federal specifications,
> quantified arsenic estimates from documented herd sizes, a site-targeting analysis built from
> nine decades of imagery, and completed field reconnaissance. It's source-graded, timestamped,
> and deliberately neutral: it documents what happened and where to test, and it does not claim
> causation — which is exactly why it holds up. I work on this as a retained consultant, and I'd
> be glad to send you an engagement outline."

## What you CAN describe freely (capabilities, at altitude)
- 1907–1912 compulsory dipping era: federal circulars held in full text with custody hashes.
- Quantified arsenic mass-balance ranges from documented cattle counts (model estimates, caveated).
- Site-targeting methodology: 1929–2025 imagery archive, federal survey layers, field visits.
- A written soils sampling plan including a dated-sediment site that can separate historic
  from modern arsenic sources.
- Ten drafted public-records requests; a complete source registry with confidence grades.

## What you do NOT share before a signed engagement
- Exact coordinates of any candidate structure or sampling target.
- The field photographs and reconnaissance results.
- The targeting package, mass-balance spreadsheets/JSONs, or any file transfer at all.
- Names or details of anyone in the community. (Never share family/health details — period.)

## Your questions for her (her answers size this up)
1. "Who else is on the team — co-counsel, environmental experts, epidemiology?"
2. "Who is the soils company, and who funds the testing?"
3. "How do you see the council's independent testing effort fitting with your investigation?"
4. "What's your working theory of the case and rough timeline?"
5. "How do you typically engage research consultants — and are you open to a licensing
   arrangement for pre-existing research?"

## Red flags (any of these → slow down, commit to nothing)
- Asks you to send "everything you have" before any agreement.
- Floats paying you a percentage of recovery. (Prohibited for experts/consultants —
  and it would be used to discredit your research. Decline on the spot.)
- Wants the council's soils testing brought under the firm's control or delayed.
- Resists the idea that hazardous findings get reported to health authorities promptly.
- Vague about co-counsel or funding for testing.

## Your red lines (state plainly if raised)
- Compensation is **non-contingent**: hourly consulting plus a license fee for existing work.
- Public-health disclosure is never delayed for litigation timing.
- Your research stays hypothesis-neutral; you document findings, not conclusions to order.
- Attribution: the research corpus is credited to you in any use.

## Closing script
> "This is exactly the kind of collaboration I hoped for. Let me send you a short engagement
> outline this week — once we have terms in place I can deliver the full targeting package
> quickly, and I think you'll find it saves your team months."
