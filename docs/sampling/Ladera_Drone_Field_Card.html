Ungraded Ground Only

Seven stations. Every one checked frame-by-frame against the 1990 county aerial — which predates both the golf course and the Ladera grading — and kept only where the ground is unchanged. Sixteen of the original twenty-three targets are gone from this plan.

1 October 2026 · 2025 OC Survey 1 ft, filtered against OC Survey 1990 · Hypothesis-neutral: nothing here asserts a dipping vat exists on this land

Two rules before anything else

This document carries locational data and is not for publication. The project's own rule is that no coordinates of the concrete feature or the pond appear on the public site. Keep geotagged originals off anything public until the staged-disclosure decision is made.

Do not dig or probe. If the feature is what it might be, the soil around it is the evidence, and disturbing it compromises the sampling that would actually settle the question. Photograph, measure from the air, leave the ground as you found it.

What came off the map, and why

The 1968 USGS survey put stock water at twenty-three points. That tells you where cattle congregated in the ranch era. It does not tell you whether that ground still exists.

The 1990 aerial is the filter

Orange County's 1990 countywide frame predates the Arroyo Trabuco golf course and predates the 1999–2003 Ladera mass grading. Comparing it to the 2025 frame at every single target separates ground that was cut, filled and shaped from ground that was only ever grazed.

The golf course was built on exactly the valley floor the 1968 survey marks as water — which is unfortunate, because it is also the ground a dipping station would most plausibly have occupied. That ground is gone, and no amount of flying recovers it.

The canopy problem, which changes the flight

Two of the seven cannot be mapped from directly overhead

D4 and D5 sit under closed oak canopy. This matters more than it sounds: a nadir mapping grid over closed canopy produces a beautiful orthomosaic of treetops, and the ground beneath stays completely invisible. It is the single most common way a drone survey of a wooded corridor wastes a day.

What to do instead at D4 and D5: fly the open creek channel and the gaps between crowns, low and slow; shoot obliques from outside the canopy edge, looking in under the branches; and accept that these two stations are reconnaissance rather than survey.

Where the grid does earn its battery: D1, D3 and D7 — scrub and open ground, where the camera sees soil.

What you are looking for — and what would rule it out

The vat
A narrow rectangle about 8 m long overall. USDA Circular 183 (1911): 26 ft at the top, 12 ft at the bottom. Deep — cattle swam it.
The drip pen
A wider apron at one end, sloping back toward the vat so the dip drained in and was reused. Usually the largest surviving concrete, and the most contaminated ground on a site.
The pens
A funnel of fence lines converging on the entrance, fed by a straight narrow alley. Reads as a V in the vegetation long after the posts are gone.
The cut
Often dug into a low bank so cattle entered at grade and climbed out a ramp. Look for a notch nothing natural explains.
The water
Within about 30 m. Thousands of gallons a charge; nobody carried it far.
The scar
A bare or stunted patch that slope and aspect do not explain. Early October is the best soil-mark season of the year.

Shoot the rule-outs just as carefully

  • Shallow and open with a pipe inlet, no ramp — a water trough.
  • Circular or square pad, anchor bolts, no apron — a tank base.
  • Walls on four sides with a doorway — a building foundation.
  • No ramp, no apron, no pen lines anywhere around — probably not a dipping station at all.

A well-photographed negative closes a question. This project has more open questions than it needs.

Waypoints, and how to get them into the aircraft

Ninety-nine waypoints across the seven stations, with ground elevation pulled from the USGS 1 m DEM at every single one.

The altitudes are terrain-corrected, and that only holds if you launch at the station centre

Every altitude in the waypoint files is relative to the launch point, and each one has been adjusted by the difference in ground height between that waypoint and the station centre, so the aircraft holds a constant height above the ground rather than above wherever it took off.

It matters most where the ground moves under the flight path: D7 has 22.1 m of relief across its grid, D6 has 14.3 m, D6b 12.3 m, D3 11.6 m.

If you launch somewhere other than the station centre, these numbers are wrong. Either re-launch at the centre or add the ground-height difference yourself. And keep terrain-follow and obstacle avoidance on regardless — this is a plan, not a guarantee.

D1 — generate it yourself

The coordinate of your August find is deliberately not stored in the project, so its waypoints are not in the pack. Run this with your own coordinate and it writes the identical set into out_D1/, pulling real ground elevation for every point:

python3 d1.py 33.5xxxxx -117.6xxxxx

It produces the Litchi orbit and grid CSVs, a KML with the survey polygon, a GPX and a plain list — same structure, same corrections as the other seven.

What is in each station

Orbit8 points on a 40 m radius, 28 m above ground, gimbal −35°, heading toward the centre, one photo per point. Every station has one.
Grid150 × 150 m, 30 m line spacing, 60 m above ground, nadir. D2, D3 and D7 only — plus D1 when you generate it.
No gridD4 and D5 have no grid and that is deliberate. They sit under closed oak canopy, where a nadir mapping run photographs treetops and the ground stays invisible.

Flight settings

Nadir grid60 m (200 ft) AGL, 75% front, 65% side overlap — an orthomosaic at roughly 1.5 cm/px, which means you can take real dimensions off it and test the 26 ft / 12 ft specification instead of eyeballing it. D1, D3, D7 only.
Low obliques10–20 m AGL, about 35° down, from all four cardinal directions. A ramp reads as a ramp from one direction and as nothing from another.
Second sun angleRepeat the D1 grid in the evening at the same altitude and track. The comparison is the evidence.
FormatRAW plus JPEG. Geotags on. Note the clock time of every flight — sun azimuth is what makes the sets comparable.
ScaleA 1 m object in at least one frame at D1, or the orthomosaic scale rests entirely on GPS.

What to come back with

  1. A complete nadir set for D1, morning and evening, overlapping enough to build an orthomosaic.
  2. A measurement of the concrete floor that does not depend on memory — the scale object in frame.
  3. How far the iron rails run, and in which direction. They lead somewhere; from the air you can see where.
  4. Whether there is a second concrete surface. The apron is the thing that would most change the reading.
  5. Any bare patch, with enough surrounding ground to judge whether slope and aspect explain it.
  6. The GPS track log, so every frame can be placed later.

Before you launch

Airspace. Check at the launch point. At or below 400 ft AGL, visual line of sight, not over people.

Land. Orange County Parks and the City of Mission Viejo restrict launching and landing on their open space without a permit. The Arroyo Trabuco Golf Club and the Rancho Mission Viejo land east of the corridor are private. Launch from public right-of-way, or with permission.

Season. October. Check for red-flag closures, and watch for rattlesnakes and poison oak anywhere you walk in.
