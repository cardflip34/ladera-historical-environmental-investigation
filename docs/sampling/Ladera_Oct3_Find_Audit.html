The 3 October Drone Find

Four photographs, one landmark, and a structure that is not yet identified. What arrived, what it shows, where it is, what it might be, what would settle it, and the one thing that would collapse most of this uncertainty in about thirty seconds.

Audited 3 October 2026 · Hypothesis-neutral · Nothing here identifies the structure as anything

The thirty-second fix, before anything else

The files that reached me had their EXIF stripped and were downscaled — photos 1 and 3 arrived at 1125 × 843, photo 2 at 1500 × 1124, all with zero EXIF tags and no DJI XMP block. I checked for both.

Your originals on the SD card still carry GPS latitude, longitude and altitude, relative altitude above the launch point, gimbal pitch and yaw, flight yaw, focal length and the timestamp. From a single nadir frame that gives me the structure's position to within a few metres and its exact dimensions in centimetres — both of which are currently unresolved and are doing real damage to the analysis below.

What to send: the original DJI_*.JPG files straight off the card or from the DJI album, by AirDrop or by copying the folder. Do not re-export, resize, screenshot, or send through anything that re-encodes. One nadir frame and one oblique is enough; all of them is better.

1 · Where it is

The landmark worked. The position did not come out of it as precisely as it could have, but the search area is now small and specific.

The landmark

The Home Depot at 27952 Hillcrest, Mission Viejo — 33.55675, −117.66050. Geocoded from OpenStreetMap and then confirmed against the county's 2025 one-foot aerial: the building footprint, the garden centre at its south end, the curving access road, the adjacent solar-roofed building and parking structures to the south-west, and — decisively — the open scrub with its network of dirt trails running south and south-east of the store, which is the ground in your photos 1 and 3.

That places you on the western flank of the Arroyo Trabuco corridor, on the Mission Viejo side, below the Hillcrest commercial block.

The search area, and the honest size of it

Longitude −117.6618 to −117.6566, latitude 33.5516 to 33.5566. Roughly 480 m east–west by 555 m north–south, now drawn on the drone map in red.

Confidence is split, and the split matters.

The landmark buildingHigh. Independently geocoded and visually confirmed against county imagery. A2
That you were in the scrub south of itHigh. The vegetation, the trail network and the view geometry all agree. A2
The structure's actual coordinateUnresolved. I could not resolve it in the 2025 three-inch county imagery — the structure is small, partly under brush, and the county frame is a different season. UNKNOWN

One EXIF-intact frame closes that last row completely.

And a discrepancy that has to be resolved before anything is built on this

The project's own Phase 2 record describes the August 2026 find as being "in ungraded open space above Ladera Ranch beside the Arroyo Trabuco golf course." The golf course is on the eastern side of the corridor. The ground in these photographs is on the western side, below a Mission Viejo commercial centre, roughly a kilometre away across the creek.

Those are two different places. So one of three things is true, and I cannot tell which from what I have:

  1. Same structure, imprecise earlier description. Most likely if the August visit was on foot and the written description was approximate.
  2. A second, different structure — in which case this is a new find and the August one is still where it was.
  3. One of the two descriptions is simply wrong.

Resolve this first. If these are two structures rather than one, the whole picture changes: two concrete features on opposite flanks of the same drainage is a different investigation from one.

3 · What it might be, ranked by what the imagery supports

I am going to put the least exciting reading first, because that is where the evidence currently points

RankReadingFor / against
1Water conveyance
concrete-lined ditch, flume, or pipeline encasement with a valve or air-release vault		For: the long narrow proportion; the pipe running the full length and beyond; the rectangular framed aperture, which reads like a valve vault or meter box; the position along a slope rather than on flat ground. Against: nothing I can see yet.
2Dipping vat with associated pipeworkFor: concrete, metal rails, abandoned in ranch-era open space. Against: a federal-specification vat is about 8 m long overall and relatively wide; this reads much longer and much narrower. A vat is not a thing you see end-on and mistake for a ditch.
3Weir, diversion or headgateWould explain the framed aperture well. Needs the drainage context to assess.
4Structural foundation remnantPossible, but foundations are rarely this linear without returns, and I see no corners.

Why say this plainly. The project rule is that a concrete structure is not a dipping vat unless evidence establishes that identification. Right now the evidence leans the other way, and recording that is worth more than hedging it. If this is a water line, saying so quickly is a result, not a loss.

And a water line here would still matter — for a reason worth holding onto

A dipping station cannot exist without water delivered to it. Thousands of gallons per charge, and nobody carried it far — the sampling plan already uses a 30 m rule for that reason.

The 1968 USGS field survey mapped two stock-water points on this same slope: T11 at 33.55057, −117.66081 and T12 at 33.55143, −117.66048, about 580 and 690 m south of the Home Depot. They are stations D6 and D6b on the drone map, and I had ranked them lowest of the seven with the dismissive note "houses within 150 m."

That ranking was wrong and I have corrected it on the map. Those "houses" are the Hillcrest commercial block — the very landmark in your photograph — and the two water points sit on the same slope as your structure. If that dark element is a pipe, the thing to do is follow it, in both directions, and see what it was feeding.

4 · What to do next, in order

#ActionWhy it is in this position
1Send the original filesResolves the coordinate and the scale simultaneously. Every ambiguity in section 3 is a scale problem. Nothing else on this list is worth doing first.
2Settle the two-structures questionIs this the August find or a second feature? Compare against your August photographs — rim, rails, corner, timber post. If the August one is on the golf-course side, you now have two.
3Re-fly it properlyNow that the ground is known: nadir grid at 60 m with 75/65 overlap for a measurable orthomosaic, plus low obliques from four sides, at both ends of the day. The waypoint generator takes the coordinate and builds it.
4Measure on the groundOverall length. Width at the top and at the bottom. Depth. Wall thickness. Pipe diameter. Whether the pale element is an open channel or solid. A tape and a photograph with the tape in frame settles rank 1 against rank 2 on its own.
5Trace the pipe both waysIt runs beyond the concrete. Where does it come from and where does it go? That answers what the structure was part of.
6Look for what a vat would need and a ditch would notA wider apron draining back toward the structure. A funnel of pen lines. A ramped end. Their absence is informative.
7Establish who owns the groundThis is Mission Viejo, not Ladera Ranch. Different city, different open-space authority, probably a different water district. The records trail changes with the location.
8Request as-builtsIf it is a water line, somebody has a drawing of it. Santa Margarita Water District and the City of Mission Viejo for anything on that slope; the county for the pre-incorporation era.

And still: do not dig

Whatever this turns out to be, the soil around it is the only thing that can answer the question the project actually exists to ask, and disturbing it compromises the sampling. Photograph it, measure it, leave it.

5 · What this does not establish

  • It does not identify the structure. Not as a vat, not as a water line, not as anything.
  • It does not establish the structure's coordinate — only a 480 by 555 metre area it sits inside.
  • It does not establish that this is the August 2026 find. The two location descriptions do not agree.
  • It does not establish any contamination, anywhere, of anything.
  • It does not establish a connection to any reported illness.

What it establishes is narrower and still worth having: there is a concrete and metal structure of unknown identity in ungraded open space on the western flank of the Arroyo Trabuco corridor, on the same slope as two mapped 1968 stock-water points, and we now know which 27 acres to look in.
