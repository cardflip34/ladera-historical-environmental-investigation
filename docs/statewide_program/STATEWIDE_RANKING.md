# Statewide Land-Use Screening — Priority Ranking (Phase 4)

> **No community named here is asserted, implied, or found to be contaminated.** The only
> measured soil fact anywhere in this program is **"unstudied."** This document ranks *where an
> unanswered question is widest* — i.e. where a former cattle rancho that sat in a tick-dipping
> quarantine county is now under homes, and no known historical environmental review ever
> reached the ~1912 end of the mandatory arsenical-dipping era. **The only thing that can
> resolve that question for any community is an actual soil test for arsenic (and associated dip
> chemistry).** A high rank means "worth asking the question here first," never "likely
> contaminated." Nothing below is a finding about any place; it is a queue for where to look.

## Scope of this synthesis — read before the table

This Phase-4 run was handed completed per-target scrub results for **six targets: T01–T06**
(the Orange / Ventura / San Diego residential ranchos). It ranks **only those six**. Deep-dive
records for **T08–T12** exist in `results/` but were **not** included in the input to this
synthesis, and **T00, T07, T13–T15** have no scrub record supplied. To avoid fabricating
findings, those targets are **not ranked here**; they remain listed at the bottom as "not in
this synthesis batch." A later run must re-execute Phase 4 against the full result set.

**How to read every row (this governs the whole table):**

- **Review gap (yrs):** how far the deepest available historical review (aerial / CEQA / Phase I
  ESA floor) sits *above* the ~1912 end of mandatory arsenical dipping. A wider gap means more of
  the dipping era is invisible to any review ever performed — it does **not** mean anything was
  found on the land.
- **Dipping proximity:** in **every one of these six targets, NO cattle-dipping vat is named to
  the specific ranch.** Where dipping is called "documented," that documentation is at the
  **county / regional** scale only and is reported as a reason the question is *open*, never as a
  site finding. **Absence of a located record is not evidence that no facility existed** — several
  primary archives (State Veterinarian and County Agricultural Commissioner tick-eradication
  files; local historical societies) were offline or unsearched, and CDNC / Library of Congress
  endpoints returned 403/404 to automated queries (a tooling limit, not a substantive negative).

## Summary

This synthesis ranks the **six targets whose deep-dive scrub results were supplied to it**
(T01–T06), scoring each on the two stated axes — the breadth of the historical
environmental-review gap, and how well *nearby* dipping is regionally documented (site-unconfirmed
in all six). The three **San Diego County** ranchos — **T06-San Dieguito, T05-San Bernardo, and
T04-Peñasquitos** — rank highest because they combine the widest, structurally unreachable gaps
with the strongest documented county-scale dipping context: San Diego was one of only four
California counties still under federal cattle-tick quarantine as of April 1, 1910, with arsenical
dipping compelled under the 1907 state law. On each of these tracts the entire ~1906–1912 dipping
window predates the oldest available aerial photography (or, for San Bernardo, was never reviewed
at all because the community was built before CEQA existed), so no review ever performed could
reach it. Critically, **no cattle-dipping vat is named to any of these three ranches** — the high
rank reflects a maximally *wide, open, unanswered question*, not a located facility and emphatically
not a soil result. **T03-Simi** (Ventura, a documented compelled-dipping quarantine county) follows
at Medium, its best-case headline gap being narrower (15 yr); the two **Orange County** ranchos,
**T01-Trabuco and T02-Irvine**, also sit at Medium — substantial 26-year gaps, but the county-scale
dipping plausibility that elevates the San Diego and Ventura rows was **not documented** in the
materials searched, leaving dipping near them neither confirmed nor excluded.

Two caveats govern every row. First, each "dipping proximity" entry rests partly on records that
could not be reached — CDNC and Library of Congress endpoints repeatedly returned 403/404 to
automated queries, and key primary archives (State Veterinarian and County Agricultural
Commissioner tick-eradication files, local historical societies) were offline or unsearched — so
every entry is an **absence of a located record**, categorically different from evidence that no
facility existed. Second, and most important: this table orders **where to look first to close an
open question**, not where any risk is higher. Ranking is a triage of *uncertainty*; the single
fact that could turn any of these open questions into an answer — for a High-ranked community or a
Medium one alike — is a **direct soil arsenic test**, which has not been performed for any of them.
Nothing here asserts, implies, or supports that any community is contaminated.
