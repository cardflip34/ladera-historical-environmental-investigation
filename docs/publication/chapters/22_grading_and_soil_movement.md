# Grading, Cut-and-Fill, and Possible Soil Redistribution

Mass grading is the mechanism that would connect a small historical contamination source to a
large modern residential area. It is also the mechanism that would make such a source
extraordinarily difficult to find afterwards.

This chapter sets out what is established about grading here, what is inferred, and what is
unknown — which is most of it.

---

## 22.1 What is established

::: classification fact
**Ladera Ranch was mass-graded.** This is not in dispute and is directly visible in the
before-and-after imagery: the 1929–1974 frames show natural drainage-cut hillslopes; the 2022
frame shows engineered pads, benched slopes, and re-contoured drainages.
:::

The confirming detail comes from an unexpected direction. The Oso Grande School Phase I
assessment discounted pesticide risk partly on this reasoning:

> Any pesticides and/or herbicides at the site were likely mixed and/or placed at lower depths
> during the development of the site.

and separately noted that surface soils "were removed prior to fill placement."

That is a description of soil being **moved**, offered as a reason not to sample. Whether it is
reassuring depends entirely on where the material went — which the document does not say.

## 22.2 Why grading cuts both ways

The instinct is to treat grading as either burial (good) or dispersal (bad). It is neither
reliably.

**Dilution is real.** A concentrated point source mixed through a much larger volume of fill
produces lower concentrations everywhere. For a contaminant with a concentration-dependent
health effect, that genuinely reduces risk at any single location.

**But dilution converts a findable problem into an unfindable one.** A twenty-metre vat footprint
is a target. The same mass of arsenic spread across forty acres of fill at low concentration is
not detectable by any sampling design anyone would think to run, because nobody would know where
to run it.

**And burial depth is not fixed.** Material placed at depth during grading can be returned to the
surface by later utility trenching, pool excavation, landscaping, or foundation work.

::: classification limit
**None of the above is a finding about Ladera Ranch.** It is a description of what mass grading
does to a hypothetical point source. No contamination has been demonstrated here, so there is
nothing established to have been diluted, buried, or redistributed.
:::

## 22.3 The measurement that cannot be used

Buildings in this footprint sit on a median slope of **1.7°** against **6.7°** for available land
across the same extent.

That looks like strong evidence that housing was placed on valley bottoms — where water was, and
therefore where cattle were worked.

**It is circular.** The flatness under a house today was manufactured by the grading. The modern
elevation model describes engineered pads, not the landform anyone chose between.

::: classification limit
**EM-008 · Methodological exclusion.** This figure is recorded rather than omitted because it is
persuasive and will be rediscovered by anyone who runs the same analysis. It cannot support a
conclusion about original siting.

**What would work instead:** reconstructing pre-1980 elevations from the 1948 and 1968
topographic contours, which are held by this project. That would give original landform
independent of the grading. It is tractable and has not been done.
:::

## 22.4 What is not known

This is the substance of the chapter, and it is mostly absence.

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Cut-and-fill volumes for Ladera Ranch | **Not obtained** |
| Which areas were cut, which were filled | **Not obtained** |
| Whether fill was imported from off-site | **Not obtained** |
| If imported, from where | **Not obtained** |
| Whether any imported fill was characterised before placement | **Not obtained** |
| Depth of fill across the footprint | **Not obtained** |
| Whether grading plans reference historical land use at all | **Not obtained** |

::: classification open
**Grading and geotechnical reports for Ladera Ranch are not in any public repository searched.**
CEQAnet holds no attachments for filings of that vintage; EnviroStor and GeoTracker hold one
school-site record and two permitted fuel tanks between them.

These documents almost certainly exist — mass grading requires permits, plans, and compaction
reports. They are held by the county, the developer, or a geotechnical firm, and they have not
been requested. **This is a records-access gap, not a demonstrated absence.**
:::

## 22.5 Why this matters for sampling design

If soil investigation is ever undertaken, grading changes the design in two specific ways.

**Depth matters more than usual.** Contamination that predates grading may sit under fill rather
than at the surface. Surface-only sampling could return clean results over a buried source. Any
programme should sample at depth in cut-and-fill transition areas, which requires knowing where
those are — which requires the grading plans.

**Location logic partly breaks down.** The sampling frame this project produced — 41 water bodies
plus one ranch structure — assumes contamination stayed where it was deposited. Grading may have
moved it. The frame remains the best available starting point, because a point source is more
likely to be near its origin than anywhere else, but it should not be treated as exhaustive.

::: classification lead
**Obtaining the grading plans and cut-and-fill records is a prerequisite for competent sampling
design**, not an optional refinement. Sampling without them risks a negative result that means
nothing — clean surface soil over undisturbed fill would look identical to clean soil over
nothing at all.
:::

## 22.6 An honest summary

Grading is the most-invoked and least-evidenced mechanism in this investigation. It is
frequently offered — including by this project, in earlier drafts — as the explanation for how a
historical source could reach a modern community.

It remains a **plausible mechanism with no supporting documentation whatsoever**. No cut-and-fill
volume, no import source, no depth profile, no plan has been obtained. Until those records are in
hand, any statement about soil redistribution at Ladera Ranch is speculation with an engineering
vocabulary.
