# Historical Timeline

Every entry carries a source and a confidence level. Entries marked **UNVERIFIED** are recorded
because they appear in secondary accounts and are useful for orientation, but have not been
confirmed against a primary source by this investigation. They should not be relied upon.

Dates in the cattle-tick section are the most rigorously established in this timeline, because
they were the investigation's decisive question and were pursued to primary sources.

::: classification fact
**The Orange County quarantine window is therefore approximately February 1908 to March 1912.**

This **narrows** the "1907–1917" figure this project used in earlier work, which was a general
California or national approximation rather than an Orange County finding.

It also **works against this investigation's own imagery argument**: a programme ending in March
1912 leaves a **seventeen-year** gap to the earliest aerial photograph (1929), not the twelve
years previously stated.
:::

::: classification limit
**The unnamed "one large ranch."** Where period sources refer to dipping at an unnamed large
ranch, or to "only one ranch" remaining infected, **that reference has not been demonstrated to
be Rancho Mission Viejo.** Southern California had several very large cattle operations in this
era, and the four ranches actually named in 1908 — Joplin, Bixby, and the Capistrano and Yorba
districts — do not include it. Treating an unattributed reference as an identification would be
inference dressed as fact.
:::

## The two windows, side by side

This is the timeline's central observation, and the reason the investigation exists.

| | Period |
|---|---|
| **Orange County cattle tick quarantine** | ~Feb 1908 – Mar 1912 |
| **Earliest aerial photography that exists** | 1929 |
| **Earliest aerial photography anyone reviewed before development** | **1952** |
| **Ladera Ranch construction** | ~1999 onward |

The environmental review looked back to 1952. The activity of interest ended in 1912. **Forty
years separate them.**

::: classification limit
That gap is a **product of when the assessment was done**, not of how carefully it was done.
Phase I assessments must use *reasonably ascertainable* sources, and the aerial archive
available to consultants in 2002 began in 1952. The 1929–1947 county imagery this project used
is a modern digitisation.

It is a reason to look again. It is not a reason to conclude that anyone should have looked
harder at the time.
:::
