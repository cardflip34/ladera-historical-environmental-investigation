# Conclusion

This investigation set out to answer one question: **has every reasonable historical question
about this land already been considered?**

It can now answer that, and the answer is narrower and more useful than either the affirmative
or the accusation.

---

## 32.1 What was established

**The land was open cattle rangeland until development.** Confirmed across six aerial
photographs from 1929 to 1947, four USGS topographic editions, and the land-grant labelling on
the sheets themselves.

**Orange County was in the cattle tick eradication programme.** USDA Bureau of Animal Industry
Circular 174 (1911), page 285, lists it among counties "heavily infested," south of the
California quarantine line. Read directly from the primary source.

**Dipping actually happened in this county, under public order.** The *Los Angeles Herald* of
27 May 1908 records cattle being dipped at four named Orange County locations. Six to seven
hundred head at Capistrano alone. A companion article records a claim against the county after
six cows died following a dip supervised by the county veterinarian.

**The quarantine ran from approximately February 1908 to March 1912.**

**The environmental review of this land began in 1952.** Forty years after the dipping era
ended, and twenty-three years after the earliest aerial photograph that exists.

**No document obtained contains the terms "dip vat," "cattle dip," or "arsenical dip."**

**The one assessed site inside the community was cleared with no soil sampling.**

---

## 32.2 What was not established

**Nothing places a dipping vat on this land.** No vat, no corral, no map label, no photograph, no
purchase record, no employee account, no soil measurement. The four locations named in 1908 do
not include Rancho Mission Viejo, and the nearest — Capistrano at 3.4 miles — is outside the
community.

**No dip chemistry is named for any Orange County location** in any source located. The cattle
deaths are consistent with an arsenical dip. The sources do not say so.

**Nothing in this publication bears on the reported illnesses.** Not one finding here
establishes that any substance was present in Ladera Ranch soil, that anyone was exposed to
anything, or that any party caused any harm. That was never within the reach of a records
investigation, and it is not claimed.

---

## 32.3 The hypothesis, six links later

| # | Proposition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The programme applied to Orange County | **Established** |
| 1a | *Dipping occurred in the county, at named sites* | **Established** |
| 2 | This ranch dipped cattle at its own facility | Unevidenced |
| 3 | The facility stood within or near the footprint | Unevidenced |
| 4 | Arsenic residue persists there | Unevidenced |
| 5 | Development disturbed or built over it | Unevidenced |
| 6 | A pathway to human exposure exists | Unevidenced |

One link of six, plus a partial second. **The gap between "the programme reached this county"
and "a vat stood here" remains completely unbridged.**

The honest position is unchanged from where this publication began, though it now rests on far
more evidence: **the arsenical-dipping hypothesis is coherent, testable, and unsupported by any
site-specific evidence.** It should be neither asserted nor abandoned.

---

## 32.4 What this investigation actually found

Not a contaminant. A **gap in a historical window**, described precisely enough to be closed.

The environmental assessment looked back to 1952 using the aerial archive available in 2002.
The activity of interest ended in 1912. The imagery that would have covered it — 1929, 1931,
1937–38, 1946–47 — exists, is free, is public, and is a modern digitisation that was not
reasonably ascertainable when the assessment was done.

**That is a reason to look again. It is not a finding that anyone failed.** The consultants
complied with the applicable standard using the sources they had. This publication says so
plainly, in the audit chapter and here, because the distinction between *a gap* and *a failure*
is the difference between a document that can be acted on and one that will be dismissed.

---

## 32.5 What the evidence actually points at

A finding this investigation did not expect and did not go looking for.

The hypothesis that prompted all of this ranks **last** among the pathways examined, on
documentation. Meanwhile two plugged exploratory wells sit within about a mile of the community
centroid — one at roughly a quarter-mile — and that is the **only pathway with any published
epidemiological association with Ewing sarcoma specifically**. Suggestive, statistically
non-significant, and more direct than anything supporting cattle dipping.

It surfaced from correcting an error in this project's own geometry. It has received a fraction
of the analytical effort.

An investigation that reordered that table to protect its founding premise would forfeit any
claim to being taken seriously. It is stated here, and in the executive summary, at the same
volume as everything else.

---

## 32.6 Four corrections, one pattern

Four corrections were issued and published in full. Three share a shape:

**An assumption about the physical evidence was adopted without checking it, and the assumption
happened to make the analysis simpler.**

- A centroid inherited from a brief, 1.93 miles wrong (C-001)
- The earliest imagery assumed to be 1948, making a vat undetectable *in principle* (C-003)
- Having found better imagery, the target assumed to be large, making the negative result strong
  (C-004)

Every one was caught by going to a primary source or by rendering the data instead of trusting a
table. Two of them **weakened** conclusions this project had already published.

The countermeasures — mandatory counter-evidence on every claim, a published dead-end log,
source grading with no silent promotion, corrections issued in full — are not decoration. They
are the reason this document can be checked. They are also, demonstrably, insufficient on their
own: each correction was caught late, and there is no reason to believe a fifth is not sitting
in these pages right now.

---

## 32.7 What should happen next

**In an archive, not in the ground.** In order:

1. **The Michael Brandman Associates May 1995 Appendix H hazardous-materials ESA** — named in
   EIR 589, not online. It would answer, for the residential footprint itself, how far back the
   review looked. *Orange County Planning, (714) 834-2166.*
2. **Orange County Board of Supervisors minutes, 1907–1912.** County-ordered dipping means board
   action, appropriations, and an inspector appointment. The Levengood claim is dated and
   specific enough to locate.
3. **Anaheim Gazette and Santa Ana Blade on microfilm** — the papers of record, digitised
   nowhere. Target windows: Feb 1908, May–Aug 1908, Mar–Apr 1910, Mar 1912.
4. **Rancho Mission Viejo's own operating records** — private, and the single place a facility
   would necessarily have been documented.
5. **BLM GLO survey field notes** — where surveyors recorded springs, corrals and improvements
   that never reached the published plat. Requires a Login.gov account.
6. **The 1907 Governor's proclamation** at the California State Library — blocked to automated
   access by a bot filter, and probably retrievable in an ordinary browser in under a minute.

**Then, if the archival work supports it:** geophysics at candidate locations, and soil sampling
that tests **arsenic and lead together** — the ratio distinguishes an orchard lead-arsenate
signature (broad, both elevated proportionally) from a dipping-vat signature (tiny, arsenic
without proportionate lead). Local background must be established first, or no result can be
interpreted.

---

## 32.8 The bottom line

The reported pattern warrants investigation. The available evidence does not establish
causation, and this publication does not attempt to.

What it offers is a **sampling frame and a records request**: forty-one mapped water bodies, one
ranch structure, a named document sitting in a county office, and a forty-year window that
nobody has yet looked into with the imagery that now exists.

That is less than the investigation set out to find. It is what the evidence supports, and it is
enough to act on.
