# Agricultural Pesticides and Other Potential Exposure Pathways

A report that examines only one hypothesis is not an investigation; it is an argument. This
chapter sets out every environmental pathway that has been identified as potentially relevant
to this community, and grades each on the same scale — including the pathways that are better
documented than the arsenical-dipping hypothesis that prompted the work.

Several of them are.

---

## 20.1 How these are ranked

Each pathway is assessed on two independent axes, which are frequently confused:

**Documentation** — how well established is it that this thing was present or occurred here?
**Plausibility of exposure** — if present, how credible is a pathway to human contact?

A pathway can be well-documented and low-exposure (a plugged well a mile away), or
poorly-documented and high-exposure-if-real (a dip vat under a park). Collapsing these into a
single "risk" score would hide exactly the distinction that matters.

---

## 20.2 Documented: arsenic in soil at nearby school sites

::: classification fact
**EM-013 · ESTABLISHED FACT · Confidence: High**
DTSC found arsenic in soil at several school sites on former agricultural land near Ladera
Ranch.
*Supporting:* EnviroStor records for Carl Hankey Elementary, the Plant Depot School Site, and
San Juan Elementary list arsenic among contaminants identified.
*Counter-evidence:* Arsenic occurs naturally in California soils at background concentrations,
and presence alone establishes neither an anthropogenic source nor any link to cattle dipping.
Former orchard land carries **lead arsenate** residues from an entirely different practice —
insecticide application to fruit trees — which is a far more common explanation for arsenic on
former California agricultural land than cattle dipping.
*Citation:* DTSC EnviroStor site records [S-ENV-01].
:::

This is the **best-documented arsenic finding in the study area**, and it is important to state
plainly that it does not support the dipping hypothesis over the orchard hypothesis. Lead
arsenate was applied to California orchards for decades and is the default explanation for
elevated arsenic on former orchard ground. Where lead and arsenic are elevated *together* in
roughly the ratio of the compound, that is a lead-arsenate signature. A dipping-vat signature
would show arsenic strongly elevated in a very small area **without** proportionate lead.

That distinction — the lead-to-arsenic ratio and the spatial extent — is testable, and is the
single most useful discriminator available. It is developed in the recommendations chapter.

## 20.3 Documented: organochlorine pesticide residues

::: classification fact
**ESTABLISHED FACT · Confidence: High**
DDT and its breakdown products, chlordane, and toxaphene have been identified in soil at former
agricultural sites in the immediate area.
*Supporting:* EnviroStor records for San Juan Elementary (chlordane, DDD, DDE, DDT, plus lead
and arsenic) and Ambuehl Elementary (DDD, DDE, DDT, toxaphene).
*Counter-evidence:* These findings are at **school sites on former agricultural land**, not
within the Ladera Ranch residential footprint. Extrapolating them to Ladera Ranch soil is not
supported by any measurement.
*Citation:* DTSC EnviroStor site records [S-ENV-01].
:::

Organochlorines are persistent, though unlike arsenic they do degrade. Their presence confirms
that this general area carries a legacy agricultural chemical burden — which is unremarkable for
former Southern California farmland and is exactly what the school-site assessment programme
exists to catch.

## 20.4 Documented: proximity to plugged oil and gas wells

::: classification fact
**ESTABLISHED FACT (proximity) / INVESTIGATIVE LEAD (relevance) · Confidence: High for
geometry**
Two plugged exploratory wells lie within approximately one mile of the community centroid, one
at roughly 0.25 miles. Six lie within ten kilometres.
*Supporting:* CalGEM WellSTAR database coordinates, distances recomputed by haversine from the
corrected community centroid.
*Counter-evidence:* These are mid-twentieth-century **plugged dry holes**, generally a lower
leakage risk than production wells. The community's drinking water is imported rather than
drawn from local groundwater. Proximity is a screening signal, not a dose.
*Citation:* CalGEM WellSTAR [S-ENV-02]; correction C-001.
:::

This pathway deserves particular attention because it has something the dipping hypothesis
lacks: **a published epidemiological association with the specific cancer in question.** A 2026
peer-reviewed California study reported a suggestive but non-significant association between
residential proximity to abandoned oil and gas wells within 10 km and childhood Ewing sarcoma
(OR 1.27, 95% CI 0.96–1.66), stronger in Hispanic children.

A confidence interval spanning 1.0 is not a positive finding, and the study's authors did not
present it as one. But it is more direct evidence than exists for any other pathway considered
here — and it emerged from a correction to this project's own geometry, not from looking for it.

::: classification lead
**This pathway is under-investigated relative to its evidential support.** The well-integrity
records, plugging documentation, and any soil-gas or methane monitoring near the two nearest
wells have not been obtained. Given that this is the only pathway with a published association
with Ewing sarcoma specifically, it arguably warrants higher priority than the hypothesis that
prompted this investigation.
:::

## 20.5 Documented practice, undocumented here: landscape pesticide application

::: classification lead
**INVESTIGATIVE LEAD · Confidence: Low**
Master-planned communities maintain extensive irrigated common landscaping under professional
contract, and landscape maintenance is a recognised pesticide-use category in California's
reporting system.
*Supporting:* California Pesticide Use Reporting data include a landscape maintenance category.
*Counter-evidence and a hard limit:* Analysis of the state PUR archive established that
**94.6%** of 79,473 Orange County records could not be located more precisely than a large grid
cell, and within landscape maintenance specifically, only **22 of 15,383 records (0.1%)** carried
usable coordinates. The state reporting system, as it exists, **cannot** place applications
within Ladera Ranch. This is a limitation of the data, not a finding about the community.
*Next step:* HOA and landscape-contractor records, which are private but not necessarily
inaccessible.
:::

This is a good example of an appealing hypothesis defeated by data quality rather than by
evidence. The question remains open because the instrument cannot measure it.

## 20.6 Plausible, undocumented: imported fill and soil redistribution

::: classification lead
**INVESTIGATIVE LEAD · Confidence: Low**
Mass grading redistributes near-surface soil across a development, and imported fill may
originate off-site entirely.
*Supporting:* Ladera Ranch was mass-graded; this is not in dispute and is visible in the
before-and-after imagery.
*Counter-evidence:* No evidence has been located that imported fill was contaminated, and no
cut-and-fill records or import sources have yet been obtained. This is a *mechanism*, not a
finding.
*Why it matters regardless of source:* grading is the mechanism by which a localised point
source — if one existed — could be spread across a much wider area at lower concentration. It
converts a small, findable contamination footprint into a large, diffuse, and much harder one.
:::

## 20.7 Present by default: natural background arsenic

::: classification context
**HISTORICAL CONTEXT · Confidence: High**
California soils contain naturally occurring arsenic, with regional background concentrations
varying by geology.
:::

This is the **null hypothesis for every arsenic finding in this investigation**, and it must be
defeated by evidence before any anthropogenic explanation is entertained. Any future sampling
programme that does not establish local background concentrations first will be unable to
interpret its own results.

## 20.8 Pathways assessed and not supported

Recorded because ruling things out is part of the work.

**Drinking water.** The community's water is imported and treated, subject to routine regulatory
monitoring. No pathway is supported by available evidence.

**Airborne dust from construction.** Real during the active construction period and now
historical. No monitoring data from that period have been located, so this cannot be assessed
either way — it is unresolvable rather than ruled out.

**Industrial contamination.** No significant industrial facilities have been identified within
the study zones that would suggest a pathway.

**Recycled irrigation water.** Used for common-area landscaping in the region; no evidence of
contamination has been identified.

## 20.9 Where the evidence actually points

Setting aside the hypothesis that prompted this work and reading the evidence on its own terms:

| Pathway | Documentation | Exposure plausibility | Published link to Ewing sarcoma |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arsenic at nearby school sites | **Strong** | Moderate — off-footprint | None |
| Organochlorines at school sites | **Strong** | Moderate — off-footprint | None |
| Plugged oil/gas well proximity | **Strong** | Low–moderate | **Suggestive, non-significant** |
| Natural background arsenic | Strong | Low | None |
| Landscape pesticides | Weak — unmeasurable | Unknown | None |
| Imported fill / grading | Weak | Unknown | None |
| **Arsenical cattle dipping** | **None located** | **Unknown** | **None** |

The hypothesis that prompted this investigation currently sits at the **bottom** of this table
on documentation. It has generated the most analytical work and produced the least evidence.

That is stated plainly because it is the honest reading, and because a project that quietly
reordered this table to protect its founding premise would forfeit any claim to being taken
seriously. The dipping hypothesis remains worth resolving — it is testable, the contamination
would be persistent, and a negative result would close it permanently. But it is not currently
the best-supported explanation for anything, and this publication does not present it as one.
