# Executive Summary

## What this investigation is

An independent reconstruction of the land-use history of Rancho Mission Viejo and the community
of Ladera Ranch built on it, undertaken to answer one question: **has every reasonable
historical question about this land already been considered?**

It was prompted by reported pediatric Ewing sarcoma cases in the community. It is **not** an
attempt to explain them, and nothing in it establishes that any party or substance caused any
illness. It is a records investigation, and its subject is the completeness of a historical
record — not the health of any individual.

## What was found

**The land was open cattle rangeland until development.** Confirmed across six aerial
photographs spanning 1929 to 1947, four USGS topographic editions, and the land-grant labelling
on the sheets themselves. No structures are resolvable inside the community footprint in any
pre-1950 frame except a single building on Trabuco Creek.

**Aerial photography of this land exists back to 1929 — eighteen years earlier than this
project previously believed.** Orange County publishes it through a public service that had
been missed for a mechanical reason: an unconventional URL path. The 1937–38 frames resolve at
**1.15 feet per pixel**.

**No cattle dipping vat or corral complex was found.** Zone A was divided into a grid and
searched systematically, tile by tile, at full resolution — twenty-four tiles across two
epochs. At 1.15 ft/px a corral complex would be unmistakable. Nothing was found.

**The 1968 USGS field survey mapped 41 surface-water bodies in the study footprint**, sixteen
inside the community. These were extracted from the printed hydrography ink, meaning every one
was drawn by a surveyor who visited the ground.

**Every one of those 41 water bodies now has a house within 500 metres.** The median distance
from a ranch-era water source to the nearest home is **66 metres**.

**But homes were not preferentially built on them.** Tested against 10,618 building footprints
and a matched null: enrichment **0.97×, p = 0.51**. The old water sites are no more built over
than any other ground. Both results are true because development covered nearly everything, so
proximity to housing stopped carrying information.

## What was not found

**No evidence that arsenical cattle dipping occurred on this land.** No vat, no corral, no map
label, no photograph, no purchase record, no employee account, and no soil measurement places it
here.

More fundamentally: **it is not yet established that California's tick eradication programme
applied to Orange County at all.** That is the first link in the hypothesis chain and it remains
unverified against primary sources. A negative answer would close the question entirely.

**No soil sampling for arsenic appears to have been conducted across the residential footprint.**
Nearby *school* sites were assessed — California's Education Code requires it before a district
acquires land — and arsenic was found at several. Residential subdivisions carry no equivalent
statutory duty. This asymmetry is a matter of law, not of anyone's conduct.

## The most important caution

The hypothesis that prompted this investigation is currently the **least** well-documented of
the pathways examined.

| Pathway | Documentation | Published link to Ewing sarcoma |
|---|---|---|
| Arsenic at nearby school sites | Strong | None |
| Organochlorines at school sites | Strong | None |
| Plugged oil/gas well proximity | Strong | **Suggestive, non-significant** |
| Landscape pesticides | Unmeasurable from state data | None |
| **Arsenical cattle dipping** | **None located** | **None** |

Two plugged exploratory wells lie within about a mile of the community centroid — one at
roughly a quarter-mile — and this is the only pathway with any published association with Ewing
sarcoma specifically. That association is suggestive and statistically non-significant, and its
discovery came from correcting an error in this project's own geometry rather than from looking
for it.

## Two corrections that changed the argument

Both are published in full rather than quietly revised.

**The imagery premise was wrong.** This project had argued that a dipping vat was undetectable
*in principle* — the earliest imagery was 1948, three decades after dipping ended, at a
resolution where a vat would be a single pixel. Aerial photography from 1929 exists, at
resolution where a corral complex is obvious. The conclusion did not change; its basis did. It
now rests on adequate imagery rather than on an excuse.

**A promising spatial pattern did not survive testing.** The observation that homes appear to
sit exactly where the ranch water sources were is descriptively accurate and causally
unsupported. It was tested and it failed. It is retained in full, including the reasoning that
made it appealing.

## What should happen next

**Archival, in priority order.** The County Agricultural Commissioner and California State
Veterinarian tick-eradication files, and USDA Bureau of Animal Industry records — these can
resolve whether Orange County was ever in the programme, which is cheap and decisive. Then
Rancho Mission Viejo's own operating records. Then General Land Office survey **field notes**,
in which surveyors recorded springs, corrals, and improvements that never reached the published
plat.

**Physical, if archival work supports it.** Ground-penetrating radar at the Trabuco ranch node
and the mapped water bodies would detect buried concrete directly. Soil sampling should test
**arsenic and lead together** — the ratio distinguishes an orchard lead-arsenate signature
(broad, both elevated in proportion) from a dipping-vat signature (tiny, arsenic elevated
without proportionate lead). Local background must be established first or the results cannot
be interpreted.

**Regarding the wells.** Well-integrity and plugging records for the two nearest wells, and any
soil-gas monitoring data, have not been obtained. Given that this pathway carries the only
published Ewing sarcoma association of any considered here, it arguably warrants more attention
than the hypothesis that prompted the work.

## The honest bottom line

The reported pattern warrants investigation. The available evidence does not establish
causation, and this publication does not attempt to.

On the specific question that prompted the work: **the arsenical-dipping hypothesis is coherent,
testable, and currently unsupported by any direct evidence.** It should be neither asserted nor
abandoned. It should be resolved — and there is a clear, affordable path to resolving it that
begins in an archive rather than in the ground.

The single most useful thing this investigation has produced is not a finding. It is a
**sampling frame**: forty-one specific locations, plus one structure, where the historical
activity of interest would have concentrated if it occurred at all.
