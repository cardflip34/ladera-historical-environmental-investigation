## EM-027 — Ownership of the Forster ranch holdings passed successively to Charles Crocker, then James Flood, then Richard O'Neill

**Confidence:** Low  ·  **Status:** Downgraded from ESTABLISHED FACT per C-006

**Supporting evidence.** Armor (1921): 'in the forties John Forster, having captured the heart of Don Pico's sister, secured the ranches also... on his death left such a heritage that... his heirs assumed indebtedness to keep the property; and when much of it was heavily mortgaged, it passed into the hands first of Charles Crocker, then of James Flood, and finally of Richard O'Neill.' Passage read and verified directly.

**Limiting or contradictory evidence.** SEE CORRECTION C-006. Armor contains substantive errors in this very passage: he calls the Mexican grants 'Spanish', states Mission Viejo 'originally belonged to the Picos' (Hoffman gives Agustin Olvera), and elsewhere conflates San Juan Cajon de Santa Ana with Canon de Santa Ana. THE CHARLES CROCKER LINK IS UNCORROBORATED and appears in no other source consulted. Secondary accounts of the O'Neill/Flood transaction conflict on year (1882 vs 1884), price ($457,000 vs ~$460,000), structure (equal ownership vs Flood-owned), and the half-interest date (1906 vs 1907). All are grade B2 or C. The primary record is a deed, which - Orange County not existing until 1889 - would sit in Los Angeles and San Diego County records.

**Citation.** Armor (1921), biographical sketch of John O. Forster


## EM-031 — The Ladera Ranch entitlement records are filed under 'Ladera', not 'Ladera Ranch', which likely explains why searches for the entitlement EIR have returned nothing

**Confidence:** Medium  ·  **Status:** Open - retest inconclusive; needs a records request, not a title search

**Supporting evidence.** The county's adopted zoning instrument is titled 'LADERA PLANNED COMMUNITY' - verified from the document cover. Every CEQAnet and county-repository search conducted in this investigation used the string 'Ladera Ranch' and returned zero entitlement records, including a complete half-year sweep of Orange County filings 1990-2000.

**Limiting or contradictory evidence.** RETEST ATTEMPTED AND INCONCLUSIVE (DE-012). Re-running the search with 'Ladera' alone against CEQAnet's serp endpoint returned zero - but a CONTROL QUERY for 'Irvine', a term certain to appear in thousands of Orange County records, ALSO returned zero. That endpoint is a site-content search, not the record search, and cannot answer the question either way. The structured record search has no free-text title field at all, which is itself the likeliest reason title-based searching has failed throughout. The hypothesis is UNTESTED, not refuted. Probing adjacent document IDs in the county store also failed - all return HTML error pages (DE-013).

**Citation.** Ladera Planned Community Program Text cover [S-LADERA-PC]; CEQAnet negative sweep, this investigation


## EM-034 — The complete US Land Commission case files for Mission Vieja/La Paz, Trabuco, and Niguel have been obtained but not yet read

**Confidence:** High  ·  **Status:** Open - obtained, requires palaeographic reading

**Supporting evidence.** Three PDFs downloaded and verified as real documents: 250 SD Mission Vieja/La Paz (96pp, 98 MB), 216 SD Trabuco (84pp, 75 MB), 130 SD Niguel (102pp, 87 MB). Land Commission files contain SWORN WITNESS TESTIMONY, which is where descriptions of ranch improvements - buildings, corrals, springs, fences - are recorded.

**Limiting or contradictory evidence.** THE TESTIMONY IS HANDWRITTEN CURSIVE, partly in Spanish, and the OCR layer is useless on it. A keyword scan for 'corral', 'spring' and 'adobe' returned zero hits, which reflects OCR FAILURE, NOT ABSENCE of those terms. Extracting anything requires page-by-page palaeographic reading, which has NOT been done. No claim is made here about what these files contain.

**Citation.** Bancroft Library via UC Berkeley digicoll; archived at evidence/documents/
