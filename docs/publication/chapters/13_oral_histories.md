# Oral Histories and Ranch Personnel

This chapter is nearly empty, and the emptiness is the finding.

---

## 13.1 What this investigation has

**No oral history has been collected. No interview has been conducted. No first-hand account of
ranch operations on this land has been located in any archive searched.**

That is the complete state of the record.

## 13.2 The names the documentary record does give

Four individuals connected to Orange County cattle dipping are named in period sources. None is
connected to Rancho Mission Viejo.

| Name | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Dr. Coleman** | Deputy State quarantine officer, consulting with Orange County cattle raisers, May 1908 | *Los Angeles Herald*, 27 May 1908 |
| **Dr. W. S. McFarlane** | Orange County veterinarian; ordered the dipping and "supervised the mixing of the preparation used" | *Los Angeles Herald*, 25 Jul 1908 |
| **J. C. Joplin** | Rancher, Trabuco Canyon — dipping conducted at his ranch | *Los Angeles Herald*, 27 May 1908 |
| **E. J. Levengood** | Dairyman; claimed $400 against Orange County after six cows died following a county-ordered dip | *Los Angeles Herald*, 25 Jul 1908 |

Two further names come from the land records: **Agustín Olvera**, grantee of Rancho Mission Vieja
or La Paz in 1845, and **Juan (John) Forster**, the later claimant. **J. W. Mandeville** signed the
1858 survey certificate as US Surveyor General.

::: classification limit
**None of these people is documented in connection with dipping on the land Ladera Ranch now
occupies.** They are recorded because named individuals are traceable through deeds, probate,
census and newspaper records in a way that "a rancher" is not — and because the Levengood claim
in particular is dated and specific enough to locate in county board minutes.
:::

## 13.3 Why this gap is different from every other one

Every other line of inquiry in this report will still be available in ten years. Archives do not
decay on a human timescale. A document in the Bancroft Library, a plat at the BLM, a newspaper on
microfilm — all of these will be exactly as retrievable in 2036 as they are now.

**This one will not.**

The Orange County quarantine ended in **March 1912**. Anyone who witnessed it as an adult is long
dead. But the ranch operated as a working cattle operation for decades afterwards, and people who
worked it in the 1940s, 50s and 60s would have known the ground, the corrals, the water, and
quite possibly the older men who remembered the dipping years.

Those people are now in their eighties and nineties.

::: classification lead
**Oral history is the only time-limited lead in this investigation.** It should be prioritised
accordingly — ahead of archival work that can wait, because it cannot.
:::

## 13.4 How it should be done, if it is done

Method matters here more than in any other chapter, because the failure mode is not finding
nothing — it is manufacturing something.

**Ask open questions, not leading ones.** "Was there a dipping vat?" invites a helpful answer from
someone who can tell the question matters. "Where did you work the cattle, and what was there?"
produces testimony.

**Do not describe the hypothesis first.** Once someone knows what you are hoping to hear, their
memory reorganises around it, without any dishonesty on their part.

**Record verbatim.** Summarised recollection is not evidence.

**Grade it as C — unverified testimony** — and corroborate before it carries any weight. A single
uncorroborated account naming a vat location would be an investigative lead, not a finding.

**Ask what they do *not* remember.** "Was there ever anything like that?" answered with a clear no
from someone who worked the ground is itself useful, and negative testimony is systematically
under-collected.

## 13.5 What is being asked

If you or a family member worked on Rancho Mission Viejo, or knew people who did, the
investigation would value knowing:

- Where cattle were gathered, held, and worked
- Where the corrals, chutes, and loading pens were
- Where the water was — tanks, troughs, ponds, springs, windmills
- Whether cattle were ever run through a dip or a bath, and where
- What was said about the older days on the ranch
- Names of foremen, vaqueros, and veterinarians

**Please do not send medical information about any person.** See the invitation at the front of
this report for what can and cannot be accepted, and why.
