# Recommended Archival Work

Ordered by expected value per unit of effort. Each entry states what it would resolve and what a
negative result would mean — a recommendation that cannot fail is not a test.

**Archival work comes before ground work.** That sequencing is itself a finding: this
investigation spent considerable effort on imagery analysis, producing a negative result of
limited weight, before consulting a free 1911 government circular that established whether the
activity was even plausible in this county. The circular took minutes to obtain.

---

## 29.1 Top priority — the missing hazmat assessment

**Michael Brandman Associates, May 1995 — "Appendix H, Hazardous Materials Environmental Site
Assessment"**, for the Antonio Parkway Roadway Alignment and Land Use Plan.

**Resolves:** how far back the historical review reached *for the residential footprint itself* —
the question Ch. 17 can only answer for neighbouring land.

**Route:** Orange County Planning, **(714) 834-2166**. Public Records Act request. CEQAnet hosts
no attachments for filings of that vintage.

**If negative:** if it reviewed pre-1952 imagery and found nothing, the audit finding substantially
weakens and the hypothesis loses much of its remaining force. That is a valuable outcome.

## 29.2 Orange County Board of Supervisors minutes, 1907–1912

**Resolves:** dipping in Orange County was county-ordered, so there must be board action,
appropriations, and an inspector appointment. The *Pacific Rural Press* of 5 March 1910 records
the state veterinarian formally petitioning the board for a second tick inspector — so the board
was engaged with this in a documented way.

The **E. J. Levengood claim** (~$400, six cattle died, mid-1908) is dated and specific enough to
locate, and a claim file would name the ranch, the location, and possibly the preparation used.

**Route:** Orange County Archives, Santa Ana. Not digitised anywhere searched.

## 29.3 Anaheim Gazette and Santa Ana Blade, on microfilm

**Resolves:** quarantine proclamations were published as **paid legal notices** in the papers of
record. A *Pacific Rural Press* item of 1903 names both titles as exactly that.

**Neither is digitised anywhere.** Every Orange County finding in this investigation came from
*out-of-county* papers reporting inward — the *Los Angeles Herald* and *Pacific Rural Press* —
because CDNC holds only four OC titles and **none covers 1900–1909** (DE-011).

**Route:** microfilm at Orange County Archives, Santa Ana Public Library, or CSU Fullerton.
**Target windows:** Feb 1908, May–Aug 1908, Mar–Apr 1910, Mar 1912.

## 29.4 BLM General Land Office survey field notes

**Resolves:** surveyors recorded springs, corrals, houses, fences and improvements in their
running notes that never reached the published plat.

**Correction C-007 opened this up:** the western edge of the study area is **sectioned** land —
T7S R8W sections 12, 13, 24, 25 — not the unsectioned grant land this project had assumed
throughout. Ordinary section-line field notes should exist for it.

**Route:** `glorecords.blm.gov/s/` — **no login required**, contrary to this project's earlier
entry (DE-007, revised). The site loads logged out and returns thousands of results. The obstacle
is a scripting one, not an access one. Filter by State CA, Meridian San Bernardino, Township 7S,
Range 7W and 8W, and **check for a separate Field Notes record** attached to each survey — it is
historically a distinct document and easy to miss.

## 29.5 The Bancroft witness testimony — already on disk

**Three complete US Land Commission case files, 272 MB, in `evidence/documents/`:**
250 SD (Mission Vieja/La Paz, 96pp), 216 SD (Trabuco, 84pp), 130 SD (Niguel, 102pp).

**Resolves:** land claim proceedings turned on occupation and improvement, so sworn testimony
routinely describes buildings, corrals, springs and fences.

**Why it has not been done:** the testimony is handwritten cursive, partly in Spanish. The OCR
layer is unusable — a keyword scan for *corral*, *spring* and *adobe* returned zero hits, which is
**OCR failure, not absence**.

**Route:** palaeographic reading. This needs a person who reads mid-nineteenth-century Spanish and
English cursive, not more searching. It is the cheapest lead here in dollar terms and the most
expensive in skill.

## 29.6 Rancho Mission Viejo's own operating records

**Resolves:** dipping was a mandated activity generating inspection records, chemical purchases,
and correspondence with state authorities. This is the single place a facility on this ranch would
necessarily have been documented.

**Route:** private corporate archive. No public access route. Requires a direct approach.

## 29.7 The 1907 Governor's proclamation

California State Library, executive-order collection, `5910-5913.pdf`. Returns **HTTP 403** to
automated access — a bot filter, not a paywall.

**An ordinary browser would probably open it in under a minute.** It would date the programme's
California commencement precisely and define its boundaries in the state's own terms.
