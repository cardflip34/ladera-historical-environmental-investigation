# Environmental Review Audit

This is the chapter the investigation was built to produce. The question it answers is not
whether anyone did anything wrong. It is narrower and more answerable:

**When this land was assessed before development, how far back did the historical review
actually look?**

The documents have been obtained and read. Every figure below was verified directly against the
source PDFs, which are archived in the evidence directory.

## 16.2 The Phase I historical source window

::: classification fact
**EM-022 · ESTABLISHED FACT · Confidence: High**

**The Phase I historical review of aerial photography began in 1952.**

*Supporting.* EIR 589 Appendix I states, in identical language across all nine Planning Areas:
"Photographs dating from **1952 to 1999** were reviewed at Continental Aerial Photo" (two areas
read 1953). Topographic maps: "dating from 1948 to 1988." Verified by direct text extraction
across all 218 pages.

The Oso Grande Phase I — the one document inside the Ladera Ranch footprint — reviewed **five
aerial photographs: 1952, 1968, 1977, 1989, 1994**. No earlier aerial appears anywhere in it.

A regex sweep for pre-1952 years across the documents returned only 1920, 1942, and 1948 —
map references, not aerial photography. **No 1929 or 1938 imagery appears in any document
obtained.**

*Counter-evidence.* This describes the documents **obtained and read**. It does not describe
documents that exist but were not located — see §16.5.
:::

### What that window excludes

The Orange County cattle tick quarantine ran from approximately **February 1908 to March 1912**.
The Phase I historical review began in **1952**.

**The dipping era falls forty years outside the earliest photograph anyone reviewed.**

This is not a criticism of the consultants. A Phase I ESA under ASTM 1527 requires review back
to first developed use or 1940, whichever is earlier, using *reasonably ascertainable* sources.
Continental Aerial Photo's holdings began in 1952. **The consultants complied with the standard
using the sources available to them.**

But it produces a specific, structural consequence:

::: classification lead
This investigation obtained aerial photography of the same ground from **1929** — twenty-three
years earlier than the review window — from a free public county service.

The 1929, 1931, 1937–38, and 1946–47 frames were **not reasonably ascertainable in 2002** in the
way they are today. Orange County's historical imagery service is a modern digitisation. The
gap is a product of when the assessment was done, not of how carefully it was done.
:::

## 16.4 Soil sampling: point-source only

::: classification fact
**EM-024 · ESTABLISHED FACT · Confidence: High**

**No footprint-wide soil sampling programme appears in any document obtained.** Sampling was
recommended only at discrete recognised environmental conditions: a used-oil release at the
Field Office, two removed 12,000-gallon underground storage tanks, a ~500-gallon tank at Cow
Camp, buried equipment and waste scraps, and former pistol ranges (for lead and copper).

A Phase I ESA is by definition non-sampling under ASTM 1527. That is the standard working
correctly — but it means the *absence* of sampling is a property of the instrument, not a
finding about the soil.
:::

### The one site inside Ladera Ranch, in detail

Oso Grande School — 10 acres at Sienna Parkway and O'Neil Drive, inside the community footprint.
EnviroStor records its past use as **"AGRICULTURAL – LIVESTOCK"** and its site history as: "Since
before 1942 and up to 1999… part of a larger cattle ranch, used for dry farming and grazing."

**No soil sampling was performed.** Pesticides were addressed **qualitatively**:

> Although the site may have been in light agricultural use (dry farming), pesticides and/or
> herbicides are not suspected within the near-surface soils beneath the site. Any pesticides
> and/or herbicides at the site were likely mixed and/or placed at lower depths during the
> development of the site.

DTSC required a Preliminary Endangerment Assessment on 8 September 2003, then on 7 October 2003
accepted the Addendum and concluded that "further environmental investigation of the site is not
required." That conclusion rested on a site visit and geotechnical borings **reviewed for
methane and organics** — not for pesticides or arsenic.

Final EnviroStor status: contaminants **"NO CONTAMINANTS FOUND"**, status **"NO ACTION
REQUIRED."**

::: classification limit
**"No contaminants found" here means no contaminants were looked for in soil.** The site was
cleared on a documentary and visual basis. That is a legitimate outcome under the applicable
process. It is not a measurement, and it should not be read as one.

Note also the reasoning: contamination was discounted partly on the grounds that surface soils
"were removed prior to fill placement" — an argument about **grading**, which relocates material
rather than eliminating it.
:::

## 16.5 What could not be found, and why it matters most

::: classification open
**The Ladera Ranch entitlement EIR was not located.**

A complete half-year sweep of CEQAnet — 22 queries with lead agency Orange County across
1990–2000, plus 14 queries by county across 1993–1999, every slice under the row cap — returned
**zero** records matching Ladera Ranch. The only hit was a Santa Margarita Water District
negative declaration for water and sewer lines (SCH 1999031033).

The closest candidate is **SCH 1994031075**, the Antonio Parkway Alignment Study and Surrounding
Land Use Plan — 5,000 acres between Ortega Highway and Crown Valley Parkway, 9,800 units, three
school sites. The geography fits. **The identification is inferred and unconfirmed.**
:::

::: classification lead
**The single highest-value document in this entire investigation has been identified by name and
is not online.**

EIR 589 Appendix I, page 21, cites:

> Michael Brandman Associates, May 1995, Environmental Impact Report for the Antonio Parkway
> Roadway Alignment and Land Use Plan, **Appendix H — Hazardous Materials Environmental Site
> Assessment**.

This is the hazardous-materials assessment for the land Ladera Ranch was built on. It would
answer, for the residential footprint itself, the question this chapter can only answer for
neighbouring land: **how far back did the historical review go, and was cattle-era land use ever
considered?**

It is the top-priority records request arising from this investigation. CEQAnet hosts no
attachments for filings of that vintage — its own note reads that the document "was originally
posted before CEQAnet had the capability to host attachments." Contact: Orange County Planning,
(714) 834-2166.
:::

## 16.6 The audit conclusion, worded precisely

::: classification fact
**On the documents obtained and read:**

1. The Phase I historical review of aerial photography began in **1952**.
2. The Orange County cattle tick quarantine ended in approximately **March 1912** — forty years
   earlier.
3. No document obtained contains any reference to cattle dipping, dip vats, or arsenical dip.
4. No footprint-wide soil sampling programme appears in any document obtained.
5. The one site inside the community footprint was cleared with **no soil sampling**, on a
   documentary and visual basis, with borings reviewed for methane and organics only.

**On what this does not establish:**

The Ladera Ranch entitlement EIR was not located. Its hazardous-materials appendix — identified
by name — is not online. Tract-level grading and geotechnical reports, and any
developer-commissioned sampling, are not in the public repositories searched.

**The searchable materials examined in this investigation do not appear to identify cattle-era
land use as a potential source. That is not the same as saying it was never considered.** The
distinction is not a hedge; it is the difference between what was checked and what exists.
:::

The honest summary: this is a **documented gap in the historical window**, not a documented
failure. The pre-1952 record was, in 2002, genuinely difficult to obtain. It is easy to obtain
now. That is a reason to look again — not a reason to conclude that anyone should have looked
harder then.
