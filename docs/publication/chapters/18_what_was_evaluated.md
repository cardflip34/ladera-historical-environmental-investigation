# What Was Evaluated

The environmental review of this land was not cursory. This chapter records what it actually
covered, because an audit that only lists omissions is not an audit.

## 18.2 What the Phase I assessments did cover

Read in full, the assessments were thorough within their scope:

**Site reconnaissance.** Physical walkover, observation of storage, staining, containers,
structures, and evidence of releases.

**Regulatory database review.** Federal, state and local environmental databases for the site and
surrounding properties, at the radii ASTM specifies.

**Historical aerial photography.** 1952–1999, at intervals — the window examined in **Ch. 17**.

**Historical topographic maps.** 1942–1988, including the 1948 USGS sheet.

**Recognised environmental conditions, identified and pursued.** The assessments found specific
issues and recommended specific follow-up:

| Condition identified | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Used-oil release at the Field Office | Sampling |
| Two removed 12,000-gallon underground storage tanks | Sampling |
| ~500-gallon UST at Cow Camp | Sampling |
| Buried equipment and waste scraps | Sampling |
| Former pistol ranges | Lead and copper sampling (FEIR 589 MM 4.14-11) |

::: classification fact
**This is a Phase I working correctly.** It identified point sources from documentary and visual
evidence and recommended targeted sampling at each. The methodology is not in question.
:::

## 18.3 Agricultural land use — considered, and reasoned about

Former agricultural use **was** recognised. The Oso Grande assessment addressed it explicitly:

> Although the site may have been in light agricultural use (dry farming), pesticides and/or
> herbicides are not suspected within the near-surface soils beneath the site. Any pesticides
> and/or herbicides at the site were likely mixed and/or placed at lower depths during the
> development of the site.

DTSC engaged with it. On **8 September 2003** the department required a Preliminary Endangerment
Assessment. On **7 October 2003**, having received an addendum, it concluded that "further
environmental investigation of the site is not required."

::: classification limit
**The reasoning was documentary and visual, not analytical.** The conclusion rested on a site
visit and geotechnical borings that were reviewed for **methane and organics** — not for
pesticides or arsenic. No soil sample was analysed for either.

Note also the logic of the pesticide argument: contamination was discounted partly because
surface soils "were removed prior to fill placement." That is an argument about **grading**, which
relocates material rather than eliminating it. See **Ch. 22**.
:::

| Database | Records for Ladera Ranch |
|---|---|
| DTSC EnviroStor | **1** — Oso Grande School. Past use "AGRICULTURAL – LIVESTOCK". Status: NO CONTAMINANTS FOUND / NO ACTION REQUIRED |
| State Water Board GeoTracker | **2** — permitted fuel tanks at two retail stations. No cleanup cases |
| LUST / Cortese / cleanup sites | **None** covering the residential footprint |

::: classification limit
**"NO CONTAMINANTS FOUND" means no contaminants were sought in soil.** The site was cleared
without sampling. That is a legitimate outcome under the applicable process and it is not a
measurement — a distinction that matters because the phrase reads, to a non-specialist, as a
laboratory result.
:::

## 18.5 The fair summary

Environmental review of this land **happened**, was **documented**, engaged a **regulator**, and
**identified and pursued** specific contamination sources.

Its limits were the limits of its instrument and its era: a Phase I does not sample, ASTM
required review only to 1940 using reasonably ascertainable sources, and the aerial archive
available in 2002 began in 1952.

What follows in **Ch. 19** is what does not appear in it — worded with care, because the
distinction between *absent from the documents obtained* and *never considered* is the difference
between a finding and an accusation.
