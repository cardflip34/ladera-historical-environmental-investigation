# Scope, Methodology, and Scientific Caution

## What this investigation covers

**Geographically**, three nested zones:

| Zone | Definition | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Core** | Present-day Ladera Ranch: all villages, HOAs, parks, schools, trails, common areas, slopes, drainage corridors, and commercial centers | Primary study area |
| **B — Exposure ring** | Approximately five miles surrounding: Las Flores, Rancho Mission Viejo, Mission Viejo, Coto de Caza, Rancho Santa Margarita, San Juan Capistrano, Laguna Niguel, adjacent unincorporated Orange County | Sources that need not be underfoot to matter |
| **C — Comparison** | Talega, Aliso Viejo, Foothill Ranch and similar master-planned communities | Matched on **objective criteria only** — development age, demographics, landscaping intensity, climate, housing stock — and **never on cancer counts** |

That last constraint matters more than it may appear. Selecting comparison communities by
disease rate would manufacture the contrast the investigation is supposed to test. The
comparison set was fixed on development characteristics before any health data were consulted.

**Temporally**, the primary window is January 2005 to present, extended to January 2000 where
records allow. Land-use history reaches as far back as reliable public records permit — in
practice to the Spanish land grant period, with photographic evidence beginning in 1929.

Every dataset records its source publication date, observation period, retrieval date,
temporal precision, known update schedule, and whether the record is current, historical,
inferred, or archived.

## The four-way classification

Every substantive statement in this publication carries one of four labels. They are not
decorative. They encode how much weight a statement can bear.

**ESTABLISHED FACT** — Documented in a primary or official source, verified against that
source, and reproducible by a reader who follows the citation. Example: the 1968 USGS field
survey mapped 41 surface-water bodies within the study footprint. Anyone can pull that sheet
and count them.

**HISTORICAL CONTEXT** — Accurate general background that situates the investigation but is
not itself a finding about this specific land. Example: how arsenical cattle dipping worked
as a practice. True, well-documented, and *not* evidence that it happened here.

**INVESTIGATIVE LEAD** — A specific, testable proposition supported by partial evidence,
carrying a defined next step. A lead is a question with a method attached. It is not a
finding, and it is never presented as one.

**OPEN QUESTION** — Identified as material, and genuinely unresolved. These are stated
plainly rather than smoothed over, because the shape of what is unknown is itself a result.

## The evidence standard

Every major claim carries a claim ID, a classification, a confidence level, supporting
sources, and **limiting or contradictory evidence**. The counter-evidence field is mandatory.
Where a claim has no meaningful counter-evidence, that is stated explicitly rather than left
blank — an empty field is ambiguous between "none exists" and "none was sought."

Sources are graded on a fixed hierarchy:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A1** | Official machine-readable government dataset; peer-reviewed primary research; official registry publication; official agency report |
| **A2** | Official government webpage; regulatory filing; official meeting document; official GIS service |
| **B1** | University or research-institution report; systematic review; nonprofit technical report with transparent methodology |
| **B2** | Reputable news outlet quoting **named** sources or documents; public statements from identifiable stakeholders |
| **C** | Advocacy materials; law-firm summaries; community petitions; social media; unverified case counts; anonymous claims |
| **D** | Speculation; unsourced reposts; unsupported online claims |

A record carries exactly one grade, and **a lower grade is never silently promoted**. A claim
that appears in a newspaper (B2) does not become A1 because it is later repeated by someone
official; it becomes A1 only when the underlying official record is located and read.

## What was actually done

The methodology is deliberately mundane, because mundane methods are checkable.

**Imagery.** Historical aerial photography and topographic sheets were retrieved from Orange
County's public GIS services and the USGS, georeferenced to a single common extent
(−117.680 to −117.616 longitude, 33.520 to 33.575 latitude), and examined systematically —
Zone A was divided into a grid and each tile inspected at full resolution, rather than scanned
by eye for anything that jumped out. Georeferencing was verified by confirming that modern
landmarks land where they belong.

**Feature extraction.** Where a feature needed to be extracted from a historical map, the
method was chosen to maximize source quality rather than convenience. Surface water was taken
from the 1968 USGS sheet by isolating the printed cyan hydrography ink — meaning every
resulting feature was drawn by a surveyor who visited the ground. An earlier attempt to detect
water directly from the aerial photography by brightness and texture was **abandoned** after
review showed it was detecting hillslope shadow. Both the failure and the reasoning are
recorded in the dead-end log.

**Statistics.** Where a spatial claim is tested, it is tested against an explicit null model,
with the null reported alongside the result so a reader can judge the comparison rather than
accept a ratio on trust. Where the choice of null materially changes the answer, both are
reported and the fairer one is identified and justified.

## Scientific caution — the limits that do not move

Several limits apply throughout and are not overcome by any amount of additional archival work.

**Spatial coincidence is not exposure.** That a historical activity occurred where houses now
stand establishes proximity in space and nothing else. It does not establish that any material
was present, that it persisted, that anyone contacted it, or in what quantity.

**Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — and the reverse is also true.** No dipping
vat has been found in the imagery. That is meaningful, because the imagery is now good enough
that a vat complex should have been visible. It is *not* proof that none existed, because a
facility demolished and backfilled before 1929 would leave no surface trace. Both halves of
that sentence are load-bearing.

**Ecological inference has hard limits.** Nothing here is an individual-level analysis.
Community-scale patterns cannot be transferred to individuals, and this publication does not
attempt it.

**The health question is out of scope by design.** This is a land-use and records
investigation. Whether the reported cases represent a statistical excess, and if so why, is a
question for authorized epidemiological analysis with access to verified medical information —
which this project does not have and does not seek.

**Modern terrain cannot describe historical terrain here.** Ladera Ranch was mass-graded. Any
measurement taken from the modern land surface inside the built area describes engineered
pads, not original landform. This specifically invalidates one otherwise-attractive analysis,
which is documented in the GIS chapter rather than quietly omitted.

## Privacy and ethics

Fixed constraints, applied throughout, without exception:

- No identification or inference of individual children.
- No exact residential addresses or residential coordinates, ever.
- No inference of the schools, parks, or routines of specific children from any source.
- No scraping of private profiles; no deanonymization.
- No medical speculation about named individuals.
- Health data stored and presented at **aggregate level only**.
- No fabricated data, no silently filled missing values, no invented records.

Where a dataset would permit finer resolution than these rules allow, the rules win.

## Language discipline

The publication distinguishes throughout between: verified fact, credible report, official
statement, scientific finding, public allegation, unverified case report, model-based
estimate, inference, hypothesis, and missing evidence. Phrases such as "this proves,"
"they covered it up," or "the cancer was caused by" do not appear, because the evidence does
not support them.

Where the public record appears not to contain something, the wording is careful and
deliberate: *"the searchable materials examined in this investigation do not appear to
identify…"* rather than *"they never studied…"*. The distinction is not hedging. A document
that exists but is not online, or that exists in a file cabinet in Santa Ana, is invisible to
this investigation and must not be declared nonexistent by it.
