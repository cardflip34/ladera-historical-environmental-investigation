## The six-link chain

The hypothesis that prompted this investigation requires six propositions to hold. This is where
they stand.

| # | Proposition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The cattle tick programme applied to Orange County | **ESTABLISHED** |
| 1a | Dipping occurred in the county, at named locations, under county order | **ESTABLISHED** |
| 2 | This ranch dipped cattle at its own facility | **Unevidenced** |
| 3 | A facility stood within or near the community footprint | **Unevidenced** |
| 4 | Arsenic residue persists at that location | **Unevidenced** |
| 5 | Development disturbed or built over it | **Unevidenced** |
| 6 | A pathway to human exposure exists | **Unevidenced** |

**One and a half links of six.** The gap between *the programme reached this county* and *a vat
stood here* is unbridged by any evidence.

## What is established

| Finding | Where |
|---|---|
| **Orange County was classified by the USDA as heavily tick-infested**, south of the California quarantine line — USDA BAI Circular 174 (1911), p. 285, read directly | Ch. 8 |
| **Dipping happened in Orange County in 1908** at four named locations, county-ordered, 600–700 head at Capistrano alone. Six cattle died and a claim was filed against the county | Ch. 8 |
| **The quarantine ran ~Feb 1908 to Mar 1912** | Ch. 6 |
| **The land was open cattle rangeland until development**, across six aerials 1929–1947 and four topographic editions | Ch. 14 |
| **Aerial photography exists back to 1929** at up to 1.15 ft/px, free and public | Ch. 14 |
| **41 surface-water bodies** were mapped by the 1968 USGS field survey, 16 inside the community | Ch. 15 |
| **Every one of those 41 now has a house within 500 m**; median distance 66 m | Ch. 15 |
| **The environmental review looked back only to 1952** — forty years after the dipping era | Ch. 16 |
| **No document obtained contains "dip vat", "cattle dip" or "arsenical dip"** | Ch. 16 |
| **The one assessed site inside the community was cleared with no soil sampling** | Ch. 16 |
| **The 1845 grant went to Agustín Olvera**, not Forster — Forster was the later claimant. 46,432.65 acres | Ch. 6 |
| **The patent rested on an 1858 survey**, not the 1867 survey commonly indexed | Ch. 6 |

## What was tested and did not hold

These matter as much as the positive findings.

| Proposition | Result |
|---|---|
| Homes were preferentially built on the ranch-era water sites | **Not supported.** 0.97×, p = 0.51 against a matched null |
| A dipping vat would be unmistakable on 1.15 ft/px imagery | **Wrong.** USDA documents wade tanks costing under $10, ~15 ft long |
| A dipping vat was undetectable in principle | **Wrong in the other direction.** 1929 photography exists |
| Building slope proves valley-bottom siting | **Circular.** Mass grading manufactured the flatness |
| The footprint is entirely unsectioned grant land | **Wrong.** The western edge is sectioned T7S R8W |
| Automated detection can find water or structures in the aerials | **Failed.** 4% accuracy on one pass; shadow artefacts on another |

## The finding that inverts the premise

Ranked on documentation, **the hypothesis that prompted this investigation comes last**.

| Pathway | Documentation | Published Ewing sarcoma link |
|---|---|---|
| Arsenic at nearby school sites | Strong | None |
| Organochlorines at school sites | Strong | None |
| **Plugged oil & gas well proximity** | Strong | **Suggestive, non-significant** |
| Natural background arsenic | Strong | None |
| Landscape pesticides | Unmeasurable from state data | None |
| Imported fill / grading | Weak | None |
| **Arsenical cattle dipping** | **County-level only** | **None** |

Two plugged exploratory wells sit within about a mile of the community centroid, one at roughly
a quarter-mile. That is the **only** pathway examined here with any published epidemiological
association with Ewing sarcoma specifically — and it surfaced from correcting an error in this
project's own geometry, not from looking for it. **Ch. 20**

## The single most important missing document

**Michael Brandman Associates, May 1995 — "Appendix H, Hazardous Materials Environmental Site
Assessment"** for the Antonio Parkway Roadway Alignment and Land Use Plan.

Named in county records at EIR 589 Appendix I page 21. Not online. It would answer, for the
residential footprint itself, how far back the historical review looked.

*Records request: Orange County Planning, (714) 834-2166.* **Ch. 16**

## What would settle the question

| Method | What it would resolve |
|---|---|
| **Soil chemistry — arsenic AND lead together** | The ratio separates orchard lead arsenate (broad, both elevated proportionally) from a dip vat (tiny area, arsenic without proportionate lead). Background must be established first |
| **Geophysics (GPR / EM)** | Detects buried concrete — but note a wooden wade tank would leave none |
| **GLO survey field notes** | Surveyors recorded springs, corrals and improvements that never reached a plat |
| **Rancho Mission Viejo's own records** | Dipping was mandated and generated paperwork |
| **OC Board of Supervisors minutes, 1907–1912** | County-ordered dipping means board action and appropriations |
| **The Bancroft witness testimony** (272 MB, already on disk) | Sworn descriptions of ranch improvements — needs palaeographic reading |

## What this report does not say

It does **not** establish that any substance is present in Ladera Ranch soil.

It does **not** establish that anyone was exposed to anything.

It does **not** explain the reported illnesses, and does not attempt to.

It contains **no** patient information, residential addresses, or case locations, and never will.
