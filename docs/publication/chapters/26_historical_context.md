## EM-015 — USDA-specified California dipping vats included small, low-cost designs that would not necessarily produce a large or photographically distinctive surface footprint

**Confidence:** High  ·  **Status:** Verified

**Supporting evidence.** USDA BAI Circular 174 (1911) p.299: cage vat materials cost $55-65 and the vat 'is installed near the corrals and requires only a short chute'; 'Draining pens with such vats are not essential and are rarely used, as the animal drains while the cage is being raised.' The wade tank 'can be constructed with an outlay of less than $10 for materials' and is 'recommended only in the disinfection of very small herds'. Figure 10 shows a vertical section approximately 15 ft long and 4 ft deep. Pages read directly.

**Limiting or contradictory evidence.** Circular 174 also documents larger swim vats (Fig. 7, ~2,088 gallons at 5 ft depth) which WOULD leave a substantial footprint. Which design, if any, was used on any particular ranch is unknown. This describes the range of possibilities, not what existed here.

**Citation.** USDA BAI Circular No. 174 (1911), pp.295-299, figs 7 and 10. archive.org/details/CAT31283802


## EM-016 — Arsenical dip solution was officially recognised as acutely poisonous, and USDA required a poison warning sign at every vat

**Confidence:** High  ·  **Status:** Verified

**Supporting evidence.** USDA BAI Circular 174 (1911) p.293 reproduces the required vat notice, reading in part 'WARNING! The fluid in this vat is POISONOUS to man and all animals.' Circulars 183 (1911) and 207 (1912) each carry a section headed 'Precautions in the use of arsenic' addressing absorption through cuts and vapour, and warning of livestock and human fatality.

**Limiting or contradictory evidence.** This establishes that the practice was recognised as hazardous and was federally documented. It says nothing about whether any such vat operated on this land, nor about the fate of any residue.

**Citation.** USDA BAI Circulars 174 (1911) p.293, 183 (1911) p.8, and 207 (1912). archive.org


## EM-017 — Photographs of Santa Margarita Ranch cattle operations circa 1900 exist, but depict San Diego County land, not the Ladera Ranch footprint

**Confidence:** High  ·  **Status:** Verified

**Supporting evidence.** Four photographs acquired from USC Digital Library / California Historical Society Collection: CHS-2358, CHS-2359, CHS-2360 (all dated 1900) and CHS-2085 (dated 1886). All are public domain.

**Limiting or contradictory evidence.** CRITICAL INTERPRETATION BOUNDARY: the repository titles place these on the Santa Margarita ranch in SAN DIEGO COUNTY - the Rancho Santa Margarita y Las Flores property, now largely Camp Pendleton. That is NOT the Ladera Ranch footprint. The properties shared ownership under the O'Neill and Flood interests and were partitioned in the 1940s, but they are different land. These images illustrate the ranching operation generally and must NOT be presented as depicting Ladera Ranch. Also: the repository spells it 'Marguerita' for CHS-2085, and dates it 1886, not the 1887 commonly cited online.

**Citation.** USC Libraries Special Collections, California Historical Society Collection 1860-1960; DOIs 10.25549/chs-m9636, m9635, m9638, m7221
