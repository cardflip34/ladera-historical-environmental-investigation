# What Does Not Appear to Have Been Evaluated

The wording of this chapter is deliberate throughout, and the deliberation is the point.

**"The searchable materials examined in this investigation do not appear to identify…"** is not a
hedge. It is the only claim the evidence supports. A document sitting in a county file cabinet, a
consultant's archive, or a developer's records is invisible to this project — and must not be
declared nonexistent by it.

---

## 19.1 Cattle-era land use

::: classification fact
**EM-023 · ESTABLISHED FACT · Confidence: High**

Full-text search across every document obtained:

| Term | EIR 589 App. I (582k chars) | 2016 PEIR (4.4M chars) | Oso Grande Phase I |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dip vat` | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| `cattle dip` | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| `sheep dip` | **0** | **0** | — |
| `dipping` | 1 — *unrelated* | **0** | **0** |
| `arsenic` | 1 — *unrelated* | **0** | **0** |
| `livestock` | — | — | **0** |

The two non-zero hits were checked individually. **"dipping"** (p. 181) is metal-finishing at an
industrial facility: "dipping, and welding/cutting operations." **"arsenic"** (p. 119) is
groundwater above the maximum contaminant level at a **mine-tailings** reservoir.

Neither concerns cattle.
:::

**What this means:** cattle-era land use as a potential contamination source does not appear in
the documents obtained. The tick eradication programme, arsenical dipping, and vat siting are
absent from the searchable record examined.

**What it does not mean:** that the question was never considered. The Ladera Ranch entitlement
EIR was not located, and its hazardous-materials appendix — identified by name — is not online.

## 19.2 Soil sampling across the residential footprint

::: classification fact
**No footprint-wide soil sampling programme appears in any document obtained.** Sampling was
recommended only at discrete recognised environmental conditions — a used-oil release, three
underground tanks, buried scrap, and former pistol ranges.
:::

A Phase I ESA is **non-sampling by definition** under ASTM E1527. The absence of sampling is a
property of the instrument, not a finding about the soil.

::: classification limit
**Tract-level grading and geotechnical reports, and any developer-commissioned sampling, are not
in the public repositories searched.** They almost certainly exist — mass grading requires
permits, plans, and compaction testing. They have not been requested.
:::

## 19.3 The specific analytical gap

This is narrower and more useful than "they didn't sample."

**A sampling grid designed for field-scale agricultural residue cannot find a dipping vat.**

Orchard lead arsenate and row-crop organochlorines were applied across whole fields. A grid sized
to characterise field-scale application samples the thing being sought.

A dipping vat is a point source perhaps **twenty metres across**. A field-scale grid can pass
directly over it and register nothing. Both investigations are summarised identically — *"soil
was sampled for arsenic on former agricultural land"* — and they are not the same investigation.

::: classification lead
**The audit question is therefore not "was soil sampled?" but "was sampling ever targeted at
locations where a point source would have stood?"**

In the documents obtained, the answer appears to be no — sampling was targeted at documented
twentieth-century point sources (tanks, oil releases, ranges), which is correct practice for the
conditions the Phase I identified. No pre-1952 land-use feature was among them, because no
pre-1952 imagery was reviewed.
:::

## 19.4 The document that would settle this

::: classification open
**EM-025 · OPEN QUESTION**

EIR 589 Appendix I, page 21, cites:

> Michael Brandman Associates, May 1995, Environmental Impact Report for the Antonio Parkway
> Roadway Alignment and Land Use Plan, **Appendix H — Hazardous Materials Environmental Site
> Assessment**.

This is the hazardous-materials assessment for the land Ladera Ranch was built on.

A complete CEQAnet sweep — 22 queries with lead agency Orange County across 1990–2000, plus 14 by
county across 1993–1999, every slice under the row cap — returned **zero** Ladera Ranch records.
CEQAnet hosts no attachments for filings of that vintage.

**Until this document is read, every statement in this chapter is provisional.** It may address
cattle-era land use in detail. It may not. Nobody outside the county currently knows.

*Records request: Orange County Planning, (714) 834-2166.*
:::

## 19.5 The audit conclusion

**On the documents obtained and read:**

1. Historical aerial review began in **1952**; the quarantine ended in **March 1912**
2. **No reference to cattle dipping, dip vats, or arsenical dip** appears anywhere
3. **No footprint-wide soil sampling** programme appears
4. The one site inside the community was cleared **with no soil sampling**, on documentary and
   visual grounds, with borings reviewed for methane and organics only

**On what this does not establish:**

The entitlement EIR was not located. Its hazmat appendix is not online. Grading records have not
been requested. Developer-held documents have not been sought.

::: classification limit
**This is a documented gap in the historical window, not a documented failure.** The pre-1952
record was genuinely difficult to obtain in 2002 and is trivially available now.

The correct conclusion is *look again* — not *someone should have looked harder*.
:::
