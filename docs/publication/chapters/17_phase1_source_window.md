# Phase I Historical Source Window

The single most consequential number in this investigation is a date on a consultant's source
list: **1952**.

This chapter examines that number — where it comes from, what standard governs it, and what it
does and does not imply.

---

## 17.1 The number, verified

::: classification fact
**EM-022 · ESTABLISHED FACT · Confidence: High**

EIR 589 Appendix I states, in identical language across **all nine Planning Areas**:

> Photographs dating from **1952 to 1999** were reviewed at Continental Aerial Photo

Two Planning Areas read 1953. Topographic maps: "dating from 1948 to 1988."

The **Oso Grande School Phase I** — the only assessment inside the Ladera Ranch footprint —
reviewed exactly five aerial photographs: **1952, 1968, 1977, 1989, 1994**.

Verified by direct text extraction across all 218 and 67 pages. A regex sweep for pre-1952 years
returned only 1920, 1942 and 1948 — all map references, none aerial photography.
:::

## 17.2 What governs the window

A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment under **ASTM E1527** requires historical review back to
**first developed use or 1940, whichever is earlier**, using sources that are *reasonably
ascertainable*.

That last phrase carries the weight. It does not mean "all sources that exist." It means sources
obtainable within reasonable time and cost by a practitioner exercising due diligence at the time
of the assessment.

Continental Aerial Photo — the commercial repository the consultants used — held coverage
beginning in 1952.

::: classification limit
**The consultants complied with the applicable standard.** This publication states that plainly
because the distinction between a *gap* and a *failure* determines whether this report is
actionable or merely accusatory.
:::

## 17.3 What was reasonably ascertainable then, and now

This is the crux, and it is a statement about **archive digitisation history**, not about
diligence.

| | 2002 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial aerial repositories | 1952 onward | 1952 onward |
| Orange County historical imagery | Physical holdings, no public digital service | **Public ArcGIS service, 1929 onward, free** |
| Practical earliest aerial | **1952** | **1929** |

Orange County's `Historic_Imagery_v2` service is a modern digitisation. In 2002 a consultant
seeking 1929 photography would have needed to know it existed, identify the physical holding,
and arrange reproduction — for imagery that, on the standard's own terms, was not required.

**Twenty-three years of photographic record separates what was reviewed from what exists.** That
gap was created by the passage of time and the digitisation of a county archive, not by anyone
cutting a corner.

## 17.4 The arithmetic that matters

| | Date |
|---|---|
| Orange County cattle tick quarantine begins | ~Feb 1908 |
| **Quarantine lifted** | **Mar 1912** |
| Earliest aerial photography that exists | 1929 |
| **Earliest aerial photography reviewed** | **1952** |
| Ladera Ranch construction | ~1999 |

**Forty years separate the end of the dipping era from the earliest photograph anyone reviewed.**

The activity of interest is entirely outside the reviewed window. Not marginally outside — four
decades outside.

## 17.5 What this does and does not imply

::: classification fact
**It implies:** the pre-1952 land use of this footprint was not examined photographically during
environmental review. Whatever the 1929–1947 imagery would have shown, nobody assessing this site
saw it.
:::

::: classification limit
**It does not imply** that a Phase I reviewing 1929 imagery would have found anything. This
project *did* review that imagery, systematically, and **found no vat and no corral complex** —
and then downgraded even that finding to low confidence on discovering that the cheap USDA vat
designs need not be visible at all.

A 1929-inclusive Phase I might well have produced the same clean result. **The gap in the window
is real; the assumption that closing it would change the answer is not supported.**
:::

## 17.6 Why it still warrants a second look

Three things are true simultaneously, and all three belong in any fair reading:

1. The historical window excluded the period of interest by forty years.
2. The consultants met the applicable standard using the sources available to them.
3. The imagery that would have covered the gap is now free, public, and takes minutes to obtain.

The third fact is what converts this from a historical observation into a recommendation. **The
cost of looking again is now close to zero.** That is a reason to look — not a reason to conclude
that anyone should have looked harder in 2002.
