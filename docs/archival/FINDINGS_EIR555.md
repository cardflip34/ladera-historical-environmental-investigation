# Archival finding: EIR 555, Resolution 77-866, and the mandated monitoring record

**Date:** 20 September 2026
**Method:** terminology sweep of 470 pages across six documents, plus OCR recovery of a
document that had no text layer. Full report: `Ladera_Terminology_Sweep.html`.

---

## 1. The Ladera environmental review is EIR 555, not EIR 589

Every prior search in this project ran against **EIR 589**, which governs the Ranch Plan —
roughly 22,815 acres east and south of Ladera Ranch, a different application and a
different footprint.

The environmental review for **Ladera Ranch itself** is:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Final EIR 555** | certified by the Orange County Board of Supervisors, **17 October 1995** |
| **Addendum PA970174** | approved by the Planning Commission, **7 April 1998** |

**Evidence.** The Ladera Planned Community Program Text cites EIR 555 twice — Exhibit 1-3
"Proposed General Plan Land Use Amendment," and "sub-measures A through Q of Mitigation
Measure 26." An Orange County Zoning Administrator summary minute for PA 180010, a project
located in Ladera Ranch, states verbatim:

> "Find that Final EIR 555, previously certified by the Board of Supervisors on 10/17/95
> and Addendum PA970174 previously approved by the Planning Commission on 4/7/98 together
> are approved for PA 180010."

The Program Text's own title page is dated 17 October 1995 — the same day the Board
certified EIR 555. The two sources corroborate independently.

**Grade:** A2 (regulatory filing + official meeting document).
**Status:** EIR 555 has **not been obtained**. It is now precisely requestable.

## 2. Archaeological monitoring of grading was a binding condition

The Ladera Planned Community Program Text, General Regulations, ARCHAEO/PALEO:

> "Prior to the approval of each Area/Subarea Plan or recordation of any final tract/parcel
> map (except for financing purposes), applicable County EMA Standard Conditions pertaining
> to archaeological and paleontological surveys, tests, reports, investigations, salvage,
> excavations and **observance of grading activities** in compliance with Board of
> Supervisors, **Resolution 77-866** shall be placed on each Area/Subarea Plan or
> Tract/parcel map in a manner meeting the approval of the Manager, Harbors, Beaches and
> Parks/Program Planning."

What this commits:

- Applies to **every** Area Plan and **every** tract map in Ladera Ranch.
- Includes **surveys and reports** — a negative survey still produces a filed report.
- Includes **observance of grading activities**, i.e. archaeological monitoring: a
  qualified person physically present while earth was moved, through the late 1990s and
  early 2000s.
- Routes through a named County approving authority with a file.

**Caveat, stated plainly.** 1990s monitoring was oriented toward prehistoric deposits and
fossils. A monitor encountering a poured concrete slab of twentieth-century date would
frequently classify it as modern debris outside the scope and not record it. The condition
guarantees **presence**, not **recording**.

## 3. Anything collected was required to be donated to the County

Orange County Board of Supervisors Resolutions **77-866** and **87-516** require all
County-permitted projects to be monitored, and finds to be donated to the County of Orange.
Those materials form the County of Orange Paleontology and Archaeology Collections — more
than six million fossils and artifacts, curated at a dedicated County facility.

This creates **two independent trails**:

1. **Paper** — monitoring and survey reports, filed at CHRIS / SCCIC (CSU Fullerton).
2. **Objects** — accession records naming the project each item came from, held by the
   County collection. `copa@ocparks.com` · 714-973-6655.

An accession search by project name is a different question from a literature search, and
it has not been asked.

## 4. A document in the evidence folder was unsearchable

`Ladera_Planned_Community_Program_Text_1995_rev2003.pdf` (136 pages) returned **135
characters of extractable text**. It is a pure image scan with no text layer. Every prior
keyword search over the evidence folder passed through it and returned nothing —
correctly and uselessly.

It was rendered at 200 dpi and OCR'd with the macOS Vision recogniser, producing 176,142
characters. Everything in sections 1–3 above comes from those pages. The OCR output is
committed as `Ladera_PC_Program_Text_OCR.txt`.

**Generalised lesson, now recorded in `LEXICON.md`:** before searching a document, confirm
it has text to search. Under ~200 characters per page means the search is meaningless.

## 6. The Phase I ESA contains none of its own figures, maps or site photographs

Every one of the nine Planning Area reports in EIR 589 Appendix I lists, in its table of
contents:

- **Plate 1** — Planning Area Location Map
- **Figure 1** — Site Location Map
- **Figure 2** — Site Map
- **Figure 3** — Aerial Photograph
- **Appendix A** — Assessors Parcel Map
- **Appendix B** — Zoning Information
- **Appendix C** — Environmental Records Search
- **Appendix D** — Orange County Fire Authority File Data
- **Appendix E — Site Photographs**

**None of them is in the 218-page document.** The file was walked object by object: it
contains nine raster images in total, each 113 x 172 px, one at the head of each Planning
Area section. They are the consultant's logo. Every section ends at "7.0 REFERENCES" and
the next begins.

**Interpretation.** Most likely mundane — the metadata shows the file was scanned on a
network print server in March 2004 for distribution, and large-format plates and photo
appendices routinely do not go through a sheet scanner. This is not evidence of
concealment.

**Why it matters anyway.** The photographs exist by reference. A consultant walked this
land and bound photographs into Appendix E of nine separate reports. That is a specific,
named, datable body of images, and it is requestable. Until now the project did not know
to ask for it. It also means every conclusion drawn from this document is drawn from its
text alone — anything recorded as a mark on a site map rather than as a sentence has not
been read.

**Also recovered from the text:** aerial photographs "dating from 1952 to 1999 were
reviewed at **Continental Aerial Photo** in Los Alamitos, California," stated fourteen
times across the nine reports, with frame identifiers printed (e.g. 261-8-29-114(1) for
1959). That is a named commercial archive and orderable frame numbers, and it independently
confirms the 1952 review-window finding.

## What this does not establish

- No dipping vat has been identified anywhere in Ladera Ranch. The Phase 2 concrete base
  remains an **unidentified concrete structure** with no sample taken and no lab result.
- No contamination is established at any location, and no exposure to any person.
- Nothing here establishes concealment. Every gap is explained by the scope of the
  instruments used.
- Section 2 establishes that a report series was **required to exist**, not that it
  records anything relevant.
- Section 6 establishes that figures and photographs are **absent from the obtained file**,
  not that they were destroyed or withheld.
