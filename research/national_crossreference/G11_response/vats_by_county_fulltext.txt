Cattle Dipping Vats in Florida
From the 1910's through the 1950's, these vats were filled
with an arsenic solution for the control and eradication of the
cattle fever tick. Other pesticides such as DDT where also
widely used. By State law, all cattle, horses, mules, goats,
and other susceptible animals were required to be dipped
every 14 days. Under certain circumstances, the arsenic and
other pesticides remaining at the site may present an
environmental or public health hazard. See listing by county
of known cattle dip vats.
The majority of the vats listed below are from old records of
the State Livestock Board, wh ich listed each vat as it was
put into operation. There were also privately operated vats
which may or may not have been listed by the Livestock
Board, so this list is not an exhau stive list of all cattle dip
vats. Note that vat locations were only iden tified by ranch
name and county, and that street address or geographic
location information is not available. In addition, some
county boundaries have changed since the vats were first
listed.
DEP has only been involved with work at a small number of
these vats. That subset is available at the “DEP Cattle Dip
Vat Cleanup Sites” link on the Cattle Dip Vat web page
(https://floridadep.gov/waste/district-business-support/
content/cattle-dipping-vats-cdv).
Anyone who wants to report a vat or can identify the exact
location of a listed vat is encouraged to contact the Florida
Department of Environmental Protection, Division of Waste
Management. Please see the contact information provided
on the Cattle Dip Vat web page.
