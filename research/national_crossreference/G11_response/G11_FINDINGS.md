## THE HEADLINE FINDING — a hole in the answer key
**Palm Beach County does not appear in the state inventory at all.** No section in the
107-page list, no row in the county table, zero entries among 3,240 parsed. Yet by the
state's own assessment report: Palm Beach was among the last "regulated" counties, the
state's FINAL fever-tick outbreak (1960) was on Palm Beach cattle, and dipping ran to
October 1961. Neighboring far-south counties DO appear (Broward 3, Dade 1, Martin 4,
Monroe 1).

**What this means, stated carefully:**
- The planned Acreage overlay CANNOT be run from the state inventory — not because
  there were no vats in Palm Beach (the state's own history says dipping was active
  there longest), but because the registry never captured that county. Late-era
  "regulated" counties were apparently recorded differently, or via records that never
  reached the Livestock Board list; the preamble also notes privately operated vats
  "may or may not have been listed."
- This NEITHER implicates NOR clears The Acreage. It documents that even America's only
  vat registry has a blind spot, and the blind spot sits over the county with the
  state's longest dipping history and a declared pediatric cancer cluster.
- National implication: if the best registry in the country misses its longest-dipping
  county, unregistered-state inventories (CA, TX, ...) are certainly incomplete in the
  same systematic way — late, private, and regulated-era vats are the least recorded.
