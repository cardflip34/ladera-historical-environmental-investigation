Fifteen states dipped cattle in arsenic. One wrote down where.

A hypothesis-neutral cross-reference of the 1906–1943 federal tick-eradication program's arsenic legacy against modern land development and documented cancer-cluster investigations — built with the same discovery methods developed for the Ladera Ranch case.

Read this first. This is an independent research and data-organization project. It does not provide medical advice and does not establish that any historic land use, property, organization, or party caused any illness. Proximity does not establish exposure; temporal overlap does not establish causation. No dip-site soil has been tested in California, and no contamination is asserted for any community named here. Every claim carries a source grade (A1 official primary → D speculation). The purpose of this page is one thing only: to rank candidates for soil testing.

Dipping was compulsory every fourteen days in quarantined counties. The Bureau's own FY1910 ledger shows California released 32,271 square miles that year — the largest release in the nation (Texas was second at 10,675) — before completing its statewide exit in March 1912: the fastest departure, and the fastest forgetting, of any major program state A1. The concrete vats were built to a federal specification and survive a century by default — Arkansas has several on the National Register; Florida keeps discovering hidden ones under later development; Australia measured soil arsenic up to 14,000 mg/kg at its registered sites (California background: 1–11 mg/kg). Thirteen states — including California and Texas, the two biggest ranch-to-suburb conversion stories in America — kept no site records at all.

The first three correlations, examined honestly

1 · Ladera Ranch — the template the case that built the method

Documented heavy-tier quarantine county; a ~21,000-head operation named in the 1911 federal circular; quantified arsenic mass estimates; a physical candidate structure located in the un-graded open-space corridor; reported diagnoses whose timing is consistent with (not probative of) in-utero exposure during the 1997–2007 grading. Soil testing is organizing now. Everything else on this page asks: where else does this template fit?

2 · Temecula / Vail Ranch — the exposure twin B2 · exposure side

An 87,000-acre cattle empire — one of California's largest, in a quarantined county — sold in the late 1970s and converted wholesale into ~60,000 homes. If the dipping-compliance logic held anywhere outside the O'Neill Ranch, it held here, and its vat sites are equally unrecorded. The honest finding: no cancer cluster has been reported there. That null matters both ways — it cautions against assuming conversions produce clusters, and it makes Temecula a clean comparison community for any future Ladera analysis. Next step: apply the imagery targeting method to the Vail corridors.

3 · The Acreage, Florida — the answer-key overlay A2 · outcome side

A state-declared pediatric cluster (girls' brain tumors at 5.5× the state rate), investigated for years and closed unresolved — on rapidly built-out former citrus and range land, in the one state that possesses a county-by-county vat registry. In everything reviewed so far, no agency or researcher appears to have overlaid Florida's own vat map onto its own cluster investigations. That overlay is executable from public records, and whichever way it comes out, it validates or disciplines this entire method. It is the single highest-value analysis in the national plan.

The Florida answer key — first results new · Aug 11

Florida's own records, pulled tonight, sharpen everything above:

When Florida tested its vat sites, 11 of 12 failed. The state's assessment program found 92% of evaluated sites exceeded acceptable soil concentrations; one produced an extensive groundwater plume. Remediation ran $40,000–$450,000 per site A2.

The failure mode is already in Florida's cleanup ledger. The state's tracked-vat list includes a vat at Stoneybrook Development (Orange Co.), a vat at Peach Creek Development (Walton Co.), and a vat at an elementary school (Clay Co.) — America's own version of Australia's built-over homes, recorded by the one state that kept records. The state report also documents disused vats reused as “swimming holes for children.”

The formula cross-validates. Florida's report specifies the same mix our federal circulars do — 8 lb arsenic trioxide per batch with soda and pine tar — independently confirming the inputs of the Ladera mass-balance work.

Palm Beach County dipped longest. Among the last counties still regulated; the state's final fever-tick outbreak (1960) was on Palm Beach cattle; the program ran to October 1961. The Acreage cluster sits in the county with Florida's longest dipping history. Open question, stated honestly: the cleanup ledger shows only one tracked vat in Palm Beach (Jupiter, ~20 km from The Acreage); whether any of the ~3,400 inventoried locations fall near The Acreage awaits the full FDOH/DEP inventory — a records request away. A null there will be published as prominently as a match.

Method — the Ladera discovery process, scaled

1) Build the exposure layer blind: county-level quarantine tables from primary USDA documents (the 1911 circulars, the 1912 proclamation, and the Bureau's annual reports — county-level releases like “In California the county of Santa Barbara is released from quarantine” are now being digitized). Inventory ranch-to-suburb conversions and score dip-site likelihood from historical imagery, water layers, and drainage — before looking at any outcome data. 2) Build the outcome layer from official investigations only, graded. 3) Join, rank, and publish nulls alongside hits. 4) The output is a soil-testing queue — never a causation claim.

What would change these findings

A soil result anywhere (either direction). The Florida overlay coming back empty (weakens the method) or matched (strengthens it). Registry-grade age and incidence data replacing community counts. Any state producing records that vats were located and remediated during development — evidence that has not surfaced for California in two years of looking.
