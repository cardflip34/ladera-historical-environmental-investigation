# Why We Know About the Schools and Not About the Houses

**Prepared:** 2026-07-18. A structural finding that explains a pattern in this platform's own
environmental-site data.

## The pattern

Every legacy-agricultural-pesticide soil record found near Ladera Ranch is a **school site**:

| Site | Contaminants found | ~Miles |
|---|---|---|
| Carl Hankey Elementary | Arsenic, lead, methane | 1.8 |
| Meggitt / former Endevco | Lead | 2.3 |
| Plant Depot School Site | Arsenic, nitrate | 2.9 |
| Ambuehl Elementary | DDD, DDE, DDT, toxaphene | 3.0 |
| San Juan Elementary | Arsenic, chlordane, DDD, DDE, DDT, lead | 3.2 |
| El Toro High School | PCBs, TPH-diesel | 6.8 |

Not one residential subdivision appears. It would be easy to read that as "schools were built on
bad land and neighbourhoods weren't." **That reading is wrong.**

## The explanation: a statutory asymmetry, not a contamination gradient

California **Education Code §§ 17210, 17213.1, 17213.2** require, before a school site is
acquired:

- a **Phase I Environmental Assessment** by a qualified environmental assessor;
- **mandatory DTSC review** of that Phase I;
- a **Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA)** where the Phase I indicates one is needed;
- evaluation against DTSC's **"residential" standard — its most protective**;
- specific notification/consultation duties where the site is **zoned agricultural**
  (§§ 17212, 17213(b)).

DTSC has published guidance specifically for **sampling agricultural fields for schools**, naming
the contaminants of concern on former orchards as **arsenic, lead, and organochlorine
pesticides** — precisely what turns up at the sites above.

**No equivalent statutory mandate attaches to a private residential subdivision.** Those proceed
through CEQA and local land-use approval. A Phase I ESA is commonly performed for lender or
liability reasons, but DTSC involvement is discretionary or referral-based, and the ag-residue
analytes (DDT/DDE, toxaphene, chlordane, arsenic) are **not automatically required** — as the
RMV-area Phase I ESAs located for this project demonstrate: they screened for recognised
environmental conditions but **did not test soil for organochlorines or arsenicals.**

## What follows from this

1. **The absence of soil data for the Ladera Ranch residential footprint is a regulatory
   artifact, not evidence that the soil is clean.** Nobody was required to look.
2. **The neighbouring school findings are a reasonable prior** for what comparable former
   citrus/grain/nursery land in the same drainage might contain — they are the only local
   places anyone was obliged to test.
3. This is not a criticism of any developer. It is how the law is written: California
   systematically protects children *at school* from legacy agricultural residue, and does not
   apply the same mandatory screen to the ground those same children live on.

## Regulatory benchmarks for interpreting any future sampling

| Jurisdiction | Analyte | Level | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Washington State Dept. of Ecology (former orchards) | Arsenic | **> 20 ppm** requires cleanup | Formal statewide former-orchard programme |
| Washington State Dept. of Ecology | Lead | **> 250 ppm** requires cleanup | |
| California DTSC | Lead | **80 mg/kg** residential screening level | Soils ≤ 80 mg/kg generally reusable unrestricted |

Washington's four accepted remedies for former orchard land are instructive because they show
what regulators consider protective: **excavation and removal; hard or soft capping** (asphalt,
or soil/grass/gravel) **with ongoing maintenance obligations and notice to future owners;
consolidate-and-cap** (contaminated soil moved on-site under permanent surfaces or engineered
caps); and **mixing** with clean soil to dilute below cleanup levels.

Three of those four **leave the contamination on the property**. Capping is accepted as
protective *conditional on the cap being maintained and disclosed*.

## The consequence for Ladera Ranch

If the footprint was never tested, then no remedy was ever selected, no cap was ever engineered,
no maintenance obligation was ever recorded, and no disclosure was ever made. Any protection
currently provided by turf, hardscape and structures is **incidental rather than engineered** —
it may well be substantial in practice, but it is undocumented, unmaintained as a remedy, and
unverified.

## Added to the evidence-gate request

Gate **G13/G14** (Orange County Development Services) is sharpened: ask explicitly whether a
**Phase I ESA or Preliminary Endangerment Assessment was performed for the Ladera Ranch
residential entitlement**, whether **arsenic, lead, or organochlorine pesticides** were among the
analytes, at what **depths** samples were taken, and how **stripped topsoil was handled** —
hauled off, buried under fill, or stockpiled and reused as landscape/planting medium. That last
question matters: standard grading practice strips 6–12 inches of topsoil, which is exactly the
layer where lead-arsenate residue concentrates, and reusing it as garden soil would relocate the
contaminated horizon into yards.
