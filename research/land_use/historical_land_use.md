# Historical Land Use — Ladera Ranch and Adjacent Land

**Prepared:** 2026-07-18. Alternative-hypothesis (non-pesticide) reconnaissance. See
`historical_land_use.csv` and `../environmental_sites/sites.csv`.

## Summary

Ladera Ranch was built **1999–2006** on ~4,000 acres carved from the O'Neill/Avery/Moiso
family's **Rancho Mission Viejo** — historically Orange County's largest working cattle
ranch — in the **Cañada Chiquita (Chiquita Canyon) drainage**, a tributary valley of San
Juan Creek.

## Key primary document

A bundled set of **Phase I Environmental Site Assessments (EEI, May 2003 / rev. Feb 2004)**
for Rancho Mission Viejo's Ranch Plan covers planning areas immediately adjacent to and
hydrologically continuous with Ladera Ranch, using historic aerials from 1952, 1959, 1973,
1977, 1981, 1983, 1999, and 2002 plus ranch-personnel interviews. It documents, for the
Chiquita Canyon area: grazing land throughout, **barley** dry-farming before 1983, use by a
**tree nursery (Sea Tree Nursery) 1983–1998**, and, in the adjacent Ortega Gateway area,
~150 acres of **lemon groves** with frost-protection wind machines, commercial plant
nurseries, and stored agricultural chemicals.

## The consequential finding

Whether the **exact Ladera Ranch residential footprint** grew orchards/row crops versus
pure grazing **cannot be confirmed definitively** from available public sources. The best
inference (moderate–high confidence) — triangulating the adjacent-parcel ESA with a DTSC
school record on former ag land ~1.2 miles from center (Carl Hankey Elementary: past use
"agricultural – orchard, agricultural – row crops") — is that the footprint was
**predominantly cattle grazing with pockets of dry-farmed barley, lemon/citrus orchard, and
tree-nursery use** in the canyon bottoms.

**The alternative hypothesis this raises:** former California citrus/orchard/grain land
commonly carries **legacy soil residues** — arsenic and lead (from lead-arsenate), and
organochlorines (**DDT/DDE/DDD, toxaphene, chlordane**). DTSC's own school-site
investigations on former ag land **in the immediate area repeatedly found exactly these**
(see `environmental_sites/sites.csv`: Carl Hankey, San Juan Elementary, Ambuehl, Plant
Depot). Yet the RMV-area Phase I ESAs located here **screened for recognized environmental
conditions but did not test soil for organochlorine or arsenical pesticide residues.**
Whether the Ladera Ranch residential entitlement/EIR included any agricultural-residue soil
testing is **unresolved** and is logged as an evidence gate.

A mechanistically coherent extension: children lived in Ladera Ranch during active
**mass grading (2000–2006)** of this former ag land, a plausible route to mobilize any
legacy arsenic/pesticide-laden soil as respirable/ingestible dust. This ties the
soil-residue hypothesis to a concrete exposure route and is worth a construction-timeline
vs residency-timeline analysis.

## UPDATE 2026-07-26 — full-text search of the 2016 PEIR + FEIR 589 Phase I ESA appendix
Both documents were held on file; full-text searched this pass (see
`ARSENIC_FULLTEXT_SEARCH_2026-07-26.md` for method and complete results). **Zero** mentions of
arsenic trioxide, cattle dipping, tick eradication, or lead-arsenate in either document. One unrelated,
real arsenic mention (groundwater, an industrial silica-mine tailings site in a different planning
area, non-speciated method) does not change the conclusion. This corroborates, and does not weaken,
the "never tested" finding for the Ladera footprint and its immediately adjacent Ranch Plan lands.
