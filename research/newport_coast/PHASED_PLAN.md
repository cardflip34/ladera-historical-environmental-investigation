# Newport Coast — Deep Screening Plan (the next phase)

> **Hypothesis-neutral. No contamination is assumed or claimed.** This applies the Ladera Ranch
> methodology to the Newport Coast area of Orange County, at depth, and adds two lenses Ladera did
> not need: (1) an explicit **arsenic mass-balance / exposure** layer, and (2) a **grading
> reconstruction** layer, because Newport Coast is among the most heavily earth-moved master-planned
> landscapes in the county. Working area only — **not part of the publication.**

## Why Newport Coast is a strong test case

- It sits on former **Irvine Ranch / Rancho San Joaquin** land in **Orange County** — a county the
  USDA named **"heavily infested"** in the 1907–1915 tick program, i.e. a *higher-dip-intensity*
  county on the model map.
- It was **mass-graded** comparatively recently (Irvine Company, ~late 1980s–2000s): terraced
  hillsides, large cut-and-fill. That makes the **grading-reconstruction** question sharp — if a
  dip-era feature existed, where did that soil go?
- Like Ladera, its **environmental review floor** almost certainly post-dates 1912 — the review-gap
  question applies directly.

Every one of those is an **open question**, not a finding. The point of the plan is to *ask each one
rigorously*, and to end — as always — at the single resolving act nobody has performed: a soil test.

## Phase 0 — Land & cattle history *(feeds: was this even dip country?)*

**Questions.** Which grant/ranch underlies Newport Coast (Rancho San Joaquin? Irvine Ranch?)?
Ownership chain to the Irvine Company. Was the specific land cattle range in 1907–1915? Any dipping
record tied to *this* land by name (State Veterinarian biennial reports; Santa Ana / LA newspapers;
Irvine Ranch operational records)?

**Method.** Land-grant maps (Bancroft diseños, GLO plats), Irvine Ranch histories, CDNC/Chronicling
America keyword runs, the S-CASTATEVET reports. Grade every source; counter-evidence mandatory.
**Output.** A graded yes/no/unknown on "dip-era cattle land," with the evidence and its gaps.

## Phase 1 — Earliest imagery, water & ranch features *(the sampling-frame context)*

**Questions.** Earliest aerial/topo coverage of the land and its date. Historic water sources,
stock ponds, wells, corrals, ranch structures (the terrain that co-locates with vat siting — water +
stock handling + access).

**Method.** USGS topoView 1890s–1930s quads, UCSB FrameFinder aerials, OC Survey historical imagery
(root `/arcpub/`, aerials to 1929 at 1.15 ft/px — per project memory), GNIS. Georeference; extract
water features as in the Ladera pipeline. **No exact residential coordinates** in any output.
**Output.** A georeferenced pre-development feature map and the earliest-imagery date.

## Phase 2 — Environmental-review audit *(the review-gap number)*

**Questions.** What CEQA review was done (Newport Coast LCP EIR; Pelican Hill; Crystal Cove;
Coastal Commission)? How far back did any historical/Phase I review actually look? Did any address
arsenic, lead-arsenate, or historic agricultural/ranch chemical use? Anything in **DTSC EnviroStor**
or **Water Board GeoTracker** for the vicinity?

**Method.** CEQAnet, EIR appendices, Coastal Commission staff reports, EnviroStor/GeoTracker pulls.
**Output.** The **review-gap** = (earliest date any review reached) − (~1912). Same headline number
the whole project turns on.

## Phase 3 — Grading reconstruction *(Newport Coast's distinctive lens)*

**Questions.** Where was the original ground surface vs. the graded surface? Cut zones vs. fill
zones, and cubic yards moved (from EIR grading plans). If a dip-era feature existed on the original
surface, was its soil **cut and hauled**, **buried under fill**, or **left in a slope face** — and
was it **diluted** (mixed into large fill volumes) or **relocated intact**? When was grading dust
greatest (the inhalation-pathway window)?

**Method.** Compare pre-grading topo (Phase 1) to post-grading LiDAR/DEM and EIR grading plans;
difference the surfaces. This is a GIS earthwork reconstruction. **Output.** A cut/fill map and a
qualitative "where did the surface soil go" model — explicitly a **model estimate**.

## Phase 4 — Candidate-siting model & testable sampling frame

**Questions.** Given water + stock-handling terrain + access + parcel history, where would a vat
most plausibly have stood? (A *probability surface*, not a claim.)

**Method.** Weight the Phase 1 feature layers; produce a ranked candidate surface. **Privacy gate:**
outputs are **aggregate/area-level only** — no exact residential coordinates, no address, nothing
that fingerprints a specific home. A candidate surface is a *sampling frame for authorized testing*,
not a public "X marks the spot." **Output.** A coarse candidate-zone map at low spatial precision.

## Phase 5 — Arsenic quantification & exposure pathways (Newport Coast-specific)

**Questions.** Apply the mass-balance model (`research/arsenic_model/`) to the Newport Coast case:
if a vat operated N years here, plausible near-vat soil arsenic and mass. Which exposure pathways
apply given current land use — **child soil ingestion**, **grading-era dust inhalation**,
**household dust** — described at **aggregate** level only (proximity of *categories* of land use:
parks, trails, schools — never specific children, never specific homes).

**Method.** The mass-balance + the ATSDR/EPA/DTSC yardsticks (background 11–12 mg/kg; EPA RSL
0.68; DTSC 0.11). **Output.** A clearly-labeled **model-estimate** exposure narrative with all
caveats; no health claim about any individual.

## Phase 6 — Records requests & the resolving act

**Questions.** What physical archives could locate an actual vat (Irvine Company records; **Sherman
Library** (Irvine Ranch collections); **First American** title archive; **UC Irvine Special
Collections**; **OC Agricultural Commissioner**; State Vet reports)? And the endpoint: a **direct
soil arsenic test** at the top candidate zone under proper authorization.

**Output.** A drafted records-request list (built only *after* the gaps are known, per project rule)
and the single-line recommendation the whole plan exists to earn: *test the soil.*

---

## Discipline gates (every phase)

1. Hypothesis-neutral; equal openness to "nothing here."
2. Grade every source A1–D; no silent promotion.
3. Mandatory counter-evidence on every finding.
4. **No exact residential coordinates/addresses; no individual data; health only at aggregate level.**
5. Distinguish absence-of-record from absence-of-fact.
6. Label every model output **MODEL ESTIMATE**; never display it as measured fact.
7. No sensationalism; the deliverable is a *prioritised question*, resolved only by a soil test.

## Recon findings (2026-07-21) — what Phases 0–4 now know

**Phase 0 — Land & cattle history.**
- Newport Coast sits on **Rancho San Joaquin** (Sepúlveda grant 1837/42) → **Irvine Ranch** (James
  Irvine, 1864) → Irvine Company (Donald Bren, sole owner by 1996). *[C corroborated / B2]*
- The land **was cattle-grazed for a century** — a California Coastal Commission staff report states
  the inland slopes "were extensively used for cattle grazing," with brush cleared (herbicides) to
  expand grassland; grazing continued until ~1979. *[A2, read directly]*
- **Orange County was under the cattle-tick quarantine in 1910** — by 1 Apr 1910 only San Diego,
  Orange, Santa Barbara, and part of SLO remained; arsenical dips were standard. *[B1, Pulling 1965]*
- **BUT: no located record ties a dip vat, dipping, or arsenical treatment to this land by name.**
  The one quarantined "large ranch" in the source is **unnamed and unlocated** (possibly San Diego).
  The Irvine Ranch cattle HQ was **Bommer Canyon** (inland) — corrals/roundups, **no vat** in the
  searchable record. Counter-considerations: SoCal ticks framed as a **Baja-border** phenomenon;
  California scabies dipping often used **lime-sulfur, not arsenic**; documented use is **extensive
  grazing, not intensive dipping**.

**Phase 1 — Earliest imagery.** Earliest topo **1896** (Santa Ana 15′, *predates* the ~1912 dipping
end) and 1901; earliest aerials **~1927** (Fairchild/UCSB), OC 1938 well represented. *[A1/B1]* —
**the imagery to examine the dipping-era landscape exists.** (Frame-level footprints over the slopes
not yet pulled from FrameFinder.)

**Phase 2 — Review audit.** CEQA/Coastal review was extensive (biology, hydrology, grading, views,
archaeology) but a full-text scan of the 273-pp Coastal staff report found **zero** substantive hits
for arsenic, lead-arsenate, cattle dip/vat, Phase I ESA, or aerial review. **EnviroStor and
GeoTracker (queried live via ArcGIS REST, A1) show no arsenic/ag-residue case in the Newport Coast
core** — the nearest agricultural-past-use record (Turtle Ridge Elementary) found **methane**, not
arsenic; the core's registered sites are a closed MSW landfill (Coyote Canyon), dry cleaners, and
aerospace. *(Caveat: CEQAnet holds abstracts, not full EIR appendices; a soils/Phase-I appendix could
exist in an unretrieved volume. "Searchable materials do not appear to address arsenical residue" —
not "never studied.")* **Verified dead-end:** a web result claiming GeoTracker site SL0608756247 was
"Newport Coast" is actually **Olin Corp., Morgan Hill** — not this area.

**Phase 3 — Grading.** Massive. The **final phase alone** (~980 ac) moved ~**22M cy cut / 25M cy
fill / 13M cy remedial** and **filled headland streams**; total across the 9,432-ac community is far
larger, plus the SR-73 cut through the same hills. *[A2, read directly]* This **rearranged any
shallow historic surface** — a genuine complicating factor, evidence of neither presence nor absence.

**Phase 4/5 — Features.** GNIS shows only modern (1960s) reservoirs and **zero** historical
well/stock-pond/corral features (it is a names gazetteer). The Coastal report notes **rancher-built
cattle *watering* ponds** — the opposite end of the operation from a dip vat, expected on any grazed
range, **not** a dipping indicator.

**Net:** Newport Coast has the **same preconditions as Ladera** (former ranch, quarantine county, a
review that never looked back for this question) — but with a twist: **the look-back imagery exists
(1896 topo, 1927 aerials), so the gap is one of *analysis*, not data.** No vat is tied to the land;
strong counter-evidence applies. **No contamination is asserted.** The live next steps are physical
archives + direct imagery analysis, not more database queries.
