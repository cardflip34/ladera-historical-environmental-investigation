## The unifying insight (why one plan for two areas)
**Ladera Ranch and Coto de Caza / Trabuco Canyon were the same ownership** — both sat on the O'Neill /
Flood **Rancho Mission Viejo** holdings (with Joplin a *homesteader* inholding at Bell Canyon). So the
**single highest-value target serves both investigations at once: the Rancho Mission Viejo / O'Neill ranch
archive**, plus the one county office that regulated dipping across all of it — the **Orange County
Veterinarian / Agricultural Commissioner** (Dr. W. S. McFarlane, 1908). Find the ranch's own dipping
paperwork and you advance Coto *and* Ladera in one pull.

## Where the two areas stand (so we don't re-derive)
- **Coto/Joplin — STRONGEST case.** Documented 1908 arsenical dip at Josiah C. Joplin's Bell Canyon
  ranch (LA Herald 27 May 1908); full 1908 chain + Joplin biography 1844–1933 verified in CDNC; site =
  today's **Starr Ranch Audubon Sanctuary**, undisturbed open space. *Open:* exact vat parcel; the
  un-digitized county/state records; the soil test.
- **Ladera — OPEN question.** Open rangeland in every aerial 1929–2022; **no vat found, no soil tested**;
  node A (one 1937 structure) + 16 water bodies mapped; best-guess siting done; conditional mass estimate
  ~0 or ~100–500 lb. *Open:* whether a vat was ever in the footprint at all; the ranch records; a test.

## PHASE 3 — PHYSICAL archives  *(in person — this is the digital hang-up)*
The granular vat records were **never digitized**. Prioritized visit targets (user lives near Sherman):
1. **Sherman Library & Gardens (Corona del Mar)** — *top, and closest.* **O'Neill / Rancho Mission Viejo**
   and Irvine Ranch collections; OC agricultural & cattle history; tick-eradication era. **Serves both
   areas.** → pull-list before visiting.
2. **Orange County Archives** — **Board of Supervisors minutes + County Veterinarian (McFarlane) orders,
   1907–1917**; any vat ledger/location list. → the county-order paper trail.
3. **California State Archives (Sacramento)** — **State Veterinarian** biennial/administrative files; GLO
   patent legal descriptions. → remote reference request feasible first.
4. **Saddleback Area Historical Society** — Trabuco/**Joplin/Straw** family papers; local dip memory,
   photos, corral/vat recollections.
5. **National Audubon — Starr Ranch Sanctuary** — landowner contact; Bell Canyon site history; the
   permission pathway for an eventual soil test on the *documented* dip ground.
**Exit:** either a named vat location surfaces, or absence-of-record is established at the source level.

## PHASE 4 — Field verification & the resolver  *(the endpoint)*
1. **Records-request letters** drafted from exact Phase-1/3 gaps (OC Archives, State Archives, Audubon).
2. **Soil-sampling / geophysics scope** for the two testable grounds, in priority order:
   - **(a) Starr Ranch / Joplin, Bell Canyon** — *documented* dip, *undisturbed* — the single cleanest
     place a test could ever be sited. Requires Audubon permission.
   - **(b) Ladera node A greenbelt** — accessible open space; tests the strongest in-footprint candidate.
3. **Discriminator protocol:** total **As + Pb + arsenic speciation** at every sample, to separate
   **cattle-dip arsenic-trioxide** (As, little Pb) from **orchard lead-arsenate** (As *with* Pb) — the
   single most important analytical control, and the thing that would tell cattle-dip from crop residue.
**Exit:** the open question is finally answerable with a measurement, on ground where it is appropriate.

## What would confirm vs. falsify (rigor gate)
- **Confirm a specific dip site:** a named vat location in ranch/county/state records, OR imagery of a
  vat/drain-pen, OR soil As ≫ background (≥~10× ~11 mg/kg) with the **low-Pb** cattle-dip signature.
- **Falsify / down-weight:** no vat in the RMV/county/state records for the footprint; soil As at
  background; or an As-**with**-Pb signature pointing to orchard lead-arsenate instead.
- **Neither provable digitally alone** for Ladera — hence Phase 3–4 are decisive there; Coto is already
  documented and only needs the parcel + the test.
