# Horno Basin runoff routing — findings and open items (2026-09-11)

## Question (from John Gresko / Andy)
All Ladera drainage reaches the Horno Basin filtration facility. Where is that water
pumped: to the smaller of the two ridge tanks and directly into Ladera irrigation, or to
the Chiquita Water Reclamation Plant? Civil engineer of record: Huitt-Zollars. Plans not
located by Gresko.

## ANSWERED at A2 — the water comes back as irrigation
1. SMWD (official page, Recycled Water): the district "collects urban return (runoff)
   water from Oso Creek, Dove Creek, and HORNO CREEK to blend with the recycled water as
   an additional source for irrigation." [A2]
2. SMWD On Tap newsletter (May 2012, held: smwd_ontap_2012.pdf): "Upper Oso Barrier,
   HORNO BASIN and Bell Canyon Project... designed to capture, treat and deliver millions
   of gallons of runoff water FOR LOCAL IRRIGATION USE." [A2]
Conclusion: Gresko's belief is CONFIRMED in direction — captured Horno runoff re-enters
the non-potable/recycled irrigation system that waters Ladera's slopes, parks, medians.

## OPEN at document level — the physical route
Two candidate routes (not yet distinguished by held documents):
  A. Basin filtration -> pump station -> recycled storage tank on the ridge ("the smaller
     one" of the tank pair) -> gravity into Ladera irrigation zones. ("Capture, treat and
     deliver" phrasing and the observed on-site filtration support local treatment;
     typical dry-weather-diversion design.) [Inference]
  B. Basin -> force main to Chiquita WRP -> blended into plant recycled output -> back to
     the same distribution. [Possible; "blend with the recycled water" phrasing]
Facilities located on 2025 imagery: Chiquita WRP (~33.5435, -117.6079, clarifiers +
solar + ponds, orchards irrigated around it); tank pair + one larger tank on ridge
(~33.5563, -117.6042). Andy to drop a pin on the basin/filtration site he visited.

## Why it matters (neutral framing)
If soil-borne constituents exist anywhere in the drainage (unmeasured — no claim), a
capture-and-respray loop redistributes whatever runoff carries onto community
landscaping. Routing determines whether such water receives plant-level treatment
(route B) or filtration-only treatment (route A) before spray. This is an exposure-
pathway question for the sampling plan (irrigation-water sample = cheap test).
