# Precautions & measures for former arsenical cattle-dip sites — SE US vs Australia vs California

> Hypothesis-neutral. This compares **regulatory/administrative measures**, not contamination.
> California findings are stated as *"no such program was located in searchable public records,"*
> never *"California did nothing."* Working file — not part of the publication.

## The three sharpest findings

1. **Australia built the full apparatus California lacks.** NSW keeps a **public ~1,648-site register**, flags dip land in **planning/s.10.7**, issued **1996 residential cleanup guidelines**, and created a **waste-disposal pathway** for dip soil — because the network was **government-owned**, which is *why* the records survive.
2. **Even the messy US Southeast did more than California.** Florida keeps a (weak) public list and a statutory regime (a **liability exemption**, §376.306, plus voluntary cleanup to a **2.1 mg/kg** residential target); the **USDA Forest Service funds CERCLA cleanups** of vats on its land.
3. **California's closest analog program explicitly excludes dip vats.** DTSC's *Interim Guidance for Sampling Agricultural Properties* (2008) targets land where chemicals were applied **uniformly** (orchards, row crops) and **expressly excludes** "pesticide mixing/loading areas … **animal facilities** … and other areas that may have been treated differently." **A cattle-dip vat is exactly that excluded point source.**

## The honest framing (no cover-up)

California **did** run a state-administered arsenical-dip quarantine (State Veterinarian + County
Boards of Supervisors; compulsory under the 1907 law), and **Orange County was among the last
counties cleared** (~1911, persistent reinfestation from Baja California). California **does** operate
general arsenic-screening and agricultural-soil programs. What is absent is any **dip-vat-specific**
inventory, notification, cleanup guideline, or remediation program.

Why the asymmetry is plausible without any cover-up: (i) California's dipping ended **~1911, roughly
50 years before Florida's (1961)** and the Forest Service era — vats were demolished/buried long
before modern environmental law; (ii) California was a smaller, earlier theater than the Gulf South;
(iii) California's **high natural background arsenic (~12 mg/kg)** and its background-not-below policy
lower the regulatory salience of diffuse arsenic; (iv) California's agricultural-soil attention went
to **orchard lead-arsenate and DDT**, which its programs *do* address. None of these prove dip
residues are absent — only that a dedicated program was never built.

## Caveats (whole comparison)

- **Thresholds are not comparable:** FL residential 2.1, NEPM residential 100, FS recreational
  456/4,560, CA background-governed ~12 mg/kg rest on different exposure scenarios, bioavailability,
  and background policies.
- **"Inventory" ≠ "mapped locations":** FL's ~3,241 and NSW's ~1,648 include name/landmark records;
  only a subset are precisely located.
- **Verification gaps (flagged by the researcher):** NSW 1996 guideline PDF not opened (its exact
  arsenic number is unverified — do **not** attribute "100 mg/kg" to it); s.10.7 exact wording not
  retrieved (B2); FL SCTL and DTSC CHHSL numbers are search-extractions of official rules, not direct
  table parses; USDA-FS fact sheet 404'd (facts rest on ITRC).
- **California "not located" ≠ "does not exist":** reflects web-searchable records + Cloudflare-blocked
  agency pages. A **direct records request** to DTSC, the State Water Board, CDFA, and the Orange/San
  Diego County Agricultural Commissioners (and the CA State Veterinarian biennial reports) is the next
  evidence gate → log in `FUTURE_EVIDENCE_GATES.md`.
