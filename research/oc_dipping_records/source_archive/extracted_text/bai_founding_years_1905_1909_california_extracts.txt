- 265 Eradicating cattle ticks in California.

- The history of the Texas-fever cattle tick in California covers scores of years.

- The common wood tick and castor-bean tick are very numerous in parts of California and are found on the cattle and horses in great numbers at certain seasons of the year.

- As Mexico, however, has probably been infested since the early conquests of the Spanish, it is most plausible to consider that California received her first Texas-* fever ticks from cattle driven across the line from Lower California.

- Salmon, the State of California was not included in the quarantined area.

- Further investigation, however, having shown that the northern part of the State was free of ticks, the line was gradually moved south, so that in 1906 it crossed California at the northern limits of San Luis Obispo, Fresno, Madera, and San Bernardino counties, thus including the 13 southern counties of the State.

- Such a condition of affairs existed when, in July, 1906, under the congressional appropriation for tick eradi- cation, the Bureau of Animal Industry started the work of eradicat- ing the ticks in California in cooperation with the State officials.

- With the breaking up of many of the large ranges in California the raising of cattle to the fattening stage early became cheaper in the more remote States, as Arizona, Utah, Nevada, New Mexico, and western Texas, which were free of ticks.

- the State of California, in March, 1907, enacted a law which makes it a misdemeanor to move or expose tick-infested cattle in such a man- ner as to infest other cattle or live stock not so infested.

- This law, while having weak points that should be corrected to cover certain special conditions, has been of inestimable value in the work of tick eradication in California.

- The dipping season in California begins about April 1 and must con- tinue at thirty-day intervals until eight or nine dippings have been made in order to clean the range in one season.

- Tick eradication along the California-Mexico line is complicated by the fact that parts of Lower California, especially the coast sec- tion, is heavily infested with ticks.

- ° As a result of four years' work by the Bureau and state officials, California has only 9,315 square miles, instead of 69,755 square miles, of territory in quarantine for Texas-fever ticks.
