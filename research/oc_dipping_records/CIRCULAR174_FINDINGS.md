# USDA BAI Circular 174 (1911), "Eradicating Cattle Ticks in California" — extracted findings

**Source grade A1** — official federal agency publication, primary. Held locally at
`evidence/images/usda_bai_circular174_1911_eradicating_cattle_ticks_in_CALIFORNIA.pdf`.
Data in the circular is "brought down to summer of 1910."

## 1. Orange County was in the HEAVILY infested tier

> "…showed San Luis Obispo, Santa Barbara, **San Diego, Orange**, Fresno, and Ventura counties to
> be **heavily infested**, and Tulare, Kern, Kings, Los Angeles, Riverside, San Bernardino, and
> Madera counties to be infested to a lesser degree."

This is direct federal confirmation, not inference. Orange County was one of six counties in the
top infestation tier, which is the tier subject to the most intensive compulsory dipping.

## 2. Scale of the program

> "These counties comprised 69,755 square miles of territory, and contained **660,027 head of
> cattle**, of which **153,476 were on tick-infested ranges**."

## 3. The single most important sentence for this investigation

> "Of the original 153,476 cattle infested there remain under local quarantine **41,906**… **Half of
> this number are on one large ranch where eradication work has been slow on account of its size,
> the roughness of the range, and lack of co-operation from the owner.**"

**≈20,953 head on ONE ranch, still under quarantine in summer 1910.**

### Why this matters

A single ranch holding roughly 21,000 quarantined cattle in southern California in 1910 —
described as large, rough-ranged, and slow to comply — would have required the **most sustained
dipping operation in the state**, over the **longest period**, because it was the last to clear.

### Candidate identification — NOT established

The circular does **not name the ranch.** The profile (very large, rough range, southern
California, ~21,000 head) is consistent with the O'Neill / Flood holding — Rancho Santa Margarita
y Las Flores plus Rancho Mission Viejo and Rancho Trabuco, 200,000+ acres, documented peak "over
25,000 head." It is **also** consistent with other large holdings of the era, notably the Irvine
Ranch (~110,000 acres, Orange County) and the Tejon Ranch (Kern).

**Required to identify it:** BAI correspondence files, California State Veterinarian /
Commissioner of Agriculture records for 1910–1912, or county quarantine records naming the ranch.
Until then this is a **lead**, graded *interpreted*, and must not be reported as a fact.

## 4. Dip formula and concentration escalation

> "…more arsenic may be added, **up to 9 or 10 pounds to the 500 gallons**, in the disinfection of
> range cattle."

The 8 lb / 500 gal figure is the baseline. **Range cattle — which is what this was — could be
dipped at 9–10 lb / 500 gal, 12–25% stronger.** The mass-balance calculation uses 8 and is
therefore conservative.

> "The same solution remains active as a tickicide **indefinitely**…"

Vats were topped up and reused rather than emptied, so a vat's cumulative throughput far exceeds a
single charge — which is what the drag-out model already assumes.

## 5. Rain and dilution — a documented transport-relevant fact

> "…unless **rains should dilute its contents**, when ingredients have to be added in the proper
> proportions for the amount of **rain water which has fallen or drained into the tank**."

The circular states plainly that open vats took on rainwater. An open vat that fills in a storm
also **overflows** in one. This is recorded here as a documented operational condition, **not** as
evidence that any release occurred at any location.
