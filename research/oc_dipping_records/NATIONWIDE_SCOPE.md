# The national and international scope of arsenical cattle-dipping legacy sites

*Compiled 2026-08-10. Statement classes and source grades marked throughout.*

## The program was national in scope — verified fact (A1/A2/B1)

The USDA Bureau of Animal Industry's Cattle Fever Tick Eradication Program (1906–1943)
extended across **fifteen states** — the quarantined South plus part of California — and
required dipping of cattle, horses, and mules in arsenical solution **every fourteen days**
during the growing season in quarantined areas. The U.S. was declared tick-free in 1943
(save a permanent Texas border quarantine zone that continues today).
Sources: USDA National Agricultural Library program history (A2); Encyclopedia of Arkansas;
Mississippi Encyclopedia; Arkansas Historic Preservation Program statewide context (B1).

## Vat counts — the scale of the physical legacy

- **Florida:** ~3,200–3,400 vats constructed 1906–1962 (A2 — Florida DEP; Florida DOH
  maintains a county-by-county location list). Florida is the ONLY state with a formal
  assessment program, created by 1994 legislation (HB 1959/SB 2550).
- **Other program states** (TX, GA, AL, MS, LA, AR, OK, NC, SC, TN, VA, ...): vats were
  built at comparable or greater density relative to cattle populations; **no national
  inventory exists** and most states have "no state requirements for mitigation" (B1 —
  ITRC; university studies). Documented site studies exist in Arkansas (Ozark National
  Forest ITRC case study), Georgia (Georgia Tech southern-vats investigation), and Florida
  (UF 2004: "Arsenic and Old Vats" — hidden sites under later development).
- **California:** included in the program (our A1 circulars document Orange County's heavy
  tier and the March 1912 quarantine release) but **has no state inventory, assessment
  program, or public vat registry**. This is the gap LEHRP addresses locally.

## The international control case — Australia (B1/A2)

New South Wales ran the same arsenical program (sodium arsenite, ~1900–1955) and, unlike
most U.S. states, **registered its sites**: 1,648 dip sites on the NSW far North Coast.
Documented findings:
- Soil arsenic at disused sites **up to 14,000 mg/kg** (vs ~1–11 mg/kg CA background).
- 254 vats demolished over the years; **29 had residences built directly over them**;
  houses and schools sit on or near former dip sites on flood plains.
- NSW DPI maintains public guidance on arsenic and DDT residues at dip yards (A2).

## What this means for the Ladera investigation — inference, labeled

1. **The contamination category is recognized, not speculative.** "Cattle dip vat site"
   is an established class of arsenic-contaminated site in U.S. and Australian environmental
   practice, with government assessment programs (FL), federal case studies (ITRC), and a
   peer-reviewed literature on soil concentrations, speciation, and bioavailability.
2. **Precedent exists for exactly our scenario.** Australia documents residences built
   over former vats; Florida documents hidden vats discovered under later development.
   A master-planned community on former ranch land with an undipped... *unexamined* dipping
   history is not an exotic theory — it is the known failure mode of this legacy.
3. **The gap is inventory, not science.** Florida legislated an inventory; NSW registered
   sites; California did neither. Whether a vat site lies within any California development
   is unknown *because no one has looked* — which is precisely the hypothesis-neutral
   framing this project uses.
4. **NOT established:** any nationwide cancer link to dip sites. Arsenic is an IARC
   Group 1 carcinogen (established science), but connecting any specific site to any
   specific illness requires measured exposure — the step no California agency has taken.

## Statement for briefings (approved language)

> "Compulsory arsenical cattle dipping operated across fifteen states from 1906 to 1943.
> Florida alone built ~3,400 vats and runs a state assessment program; Australia registered
> 1,648 sites and documents homes built over former vats with soil arsenic up to 14,000
> mg/kg. California participated in the same federal program but has never inventoried its
> sites. The available evidence does not establish causation anywhere — it establishes that
> no one in California has looked."
