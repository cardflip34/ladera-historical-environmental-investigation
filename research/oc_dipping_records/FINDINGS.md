# Orange County cattle-dipping — named locations in the primary record (c.1907–1912)

> Hypothesis-neutral. Absence of record ≠ absence of fact. Geography at county/district level only.
> OCR spellings corrected are flagged [inferred]. Working file — not part of the publication.

## The finding in one line
The primary record **does name specific Orange County dipping sites in 1908 — but every one is in the
eastern/southern county (the Santa Ana Mountains front and south mesas), NOT on the Irvine Ranch /
Rancho San Joaquin.** And no located source — primary, federal, state, or the definitive Irvine
history — ties a dip vat to Irvine, San Joaquin, or Bommer Canyon.

## Named OC dipping locations (1908)
Primary: *Los Angeles Herald*, 27 May 1908, p.10, "Hope to Save Cattle from the Texas Tick"
(dateline Santa Ana), quoting **Dr. Coleman, deputy state quarantine officer**. CDNC
`LAH19080527`. **Grade B2** (paper quoting a named official).

| Named site (as printed) | District (county-level) |
|---|---|
| Ranch of **J. C. Joplin** [inferred], **Trabuco Canyon** [inferred] | SE Orange County — Santa Ana Mtns foothills |
| **Capistrano** (San Juan Capistrano) | S Orange County |
| **Yorba** | NE Orange County — Santa Ana Canyon |
| **Bixby ranch, Santa Ana Canyon** [inferred] | NE Orange County |

Corroborating (both B2, both showing dipping was **county-ordered** and **arsenical**):
- *San Jose Mercury-news*, 26 Jun 1908: six head of **J. E. Levengood** died after dipping; "the dip
  was the **arsenic preparation recommended by the Government**." CDNC `SJMN19080626`.
- *Los Angeles Herald*, 25 Jul 1908: **E. J. Levengood** sued **the County of Orange** ($400); "the
  dipping was done **by order of the county veterinarian, Dr. W. S. McFarlane**." Places the Levengood
  dairy herd near **Santa Ana**. CDNC `LAH19080725`.

**Geographic pattern that matters:** the named sites ring the Santa Ana Mountains front and
south-county mesas — cattle driven to the mountain-front/canyon ranches. **Of the three study
communities, the named 1908 dipping is closest to LADERA (Trabuco Canyon), not to Newport Coast or
Irvine** (the San Joaquin Hills / coastal-central Irvine Ranch is not among the named sites).

## Nothing ties dipping to Irvine / San Joaquin / Bommer — explicit
- **Newspapers (CDNC, exhaustive):** every "Irvine" hit in-window is an unrelated person/business; the
  four named dip sites are explicitly *other* ranches.
- **USDA BAI annual reports (full-OCR grep, 1905–1911):** "Irvine," "San Joaquin," "Santa Ana,"
  "Rancho" = **zero** mentions; Orange appears only at county level.
- **Cleland, *The Irvine Ranch of Orange County, 1810–1950* (1952)** — the definitive ranch history —
  contains **no** "dipping / vat / quarantine / scab / tick fever" (single incidental "tick"). A
  meaningful **absence in the one source most likely to mention it.** [B1] *(This resolves the earlier
  "page-check Cleland" lead — it is silent on dipping.)*
- **Caveat:** absence of record, not proof of absence. The Irvine Ranch ran large herds inside the
  quarantined county and was subject to the same compulsory-dipping law; any operational dip records
  would sit in the un-digitized Irvine Company papers.

## Regulatory bookends
OC inside the federal Texas-fever quarantine by 1907; March 1907 California law made arsenical dipping
compulsory under the State Veterinarian. OC **released 21 March 1912** (USDA BAI Order 111, reprinted
*Visalia Times Delta* 15 Mar 1912). At release "only one ranch in that section is afflicted" (*Hanford
Journal*, 18 Oct 1911) — **unnamed**; contextually the late holdout leans toward a **San Diego /
border** ranch (reinfestation from Lower California), and **cannot be identified as the Irvine Ranch**
on any located evidence.

## Top un-digitized records to request (revised priority)
1. **⭐ Orange County Board of Supervisors minutes (1907–1912) + county live-stock inspector / county
   veterinarian monthly reports.** The decisive analog: neighboring **San Luis Obispo's inspector
   reported "63 dipping vats in county"** to its Supervisors (SLO Daily Telegram, 8 Aug 1908). OC kept
   the same office (Dr. W. S. McFarlane, 1908) and almost certainly filed comparable vat counts. →
   **OC Archives** (now the single highest-value target — Cleland being silent shifts weight here).
2. **Santa Ana Register / Santa Ana Blade, 1907–1912** — the county's own press, largely absent from
   CDNC; most likely to carry granular vat detail. → OC Public Library / UC microfilm.
3. **California State Veterinarian biennial reports, 1908–1912** (still being searched) — most likely
   *state* source to tabulate vat counts by county.
4. **Irvine Company records, Sherman Library** — the direct confirm/refute for Irvine-Ranch dipping.
5. **USDA BAI 26th Annual Report (1909) cooperative field files** — may name the unnamed "one large
   ranch."

## Bottom line
Dipping in Orange County was **real, arsenical, and county-ordered**, documented at **four named
eastern/southern ranches** in 1908 — none of them Irvine. No located source puts a vat on Irvine /
San Joaquin / Bommer land, and the definitive Irvine history is silent on dipping. The named dipping
clusters nearest **Ladera** (Trabuco Canyon); Irvine and Newport Coast remain preconditions-only, with
Irvine's actual soil arsenic tracking orchards. The resolver everywhere is unchanged: OC Supervisors /
county-vet vat records, then a soil test.
