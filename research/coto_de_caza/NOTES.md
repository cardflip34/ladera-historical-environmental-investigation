# Coto de Caza / Trabuco Canyon — the documented 1908 dip district (imagery)

> Hypothesis-neutral. Working file — not part of the publication.

**The record:** the *LA Herald*, 27 May 1908 named **"the ranch of J. C. Joplin in Trabuco canyon"**
as a site where cattle were being dipped under the county-ordered (arsenical) program [B2]. This is the
**#1 DOCUMENTED** entry on the dipping-probability map — but the source is **district-level (a ranch/
canyon), not a survey-precise vat coordinate.**

**What the 1938 aerial shows** (`imagery/coto_1938_wide(_eq).jpg`, valley zooms): the Coto de Caza /
**Cañada Chiquita–Trabuco Creek valley** was **open cattle rangeland** — grazed valley floor, a
tree-lined creek, ranch roads, and fine **cattle-trail terracettes** on the slopes. That grazing
landscape *is* the historic ranch activity. **No dip vat and no ranch-HQ/corral complex is identifiable**
at this resolution in the frames pulled (C-004 discipline). (The 1931 "Irvine Ranch" raster is *nodata*
here — Coto is O'Neill/Rancho Trabuco land, off the Irvine flight line; use "Orange County 1938" or
"South County Watersheds 1929".)

**What's there now** (`coto_dip_district_then_and_now.jpg`): the **Coto de Caza** guard-gated community
(~4,000 homes; between Rancho Santa Margarita and Trabuco Canyon; unincorporated Orange County). The old
creek/grazing **valley floor is now the Coto de Caza Golf & Racquet Club** (two 18-hole courses); the
slopes hold the gated villages, incl. **Los Ranchos Estates** (a gated enclave within, ~75 estates since
1979) and the **Equestrian Center**.

**Discipline:** documentary land-use history at neighborhood scale; no addresses/individuals; the exact
1908 vat location is unknown; **no soil has been tested; no contamination is implied.** Imagery: OC
Survey 1938 aerial; Esri World Imagery (Maxar et al.).
