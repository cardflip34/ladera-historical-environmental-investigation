# Coto de Caza / Trabuco Canyon — deep screening plan (Ladera-depth)

> Hypothesis-neutral. The #1 **DOCUMENTED** 1908 Orange County dip site: the *LA Herald* (27 May 1908)
> named **"the ranch of J. C. Joplin in Trabuco canyon"** as a county-ordered *arsenical* dip site.
> This replicates the Ladera Ranch methodology here. No contamination is assumed or claimed. Working
> file — not part of the publication.

## Phase 0 — Imagery baseline *(done; findings below)*
- **1929 "South County Watersheds"** flight (the oldest): pulled — but it **only clips the edges** of the
  Coto valley (that flight followed watershed corridors); the central dip valley is **nodata in 1929**.
  So **1938 "Orange County"** is the oldest full view of the valley.
- **1938, Coto de Caza / Cañada Chiquita valley:** open cattle rangeland — grazed floor, tree-lined
  creek, ranch roads, cattle-trail terracettes. **No vat/HQ resolvable** (district-level source).
- **1938, Trabuco Canyon *mouth* (the named canyon):** a **settled ranch/agricultural node** — a
  structure cluster at a road junction, a building by Trabuco Creek, converging roads, and a distinct
  **ORCHARD** on the valley floor. *This is the strongest candidate area for the Joplin operation* — and
  the orchard introduces the **lead-arsenate** (orchard) arsenic vector alongside any cattle dip.
  (`imagery/trabuco_mouth_1938_zoom.jpg`.)

## Phase 1 — Deep archival image & metadata hunt *(agent running)*
Metadata levers = the Rancho Trabuco owner families: **Argüello** (1841 grant), **Forster**, the
**O'Neill / Flood** Rancho Mission Viejo holdings, and **"J. C. Joplin."** Calisphere, OAC, USC, UCLA,
Huntington, Bancroft, LoC, David Rumsey, BLM GLO, San Juan Capistrano Historical Society, OC Archives,
Sherman Library, First American, Rancho Mission Viejo materials. Target: cattle-operation photos,
corrals, the canyon settlement, the Rancho Trabuco diseño/plat, ranch/topo maps. → `evidence/images/COTO/`.

## Phase 2 — Deep documentary / primary-source investigation *(agent running)*
The J. C. Joplin lead (who/where — GLO patent, deeds, census, Great Register, was he a Forster/O'Neill
lessee); State Veterinarian biennial reports (Trabuco district vat counts); USDA BAI; OC Supervisors /
county-vet (McFarlane) records; deep CDNC/Chronicling America for Joplin/Trabuco follow-ups; the O'Neill
Ranch cattle operation; the **orchard** history at the canyon mouth (lead-arsenate vs cattle-dip
arsenic); Coto de Caza CEQA/EIR + live EnviroStor/GeoTracker; earliest maps (1896/1901 Santa Ana quad,
Rancho Trabuco diseño) + GNIS water features.

## Phase 3 — Imagery deep-dive / georeferencing *(next)*
Georeference the 1938 canyon-mouth node; higher-detail passes on the structure cluster + orchard;
before/after over today's Trabuco Canyon / Robinson Ranch / Coto; check the 1929 edge-strips for any
covered ranch structures; pull mid-century (1946/1952) for the settlement's evolution.

## Phase 4 — Synthesis, candidate-siting & records requests
Combine archival + documentary + imagery into: a graded verdict on the Joplin/Trabuco dip site (what the
record does/doesn't establish); a **district-level** candidate-area map (never a parcel/coordinate); the
distinction between cattle-dip and orchard-lead-arsenate arsenic; and a records-request list
(OC Archives Supervisors/county-vet; San Juan Capistrano Historical Society; Rancho Mission Viejo/O'Neill
archives; State Archives). Endpoint, as always: a **soil test** nobody has run.

## Discipline gates
Hypothesis-neutral; grade A1–D; mandatory counter-evidence; **no exact residential coordinates/addresses,
no individual data**; distinguish absence-of-record from absence-of-fact; label all imagery reads within
resolution limits (no over-claimed "vat"); **no contamination claimed**; district/canyon geography only.
