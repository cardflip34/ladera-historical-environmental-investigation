# CDNC deep search — the Joplin / Bell Canyon 1908 dip site (primary press, read this session)

> Hypothesis-neutral. All items are contemporaneous newspaper reports (**Grade B2** — reputable papers
> quoting named officials), read directly on cdnc.ucr.edu this session via the browser. OCR corrections
> are flagged [inferred]. **No contamination claim:** the 1908 cattle deaths below are a documented
> *acute animal-poisoning event from the dip fluid in 1908* — they say nothing about soil residue today.

## 🔗 The dipping chain — fully documented, all 1908
1. **LA Herald, 27 May 1908** (`LAH19080527.2.83.22.7.3`, read verbatim): Dr. Coleman, deputy state
   quarantine officer, at Santa Ana; within ten days **several hundred head "treated by dipping at the
   ranch of J. C. Joplin in Trabuco canyon,"** 600–700 at **Capistrano**, and dipping "in progress at
   **Yorba** and at the **Bixby ranch in Santa Ana canyon**."
2. **San Jose Mercury, 26 Jun 1908, "KILLED TICKS AND CATTLE"** (`SJMN19080626.2.106`): **six head of
   cattle belonging to [E.] J. Levengood died** following their **dipping for Texas fever tick**; the
   dip was **"the arsenic preparation recommended by the Government."**
3. **LA Herald, 25 Jul 1908, "DAIRYMAN SUING FOR INJURIES TO HIS CATTLE"** (`LAH19080725.2.83.4.1`):
   **E. J. Levengood filed a $400 claim against the County of Orange**; the dipping "was done by order
   of the **county veterinarian, Dr. W. S. McFarlane**."
4. **San Pedro Daily News, 11 Aug 1908, "The Herd's Lord Goes Abroad"** (`SPDN19080811.2.24`): the herd
   "was driven from **J. C. Joplin's ranch in Bell Canyon** with a herd of cattle **bought from Joplin by
   Levengood**."

**What this establishes (new, this session):** the arsenical dip at these OC sites was **real and
potent enough to kill livestock in 1908**, it was **county-ordered** (McFarlane), and Levengood's dead
herd came **directly from Joplin's Bell Canyon ranch** — tying the fatal-dip event to the exact site.
The site's modern identity (Bell Canyon → **Starr Ranch Audubon Sanctuary**) is unchanged.

## 📍 "Bell Canyon" independently confirmed (fixes the spelling)
Two contemporaneous sources say **"Bell Canyon"** (matching the modern name / Starr Ranch), where Armor
1921 wrote "Belle Canyon":
- **San Pedro Daily News, 11 Aug 1908:** "J. C. Joplin's ranch in **Bell Canyon**."
- **La Habra Star, 17 Mar 1920** (`Active Leasing in Trabuco`): "**the old Joplin place in Bell canyon**"
  — noting it as the **W. K. Robinson ranch**, then under oil-lease option. *(Consistent with the later
  ownership chain Joplin → Robinson/Starr → Audubon at the Bell Canyon / Starr Ranch site.)*

## 👤 Josiah C. Joplin dossier — Trabuco/Bell Canyon, 1892–1920 (multiple independent sources)
- **LA Herald, 25 Oct 1892:** "A house and barn belonging to **J. C. Joplin, in Trabuco cañon**, was
  destroyed by fire" — established there by 1892.
- **LA Herald, 27 Jan 1894 & 23 Sep 1894:** Joplin is the **Trabuco precinct** figure (Democratic rep).
- **LA Herald, 12 Dec 1896:** **Andrew Joplin (Josiah's son) married Izella Straw** — the Joplin and
  **Straw** homestead families (Live Oak/Trabuco Canyon) intermarried.
- **LA Herald, 16 Apr 1902 + Pacific Rural Press, 3 May 1902:** a **mountain lion killed eight of
  Joplin's hogs** in Trabuco Canyon in one night — corroborates his **stock-raising** there.
- **Morning Free Press (Ventura), 22 Aug 1908:** apricots bought "from **bee men in Trabuco canyon and
  Capistrano**" — corroborates Joplin's **bee culture** (Armor's phrase) and orchards in the district.
- **Press-Telegram, 10 Jun 1910:** "**Andrew Joplin of Trabuco canyon**" (the son, still ranching there).

This is thorough, independent confirmation of Armor 1921: Josiah C. Joplin, stock-raiser and beekeeper,
Bell Canyon homestead in the Trabuco precinct — the man named in the 1908 dip article.

## The other named 1908 sites
- **Bixby ranch in Santa Ana canyon** — confirmed as a real ranch (*Enterprise*, Riverside, 11 Sep 1907:
  "a fire Sunday on the **Bixby ranch in Santa Ana canyon**"). This is a *different* Bixby holding from
  the Long Beach Bixby operations that dominate the search noise.
- **Capistrano** and **Yorba** — named in the 27 May 1908 article; no granular follow-up in CDNC.

## The county-vat model (the SLO analog — what OC's un-digitized records would show)
CDNC has **San Luis Obispo's** county live-stock inspector (Mazza) monthly reports: **54 dipping vats**
(Jan 1908), **63** (Jul), **64** (Oct), **65** (Dec 1908). Orange County kept the identical office
(McFarlane) and almost certainly filed a comparable **vat count/location list** — but the **Santa Ana
Register/Blade and the OC county-vet ledger are NOT in CDNC**, confirming that piece is a physical-
archive task (OC Archives / State Archives), not a digital one.

## Digital status
CDNC is **fully accessible via the browser** (the bot-gate is only for scripts). The OC-specific dipping
coverage in CDNC = the *LA Herald* items above; the granular vat detail is in the **un-digitized Santa
Ana local press + the county-veterinarian records** — the Tier-1 physical targets.
