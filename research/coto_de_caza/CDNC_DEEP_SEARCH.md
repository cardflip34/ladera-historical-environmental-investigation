# CDNC deep search — the Joplin / Bell Canyon 1908 dip site (primary press, read this session)

> Hypothesis-neutral. All items are contemporaneous newspaper reports (**Grade B2** — reputable papers
> quoting named officials), read directly on cdnc.ucr.edu this session via the browser. OCR corrections
> are flagged [inferred]. **No contamination claim:** the 1908 cattle deaths below are a documented
> *acute animal-poisoning event from the dip fluid in 1908* — they say nothing about soil residue today.

## 🔗 The dipping chain — fully documented, all 1908
1. **LA Herald, 27 May 1908** (`LAH19080527.2.83.22.7.3`, read verbatim): Dr. Coleman, deputy state
   quarantine officer, at Santa Ana; within ten days **several hundred head "treated by dipping at the
   ranch of J. C. Joplin in Trabuco canyon,"** 600–700 at **Capistrano**, and dipping "in progress at
   **Yorba** and at the **Bixby ranch in Santa Ana canyon**."
2. **San Jose Mercury, 26 Jun 1908, "KILLED TICKS AND CATTLE"** (`SJMN19080626.2.106`): **six head of
   cattle belonging to [E.] J. Levengood died** following their **dipping for Texas fever tick**; the
   dip was **"the arsenic preparation recommended by the Government."**
3. **LA Herald, 25 Jul 1908, "DAIRYMAN SUING FOR INJURIES TO HIS CATTLE"** (`LAH19080725.2.83.4.1`):
   **E. J. Levengood filed a $400 claim against the County of Orange**; the dipping "was done by order
   of the **county veterinarian, Dr. W. S. McFarlane**."
4. **San Pedro Daily News, 11 Aug 1908, "The Herd's Lord Goes Abroad"** (`SPDN19080811.2.24`): the herd
   "was driven from **J. C. Joplin's ranch in Bell Canyon** with a herd of cattle **bought from Joplin by
   Levengood**."

**What this establishes (new, this session):** the arsenical dip at these OC sites was **real and
potent enough to kill livestock in 1908**, it was **county-ordered** (McFarlane), and Levengood's dead
herd came **directly from Joplin's Bell Canyon ranch** — tying the fatal-dip event to the exact site.
The site's modern identity (Bell Canyon → **Starr Ranch Audubon Sanctuary**) is unchanged.

## 📍 "Bell Canyon" independently confirmed (fixes the spelling)
Two contemporaneous sources say **"Bell Canyon"** (matching the modern name / Starr Ranch), where Armor
1921 wrote "Belle Canyon":
- **San Pedro Daily News, 11 Aug 1908:** "J. C. Joplin's ranch in **Bell Canyon**."
- **La Habra Star, 17 Mar 1920** (`Active Leasing in Trabuco`): "**the old Joplin place in Bell canyon**"
  — noting it as the **W. K. Robinson ranch**, then under oil-lease option. *(Consistent with the later
  ownership chain Joplin → Robinson/Starr → Audubon at the Bell Canyon / Starr Ranch site.)*

## 👤 Josiah C. Joplin dossier — Trabuco/Bell Canyon, 1892–1920 (multiple independent sources)
- **LA Herald, 25 Oct 1892:** "A house and barn belonging to **J. C. Joplin, in Trabuco cañon**, was
  destroyed by fire" — established there by 1892.
- **LA Herald, 27 Jan 1894 & 23 Sep 1894:** Joplin is the **Trabuco precinct** figure (Democratic rep).
- **LA Herald, 12 Dec 1896:** **Andrew Joplin (Josiah's son) married Izella Straw** — the Joplin and
  **Straw** homestead families (Live Oak/Trabuco Canyon) intermarried.
- **LA Herald, 16 Apr 1902 + Pacific Rural Press, 3 May 1902:** a **mountain lion killed eight of
  Joplin's hogs** in Trabuco Canyon in one night — corroborates his **stock-raising** there.
- **Morning Free Press (Ventura), 22 Aug 1908:** apricots bought "from **bee men in Trabuco canyon and
  Capistrano**" — corroborates Joplin's **bee culture** (Armor's phrase) and orchards in the district.
- **Press-Telegram, 10 Jun 1910:** "**Andrew Joplin of Trabuco canyon**" (the son, still ranching there).

This is thorough, independent confirmation of Armor 1921: Josiah C. Joplin, stock-raiser and beekeeper,
Bell Canyon homestead in the Trabuco precinct — the man named in the 1908 dip article.

### Public life & death — the fuller biography (Phase B, this session)
The man in the 1908 dip article was, for the same quarter-century, **Orange County's treasurer** — so his
life is unusually well-documented in the county press, which sharpens the identification:
- **County treasurer 1898–1922, repeatedly re-elected:** *La Habra Star* 9 & 23 Aug 1918 ("**J. C. JOPLIN
  FOR TREASURER**"); 30 Aug 1918 election returns ("For Treasurer — J. C. [Joplin]…"); **14 Jun 1922
  "PIONEER TREASURER SEEKS RE-ELECTION"**; 30 Aug 1922 "Treasurer Joplin… **re-elected**." Campaign ad
  25 Aug 1922: "**old-fashioned honesty** brought about the selection of J. C. Joplin as county treasurer."
- **Reputation / civic role:** returned a **$12,825.28 tax over-collection to taxpayers** (*La Habra Star*
  3 & 10 Feb 1922); with Dr. Freeman of Fullerton **compiled the county's WWI service record** (5 & 12 Jul
  1918).
- **The 1908–09 warrant dispute:** as treasurer he **held county warrants ($20,750)** rather than cash
  them; taxpayers sued to compel payment (*LA Herald* 6 Aug 1908 "WOULD COMPEL TREASURER TO CASH THEIR
  WARRANTS"; *Press-Telegram* 21 Oct 1909).
- **A game-law scrape (the mountain man):** charged with a **state game-law violation, 1923**, fined in
  justice court, appealed (*La Habra Star* 14 Sep 1923, 21 Dec 1923, 9 Jan 1924) — of a piece with the
  1902 lion-and-hogs and 1921 "escapes death in the hills" items; he was a backcountry rancher-hunter.
- **Land dealings beyond Trabuco:** *Fresno Bee* 18 Nov 1911 "REALTY TRANSFERS" records **Josiah C. Joplin
  (with Susan V. Moore) conveying a Coalinga lot to Guy L. Butler** — i.e. he also held **west-side
  Fresno-County (Coalinga oil-belt) property**, not only the Bell Canyon homestead.
- **Death:** ***La Habra Star*, 23 Jun 1933** — "funeral services for **Josiah C. Joplin, 89, Orange
  county pioneer**." He **died June 1933, age 89** (b. 15 Sep 1844 ✓). Descendants carried the family's
  "Boyd" name (*Santa Ana Journal* 19 Nov 1935, "Miss Boyd Joplin").

**Net:** the "J. C. Joplin" of the 1908 dip article is fixed beyond reasonable doubt as **Josiah C. Joplin,
1844–1933**, Bell-Canyon homesteader and four-plus-term county treasurer — the same man Armor 1921, the
1909 GLO patent, and the Starr Ranch history describe.

## The other named 1908 sites (Phase B — searched directly this session)
- **Bixby ranch in Santa Ana canyon** — confirmed as a real ranch (*Enterprise*, Riverside, 11 Sep 1907:
  "a fire Sunday on the **Bixby ranch in Santa Ana canyon**"). This is a *different* Bixby holding from
  the Long Beach Bixby operations that dominate the search noise.
- **San Juan Capistrano** — the 600–700-head dipping there had an organized basis: *Blade Tribune*
  **18 Jan 1908** reports a **"meeting of stockmen at San Juan Capistrano"** whose "principal question…
  had to do with [the cattle tick]" — a **January-1908 stockmen's meeting on the tick**, four months
  before the May dipping. No parcel-level detail beyond that.
- **Yorba** — a dedicated `Yorba dipping` search returns **only the 27 May 1908 article itself**; no
  separate granular Yorba record survives in CDNC. Absence-of-record, not absence-of-fact.

## The Trabuco homestead families (Phase B)
- **Straw family (confirmed):** the family into which Andrew Joplin married (Izella Straw, 1896) is an
  independently attested Trabuco homestead line — **"Gus W. Straw of Trabuco"** (*LA Herald* 16 Oct 1902)
  and **"Mrs. P. W. Straw"**, an "old resident," d. 1910 (*LA Herald* 7 Jan 1910). Corroborates the
  Joplin–Straw homestead cluster around Live Oak / Trabuco Canyon.
- **Osterman (NOT confirmed — correction):** an `Osterman Trabuco` search returns **no Osterman ranch in
  the canyon** (only an unrelated 1935 CCC-fire recollection). The prior working note that the 1938
  canyon-mouth **orchard** was "likely the Osterman ranch" is **not supported by the primary press** and
  should be treated as an **unverified attribution** — the orchard owner is currently unidentified. The
  substantive point (orchard ⇒ possible lead-arsenate vector, distinct from cattle-dip arsenic) stands
  regardless of who owned it.

## The county-vat model (the SLO analog — what OC's un-digitized records would show)
CDNC has **San Luis Obispo's** county live-stock inspector (Mazza) monthly reports: **54 dipping vats**
(Jan 1908), **63** (Jul), **64** (Oct), **65** (Dec 1908). Orange County kept the identical office
(McFarlane) and almost certainly filed a comparable **vat count/location list** — but the **Santa Ana
Register/Blade and the OC county-vet ledger are NOT in CDNC**, confirming that piece is a physical-
archive task (OC Archives / State Archives), not a digital one.

## Digital status
CDNC is **fully accessible via the browser** (the bot-gate is only for scripts). The OC-specific dipping
coverage in CDNC = the *LA Herald* items above; the granular vat detail is in the **un-digitized Santa
Ana local press + the county-veterinarian records** — the Tier-1 physical targets.

## Phase C — final scrub (this session): the era's END dated; Levengood ID nailed; O'Neill absence
- **Orange County released from federal tick quarantine, March 1912:** *SF Call* 8 Mar 1912
  ("QUARANTINE AGAINST FEVER TICK IS RAISED"); *Visalia Times Delta* 15 Mar 1912 (federal order text:
  "In California, the county of Orange is released from quarantine"); *SB Sun* 9 Mar 1912 ("LIFT THE
  QUARANTINE… Orange County No Longer Dangerous"). **OC's arsenical-dipping era ≈ 1907–Mar 1912** —
  same month as the state's last-district (T6S R6W) proclamation. Timeline closed.
- **E. J. Levengood identified:** the 1908 claimant was a **Santa Ana** man — "the well known horseman
  and chariot racer" (*LA Herald* 7 Oct 1909; racing items 1907–09 "of Santa Ana") — who moved to
  **Pomona ~1910** (racing items 1910–14 "of Pomona"), exactly matching Armor 1921's "Mrs. E. J.
  Levengood of Pomona." Suit outcome NOT in digitized papers → OC Archives claim register.
- **O'Neill scale, dip year:** *LA Herald* 1 Sep 1908 — Richard O'Neil's ranches "cover nearly half the
  southern portion of Orange" county. But **no O'Neill dipping item exists in CDNC** (all variants
  searched), and the *LA Herald* has only 2 generic "dipping vat" hits 1907–12. The granular OC vat
  record is exclusively in un-digitized local press + county/state ledgers. (Beware the *SLO-county*
  "Santa Margarita ranch" dip items — a different ranch.)
- **State Vet reports circulated** (*Pacific Rural Press* 1 Feb 1913 "Report of State Veterinarian") —
  the record class for vat counts exists in print → State Archives / HathiTrust.
