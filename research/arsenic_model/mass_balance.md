# Arsenic mass-balance — MODEL ESTIMATE (not a measurement)

> **Classification: MODEL ESTIMATE · Confidence: Low.** Every number below is a mass-balance
> thought experiment built from the published USDA dip formula and soil-arsenic concentrations
> *measured in Australia and the US Southeast*. **No California dip-site soil has been tested.**
> These figures state what is *plausible*, not what *is*. They could be wrong by an order of
> magnitude in either direction. Nothing here asserts that any California soil is contaminated.

## Inputs (all sourced)

- **USDA California dip formula:** 8 lb white arsenic (arsenic trioxide, As₂O₃) per 500 gal water;
  up to 9–10 lb for range cattle. Source: USDA BAI, *Eradicating Cattle Ticks in California* (1911),
  p.291 [S-USDA-C174, grade A1].
- **Vat size:** swim vats held ~3,000–4,000 gal; the solution "remains active … indefinitely" and
  was topped up as arsenic was consumed/carried off (same source, pp.291–293).
- **Arsenic fraction of As₂O₃:** 75.7% by mass (from molar masses; As₂O₃ = 197.84 g/mol).
- **Measured soil arsenic near vats elsewhere (empirical anchor):** ~500–3,000 mg/kg near the bath
  (NSW to 1,200–3,000 mg/kg [S-NSW-DIP]; SE-US vat studies comparable).
- **California regional background arsenic:** ~2.4 mg/kg (LA County 95% UCL) to an upper bound of
  ~11–12 mg/kg; 12 mg/kg already ≈ 1×10⁻⁴ residential cancer risk (DTSC HHRA Note 11; SF Bay RWQCB
  ambient 11 mg/kg) [grade A2].
- **Health-based soil screening levels:** EPA residential RSL 0.68 mg/kg; California DTSC residential
  0.11 mg/kg (both cancer-based) [grade A2].

## Results (model)

| Quantity | Model estimate |
|---|---|
| Arsenic in the dip **fluid** | ~1,450 mg As / L (8 lb As₂O₃ / 500 gal → 2.75 kg elemental As / 500 gal) |
| Arsenic **standing in a full vat** at one time | 3,000 gal → **16.5 kg (36 lb)**; 4,000 gal → **22 kg (48 lb)** |
| Cumulative arsenic **throughput per vat over its life** (3–10 yr, replenished) | **~65–680 kg (145–1,500 lb)**; central ~100–400 kg |
| Arsenic potentially **residing in soil around one heavily-used vat** (anchored to 500–3,000 mg/kg over a vat+drain+apron zone) | **~40–225 kg (100–500 lb)**; worst case to ~1,000 kg |
| **Statewide near-vat soil legacy** (corrected — see ceiling below) | **~3–30 metric tons**; central order ~10 t |

## The hard ceiling (independent cross-check — corrects the statewide figure)

An independent throughput analysis (USGS *Arsenic Historical Statistics* DS140 + the USDA
cattle-drag-out method) bounds the **total white arsenic ever used** by the entire California
program at **~3–12 metric tons per year**, i.e. **~15–75 metric tons of elemental arsenic
program-wide** across ~6–8 seasons. That is a *hard ceiling*: no more arsenic can sit in
California soil than was ever purchased and poured.

- This **corrects an earlier over-estimate.** A first pass put statewide near-vat soil at ~20–100 t;
  that assumed 200–1,000 vats and is inconsistent with the ceiling. The throughput math implies
  California ran on the order of **~50–150 vats** (a short, comparatively small program — Florida had
  3,000+), not hundreds-to-thousands.
- Two independent methods now agree: (a) 15–40% of the ~15–75 t used ending up concentrated near
  vats → ~3–30 t; (b) 50–150 vats × 40–225 kg/site → ~2–34 t. **Revised statewide near-vat legacy:
  ~3–30 t elemental As, central ~10 t.** The remainder was carried off on cattle and dispersed thinly
  across rangeland, discarded as spent fluid, or lost to a century of leaching and erosion.
- The **per-site** figures are unchanged and are the ones that matter for screening any single
  location: a single heavily-used vat could still hold **~100–500 lb of arsenic** in its immediate
  soil regardless of the statewide total.

## Where the arsenic came from (sourced)

- **It was smelter by-product** — arsenic trioxide recovered from copper/lead/gold smelter flue dust,
  refined to 90–95% As₂O₃ [ATSDR A1; ASARCO history, *AJPH* B1].
- **The US was a net importer of white arsenic during the core dipping window (1907–~1912)**; domestic
  output (ASARCO's **Tacoma, WA** smelter, then **Anaconda, MT** from 1910) overtook imports only
  ~1913–1914 [USGS DS140, A1]. So much of the arsenic used in California dipping was **imported**
  (England/Europe, later US-owned Mexican smelters) plus West-Coast ASARCO Tacoma output.
- **Dip was a minor use.** >¾ of US arsenic went to orchard/cotton insecticides (lead arsenate, Paris
  green, calcium arsenate) [Science History Institute, B1]. The whole California dip program used
  **<1%** of national white-arsenic supply.
- **The formula was a USDA specification, not a branded product.** Ranchers **bought bulk white
  arsenic from the druggist** and mixed it on-site per BAI Circular 183 (1911): 24 lb sal soda + 8 lb
  white arsenic + ½ gal pine tar, water to 500 gal — "homemade dips are the ones most commonly used"
  [USDA BAI Circular 183, A1]. A costlier proprietary concentrate existed (likely Cooper's Dip /
  Tixol — *inferred, not established*). Note: the branded "Kreso/Zenoleum/Creolin" dips of the era
  were **coal-tar disinfectants, not arsenical** — do not conflate.
- No source ties any specific arsenic to any specific ranch; attribution to a property is not
  established.

## Toxicity framing

- **Intrinsic material toxicity (context only):** 8 lb white arsenic ≈ 2.75 kg elemental As.
  Adult acute-lethal dose of As₂O₃ ≈ 70–200 mg (ATSDR). So one 8-lb charge contains, as raw
  material, on the order of **20,000–50,000 adult lethal doses**. *This is the toxicity of the
  chemical in the bag — NOT an exposure or harm estimate. Soil is dilute, arsenic sorbs to Fe/Al
  oxides and is only partly bioavailable, and exposure depends entirely on concentration, pathway,
  and contact.*
- **The real-world concern is chronic, not acute.** Arsenic is an IARC Group 1 human carcinogen
  (skin, lung, bladder) and does not degrade. Exposure pathways that matter for a former dip site
  under/near homes: (1) **child incidental soil ingestion + hand-to-mouth** (children are the
  sensitive receptor; ATSDR notes soil is a more important arsenic route for children than adults);
  (2) **inhalation of resuspended dust**, greatest during **mass grading/earthmoving**; (3) dermal
  and household dust.
- **Scale against yardsticks:** a dip site at 500–3,000 mg/kg would be **~40–270× California's
  regional background** (11–12 mg/kg) and **~700–4,400× the EPA residential health screening level**
  (0.68 mg/kg). Because background arsenic already sits near 1×10⁻⁴ cancer risk, California regulates
  arsenic *against background* — which is exactly why a point source that rises far above background
  is the thing a soil test would reveal.

## What grading does to the picture

Mass grading (cut-and-fill) neither creates nor destroys arsenic. It can **dilute** a hot spot by
mixing high-concentration vat soil into large fill volumes (lowering mg/kg but spreading the mass),
or **relocate** it intact (immobile arsenic travels with the soil it is moved with). Grading also
**resuspends dust**, the one moment the inhalation pathway is largest. None of this is knowable for
any specific site without (a) locating the vat and (b) testing the soil — the two things never done
in California.
