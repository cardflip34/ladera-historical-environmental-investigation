# The Bluffs · Big Canyon · Santa Ana Heights — recon notes

> Hypothesis-neutral. No contamination claimed. Working file — not part of the publication.
> Question: ranch/cattle activity there in the 1907–1912 dipping era? All three ring Upper Newport Bay
> on former Rancho San Joaquin / Irvine Ranch land, inside the March-1912 last-held quarantine district.

## Imagery read (1938 → 2022 overlays, done)

**The Bluffs** (`imagery/bluffs_overlay.jpg`) — 1938: **open grazed/fallow ranch mesa** on the bluff
over the Back Bay (faint drainage swales, tracks, a possible small stock pond, scattered brush) — **no
buildings, corrals, or structures.** Today: the Bluffs residential community + a school; the Back Bay
preserved. *Same pattern as Eastbluff — open range/fallow, no cattle-handling feature.*

**Big Canyon** (`imagery/bigcanyon_overlay.jpg`) — 1938: **natural canyon-and-ridge grazing land**
(brush-filled canyons, open slopes, a ranch road, and a possible **rectilinear structure at the canyon
mouth**, lower-left). Today: **Newport Center / Fashion Island** commercial core + the Big Canyon golf
course + residential. *Open canyon range; one possible structure worth noting, no obvious dip feature.*

**Santa Ana Heights** (`imagery/santaanaheights_overlay.jpg`) — **DIFFERENT.** 1938: **already a
patchwork of small farms** — cultivated/row-crop fields, subdivided lots, and a **building complex near
the bay** (more built-up than the open mesas). Today: residential + commercial, **John Wayne Airport**
(NE), a golf course (NW), the Back Bay reserve (SW). *This matches its history as an unincorporated
area of small farms and equestrian holdings — **not** open Irvine Ranch range.*

## What the difference means (hypothesis-neutral)
- **Bluffs & Big Canyon** read like the rest of the Irvine Ranch coastal land: **open grazing/fallow**,
  no cattle-handling infrastructure imaged, and — per the Eastbluff workup — no dipping record and an
  industrial (not agricultural) modern contamination signature. **Weaker end for the dip question.**
- **Santa Ana Heights** is the one with an **agriculturally intensive** early history (small farms,
  possible orchards/row crops by the 1930s). That makes the **orchard lead-arsenate** vector — the
  arsenic DTSC *actually* finds on former-farm land in OC — **more relevant here** than the cattle-dip
  vector. Its building complex + fields warrant the documentary check (small-farm chemistry ≠ dipping).

## Open questions (documentary recon in progress)

2. Any dipping / vat / corral record tied to any of the three by name (expected: none, per prior work).
3. **EnviroStor / GeoTracker** for each — agricultural/livestock past-use or arsenic sites, and the

## Discipline
Open grazing mesa (Bluffs, Big Canyon) and small farms (Santa Ana Heights) are the *expected* land uses
across this former ranch and are **not** dipping indicators. No vat or corral is confirmed; no
contamination implied. Soil questions resolve only by the county vat records and a test.
