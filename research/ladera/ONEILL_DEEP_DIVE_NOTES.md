## CDNC primary-press pass (read in-browser this session, B2)
- **Scale in the dip year:** *LA Herald*, 1 Sep 1908 — hunting on "the ranches belonging to Richard
  O'Neil, **which cover nearly half the southern portion of Orange** [county]." The compulsory-dip
  program's OC geography was substantially O'Neill geography.
- **Jerome O'Neill** confirmed "owner of the Santa Margarita ranch" (*Times-Advocate*, 26 Oct 1917).
- **No O'Neill-named dipping item exists in CDNC** (searches: O'Neill/O'Neil × dipping/tick/
  quarantine, "Santa Margarita ranch dipping," `"dipping vat"` in the *LA Herald* 1907–1912 → only 2
  hits, neither OC-ranch-specific). The *SLO-county* "Santa Margarita ranch" dip items (e.g. "Cattle
  Were Dipped," 21 Feb 1910) are a DIFFERENT ranch — do not conflate.
- **Standing inference (unchanged):** the 600–700 head dipped "at Capistrano" (LA Herald 27 May 1908)
  and the Jan-1908 San Juan Capistrano stockmen's meeting sat on/beside O'Neill range — participation
  near-certain by law and geography, but **no direct record located digitally**. The record class that
  would name it: county-vet ledger, Supervisors minutes, State Vet files, RMV's own books (see
  `research/archives/RECORDS_REQUESTS_2026-07.md`).

## What the Coto phase contributes to Ladera (same landowner)
- The **GLO-patent method worked**: homesteader inholdings can be pinned to survey sections. Ladera's
  footprint had NO homestead inholdings (pure rancho land) — so the Ladera equivalent is the **RMV
  ranch records + county/state files**, not GLO.
- The **TTM 17325 precedent** (shooting range → Phase II → RAP → on-site burial under roads) shows the
  developer-era machinery engages **only when the historical source is documented** — the core blind-
  spot mechanism, now demonstrated on the same rancho.
