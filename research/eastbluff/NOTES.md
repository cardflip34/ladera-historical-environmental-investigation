# Eastbluff / Upper Newport Bay ("Back Bay") — recon notes

> Hypothesis-neutral. No contamination claimed. Working file — not part of the publication.
> Question: was there ranch/cattle activity here during the 1907–1912 dipping era?

## Imagery finding (direct, done)
- `imagery/eastbluff_backbay_1927.jpg` — a **1927 aerial** (only ~15 yr after dipping ended) shows the
  **Eastbluff mesa as open, undeveloped upland — grazed/fallow ranch land** — immediately against the
  **natural Back Bay estuary** (Upper Newport Bay: tidal channels, mudflats, salt marsh). No town, no
  streets; a few faint tracks/field edges and scattered dots (brush or stock).
- `imagery/eastbluff_backbay_overlay.jpg` — 1927 over 2022: the open mesa is now the **Eastbluff
  residential neighborhood** (Irvine Company, 1960s); the Back Bay is now the **Upper Newport Bay
  Ecological Reserve** (preserved wetland).

**Read:** In 1927 this was open Irvine-Ranch mesa land beside the bay — consistent with cattle/ranch
use. It was **not** developed until the 1960s.

## Land context (established from prior work)
- Eastbluff and the Upper Newport Bay east-side uplands are on **Rancho San Joaquin → the Irvine
  Ranch** (James Irvine → Irvine Company). The Irvine Ranch ran **cattle**, then diversified the mesas
  into **farming** (lima beans, etc.). So the 1900–1912 use was very likely **cattle grazing**, with
  row-crop farming coming later — but the *exact cattle-vs-crop timeline for this specific mesa* is the
  open question the documentary recon is resolving.
- **Quarantine relevance:** the **March 7, 1912 proclamation's last-held OC district begins "at the
  Pacific Ocean in the town of Newport Beach"** — so the Newport Beach / Back Bay area is right at the
  anchor of the zone where the tick (and compulsory arsenical dipping) persisted longest, to ~June 1912.

## Documentary recon — answers (done)

**1. Cattle grazing vs. farming, 1907–1912 — plausible-but-unrecorded for this parcel.**
OC Parks (A2) states "the hills above the bay were used to graze cattle and sheep" under Rancho San
Joaquin → the Irvine Ranch — but **undated and general.** Critically, the Irvine Ranch was
**converting the mesas from open range to dry-farmed lima beans and grain** in exactly this period (by
WWI ~60,000 acres in lima beans, "the world's top lima-bean producer"). So the Eastbluff mesa in
1907–1912 could have been **range or cropland** — **no located source dates this specific parcel.**

**2. Dipping tied to Eastbluff / Back Bay / the Irvine coastal section — no record located.**
Zero hits across CDNC (Orange-County dipping press for 1907–13 is thin, so absence-of-*digitized*-
record), GNIS, and the environmental databases. Consistent with prior work: the four named 1908 OC dip
sites are all **eastern/southern county**, none on Irvine/San Joaquin coastal-central land; Cleland
1952 is silent. **Absence of record, not proof of absence.**

**3. Environmental record (EnviroStor + GeoTracker, live REST, A1) — CONTRADICTS an ag/arsenic legacy.**
37 EnviroStor + 137 GeoTracker sites in the Eastbluff/Back Bay box, and **0 agricultural / livestock /
cattle-dip / ag-arsenic** sites. The footprint is entirely **post-1960 industrial**: aerospace (Ford
Aeronutronic / Loral, 1000 Ford Rd), semiconductor (Rockwell/Conexant, 4311 Jamboree), **dry-cleaner
PCE** (Mr. Best, Sunny Fresh, Back Bay Cleaners, etc.), and petroleum LUSTs. The only nearby **arsenic**
is industrial (electronics / Air National Guard) and **across the bay in Costa Mesa** — not at
Eastbluff. *(Caveat: these databases don't capture pre-1980s uses; a c.1910 vat wouldn't appear unless
rediscovered.)* Notable: a bay-wide **"Upper Newport Bay Sediment Quality Investigation"** exists —
the estuary is a recognised sediment sink.

## Quarantine placement (primary proclamation now recovered)
The full **March 7, 1912 Governor Johnson proclamation** was recovered (California State Library scan,
A2 — saved as `../oc_dipping_records/proclamation_1912_johnson.pdf`). It confirms the boundary text and
resolves the earlier OCR-illegible digit: **"…north line of township 6 south, range 6 WEST…"**
- It is a **cattle-MOVEMENT quarantine** (stock must be certified tick-free before leaving the
  district) — it **names no dip site and mandates no on-site vat.** Being "in the district" means the
  herds needed to be tick-free to move, i.e. dipped *somewhere*, not necessarily on-site.
- The T6S north line is ~**33.69°N**; **Eastbluff (33.63°N) is south of it → inside the March-7
  district**, at its coastal anchor (Newport Beach). *(This corrects a recon aside that placed Ladera
  "outside" — Ladera 33.55°N is also south of the line, i.e. inside the March-7 district; only the
  smaller June-30 remnant shrank to the extreme SW corner.)*

## Discipline
Open grazed mesa land beside a bay is **expected** across the whole Irvine Ranch and is **not** a
dipping indicator. No vat is identifiable in the imagery; no contamination is implied. The soil
question, here as everywhere, is answered only by the county vat records and a test.
