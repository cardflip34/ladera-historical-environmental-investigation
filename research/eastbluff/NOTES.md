# Eastbluff / Upper Newport Bay ("Back Bay") — recon notes

> Hypothesis-neutral. No contamination claimed. Working file — not part of the publication.
> Question: was there ranch/cattle activity here during the 1907–1912 dipping era?

## Imagery finding (direct, done)
- `imagery/eastbluff_backbay_1927.jpg` — a **1927 aerial** (only ~15 yr after dipping ended) shows the
  **Eastbluff mesa as open, undeveloped upland — grazed/fallow ranch land** — immediately against the
  **natural Back Bay estuary** (Upper Newport Bay: tidal channels, mudflats, salt marsh). No town, no
  streets; a few faint tracks/field edges and scattered dots (brush or stock).
- `imagery/eastbluff_backbay_overlay.jpg` — 1927 over 2022: the open mesa is now the **Eastbluff
  residential neighborhood** (Irvine Company, 1960s); the Back Bay is now the **Upper Newport Bay
  Ecological Reserve** (preserved wetland).

**Read:** In 1927 this was open Irvine-Ranch mesa land beside the bay — consistent with cattle/ranch
use. It was **not** developed until the 1960s.

## Land context (established from prior work)
- Eastbluff and the Upper Newport Bay east-side uplands are on **Rancho San Joaquin → the Irvine
  Ranch** (James Irvine → Irvine Company). The Irvine Ranch ran **cattle**, then diversified the mesas
  into **farming** (lima beans, etc.). So the 1900–1912 use was very likely **cattle grazing**, with
  row-crop farming coming later — but the *exact cattle-vs-crop timeline for this specific mesa* is the
  open question the documentary recon is resolving.
- **Quarantine relevance:** the **March 7, 1912 proclamation's last-held OC district begins "at the
  Pacific Ocean in the town of Newport Beach"** — so the Newport Beach / Back Bay area is right at the
  anchor of the zone where the tick (and compulsory arsenical dipping) persisted longest, to ~June 1912.

## Open questions (documentary recon in progress)
1. Cattle grazing vs. row-crop farming on the Eastbluff/Back Bay mesa in 1907–1912 (dates).
2. Any located record of dipping, a vat, or tick treatment tied to the Irvine Ranch's Newport Bay
   coastal section by name. *(Prior work: no OC dip vat is tied to Irvine/San Joaquin land; the four
   named 1908 sites are all eastern/southern county; Cleland 1952 is silent on dipping.)*
3. EnviroStor / GeoTracker record for Eastbluff / Upper Newport Bay (agricultural past-use, arsenic?).

## Discipline
Open grazed mesa land beside a bay is **expected** across the whole Irvine Ranch and is **not** a
dipping indicator. No vat is identifiable in the imagery; no contamination is implied. The soil
question, here as everywhere, is answered only by the county vat records and a test.
